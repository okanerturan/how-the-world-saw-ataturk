# 10 Kasım — Research Guidelines

> **Governance note (Aug 2026):** the expansion campaign — the full census of gazes, country by
> country — is governed by [`HUNT.md`](HUNT.md) (coverage matrix, source seams, staging pipeline
> via `candidates/`). This file remains the method-and-reading spine; `LEDGER.md` remains the
> evidence register. Same rigour end to end: lead → candidate → verified → vault + ledger.

Working thesis: **Atatürk functioned as a global Rorschach test.** Between 1919 and today,
mutually incompatible ideologies — fascists, communists, anti-colonial nationalists, Zionists,
Islamists, feminists, popes — each looked at Ankara and saw their own future. The pattern of
projections maps the 20th century's ideological landscape better than any single "true" portrait
could. The project's job is not to settle who he was, but to catalogue who everyone *needed* him
to be — and what each gaze cropped out of the frame.

This is not a stretch thesis. Stefan Ihrig already ran the method for one gaze (the Nazi one);
the transnational-Kemalism literature runs it country by country. What doesn't exist is the full
mirror array assembled for a general audience, anchored on the one day the whole world looked at
once: **10 November 1938**.

---

## 1. Four framing decisions (make these before reading anything)

**a. Apocrypha are data, not noise.** The Lloyd George "genius of centuries" quote, the MacArthur
prophecy, the Piłsudski empty-chair legend — these are probably not history. But a fabricated
quote is *evidence of what a community needed Atatürk to have said*. Don't delete the myth tier;
relabel it. Track each quote's provenance (earliest print occurrence, Quote-Investigator style)
and present the fabrication itself as reception history. This converts the vault's biggest
weakness — an unsourced AI transcript as raw material — into part of the subject.

**b. The dark mirrors stay in the deck.** Nazi admiration, Kurdish and Armenian memory, the
purged left, the Islamist demonology. A Rorschach with only flattering cards is a brochure.
Ihrig's framing is the shield: documenting the Nazi gaze is a fact about the Nazis, not a verdict
on Atatürk — but Dersim and the one-party state are facts about the republic, and they must be
present with scholarly citation, precisely because they are what most foreign mirrors cropped out.

**c. Baseline before projection.** Every "what X saw" only means something as a *delta* from what
Atatürk actually thought and did. Hanioğlu's intellectual biography is the baseline instrument;
without it the project is a quote wall.

**d. The first Rorschach card was self-drawn.** Atatürk actively managed his own image — *Nutuk*
(the 1927 six-day speech) is a constructed self-narrative, and he deliberately cultivated foreign
correspondents (Ellison, Sheridan, Price, Farrère). The test-maker was also a test-taker. Keep
this complication in the foreground; it's what elevates the project above "look how many fans he
had."

## 2. Driving questions

1. **What did each viewer's Atatürk let them do at home?** Projection is domestic politics:
   Nehru's Atatürk argues against Gandhi; Ben-Gurion's argues for the New Jew; Hitler's argues
   for tearing up Versailles. Always ask what the mirror was *for*.
2. **What does every mirror crop?** Almost no foreign gaze contains Dersim, the Independence
   Tribunals, or Fikriye. The systematic blind spots are as patterned as the projections.
3. **When does a projection die?** Modernization-theory Atatürk fades after 1979; anti-colonial
   Atatürk fades with decolonization; the Islamist anti-Atatürk is alive and growing. Projections
   have lifespans — periodize them.
4. **How has Turkey's own gaze changed?** 10 Kasım as ritual (the 9:05 sirens), the 1981 UNESCO
   centennial year, Can Dündar's *Mustafa* (2008) controversy, the 2023 Disney+ series row.
   The last mirror is domestic.

## 3. Spine reading (in this order)

**Tier 1 — the load-bearing shelf:**
1. **M. Şükrü Hanioğlu, *Atatürk: An Intellectual Biography*** (Princeton 2011) — the baseline:
   what he actually read, believed, and intended (scientism, materialism, the Ottoman officer
   milieu).
2. **Stefan Ihrig, *Atatürk in the Nazi Imagination*** (Harvard 2014) — the founding text of the
   method this project generalizes; also the source for the German press / November 1938 material.
3. **Andrew Mango, *Atatürk*** (1999) — the standard factual biography; use it to check every
   transcript claim. (Read Kinross too, but *as a source* — the Cold War Western gaze — not as a
   reference.)
4. **Nathalie Clayer, Fabio Giomi, Emmanuel Szurek (eds.), *Kemalism: Transnational Politics in
   the Post-Ottoman World*** (I.B. Tauris, 2019) — reception country by country; the scholarly
   skeleton for the mirror taxonomy.
5. **Erik-Jan Zürcher, *Turkey: A Modern History*** + **Ryan Gingeras, *Eternal Dawn: Turkey in
   the Age of Atatürk*** (Oxford UP, 2019) — the critical corrective on the one-party state.

**Tier 2 — per-mirror:**
- Nazi/Muslim-world thread: Motadel, *Islam and Nazi Germany's War*; Herf, *Nazi Propaganda for
  the Arab World* (both already named in the source transcript — they're real and good).
- South Asia: Gail Minault, *The Khilafat Movement*.
- Violence and continuity: Uğur Ümit Üngör, *The Making of Modern Turkey*; Hans-Lukas Kieser,
  *Talaat Pasha* (for the CUP-to-republic continuity debate).
- Cold War canonization: Daniel Lerner, *The Passing of Traditional Society* (1958); Bernard
  Lewis, *The Emergence of Modern Turkey* (1961) — read as artifacts of the modernization-theory
  gaze.
- Self-image: Hülya Adak, "National Myths and Self-Na(rra)tions," *South Atlantic Quarterly*
  102:2–3 (2003), 509–527 — *Nutuk* as constructed autobiography, read against Halide Edib's
  memoirs.
- The émigré sanctuary: Arnold Reisman, *Turkey's Modernization: Refugees from Nazism and
  Atatürk's Vision* (New Academia, 2006).
- One-party mechanics (Turkish): Mete Tunçay, *Tek-Parti Yönetimi*.
- Memory and the domestic gaze *(added & verified Aug 2026)*: Yael Navaro-Yashin, *Faces of the
  State* (Princeton, 2002); Esra Özyürek, *Nostalgia for the Modern* (Duke, 2006); Nicholas
  Danforth, *The Remaking of Republican Turkey* (Cambridge, 2021).
- The 10 Kasım ritual's own scholarship *(found Aug 2026)*: Nazlı Ökten, "An Endless Death and an
  Eternal Mourning: November 10 in Turkey," in Özyürek (ed.), *The Politics of Public Memory in
  Turkey* (Syracuse, 2007), 95–113 — and, proving the DergiPark seam, Bayram Akça's local study of
  the first decade of commemorations through a Muğla newspaper (*GTTAD*, 2023, 433–444), which
  settled the siren-origin question (see LEDGER.md).
- The great essay: Perry Anderson, "Kemalism: After the Ottomans," *London Review of Books* 30:17
  (11 September 2008) — ~30,000 words, the sharpest single synthesis outside the monographs.
- The military formation: George W. Gawrych, *The Young Atatürk* (I.B. Tauris, 2013) — first
  Western scholar into the Turkish General Staff archives.

**Primary / memoir tier:** *Nutuk*; *Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri*; Falih Rıfkı Atay, *Çankaya*
(essential insider); Şevket Süreyya Aydemir, *Tek Adam*; Rıza Nur's memoirs (hostile insider —
handle with tongs); Halide Edib, *The Turkish Ordeal*; Grew, *Turbulent Era*; Sherrill, *A Year's
Embassy to Mustafa Kemal* (Scribner's, 1934); Aralov, *Bir Sovyet Diplomatının Türkiye Anıları
1922–1923* (Turkish ed., İş Bankası Kültür, 2008); Ellison, *An Englishwoman in Angora*; Price,
*The Rebirth of Turkey*; Toynbee, *The Western Question in Greece and Turkey*; H.C. Armstrong,
*Grey Wolf* (1932) — the hostile first biography, banned in Turkey into the 1990s.

*Verification note (Aug 2026): every title above checks out as a real publication — as do all the
books the source transcript itself named, including the obscure one (Zvi Yehuda, *The New
Babylonian Diaspora*, Brill 2017).*

## 4. The claim ledger (first concrete task, before any new writing)

The vault's 151 transcript-derived nodes rest on an unsourced chat transcript. Before anything
public, every load-bearing claim gets a ledger row. **The ledger now exists: [`LEDGER.md`](LEDGER.md)**
— ~40 rows as of Aug 2026, with per-claim evidence tiers and next steps.

Status tiers: `✅ corroborated` · `📚 secondary-scholarly` · `🗣 circulating-unsourced` ·
`🎭 apocryphal-but-meaningful` (keep, relabeled as reception data) · `⚠️ single-source` ·
`❌ debunked-and-boring` (cut).

### Methodology — what "verified" means here so far (read before trusting any ✅)

The Aug 2026 passes were **web-corroboration at search-result level**, not document-level
scholarship. Concretely:
- Evidence is mostly **secondary and tertiary** (journalism, fact-checkers, publisher pages,
  Wikipedia); primary documents were reached in only a few cases (the Nobel nomination entry, the
  Einstein letter image, the TIME cover).
- Fact-checkers' work (Malumatfuruş, Teyit, Honest History) was **trusted, not replicated** — e.g.
  no independent Hansard sweep has been run for the Lloyd George quote.
- "Per Ihrig" currently means **per reviews of Ihrig** — page-level citations wait until the books
  are physically on the desk (Phase 0).
- Some details in `verified` nodes derived from **assistant training memory** (tier **M**).
  *Resolved 2026-08-03:* all M-tier life dates and the 1985 dedications are now source-confirmed —
  LEDGER.md keeps the resolution record. Remaining M items (anecdote scenes) stay hedged in nodes.
- All URLs accessed 2026-08-03; **archival snapshots handled**: 31/38 cited URLs had existing
  Wayback captures (inventory in `wayback_status.txt`), saves triggered for the rest. The Nobel
  record, 403 on the live site, was read through its 2026-05-14 snapshot — with a bonus find: the
  archive's annotation says Kemal made the 1934 short list but no evaluation was ever written.

This is the appropriate rigour for a scaffolding phase and would not survive peer review as-is.
The gap between "web-corroborated" and "publishable" is exactly what Phases 0–2 close: books read,
primary archives fetched, quotes traced to first print, page numbers attached.

**Known red-flag items already in the vault** — first verification pass run August 2026;
statuses below reflect it:
- Lloyd George "genius of centuries" — 🎭 **confirmed apocryphal-tier**: no trace in Hansard, and
  Parliament was in recess 4 Aug–14 Nov 1922, exactly when the scene is usually set. The traceable
  chain runs İsmet Giritli (ATAM journal) → Özakman's *Şu Çılgın Türkler* → the *Kurtuluş* TV
  series (Malumatfuruş has the workup). Exhibit-ready: the quote's provenance is Turkish
  national-epic literature, not Westminster.
- MacArthur 1932 — meeting ✅ **verified**: 27 September 1932, **Dolmabahçe Palace, Istanbul**
  (not Ankara as the transcript had it), arranged through Ambassador Sherrill. The prophecy
  dialogue 🎭: earliest known appearance is *Cumhuriyet*, 8 November 1951; Turkish researchers
  judge it a Cold War fabrication contradicted by the Interior Ministry's contemporaneous telegram
  about the meeting — and it *still* entered ATAM's collected speeches. Textbook institutional
  myth-laundering; exhibit it.
- Castro — bust ✅ (Havana, at Castro's behest, sculptor Metin Yurdanur, inscribed *"Yurtta barış,
  dünyada barış"*). The 1997 remarks survive in incompatible wordings ("a star that fell on your
  country"); *"I am a follower of Atatürk"* 🗣 has no primary source. Verified side-thread: Nâzım
  Hikmet's 1961 Havana visit as the reported origin of Castro's interest.
- Hitler's "star in the darkness" — ✅ confirmed as Ihrig's account (canonized after Atatürk's
  death), as is the 1938 "Mussolini his first and I his second student" line to a Turkish
  delegation. The "bust among his most cherished possessions" remains 🗣 unsourced — check Ihrig
  at page level.
- *"Who speaks today of the annihilation of the Armenians"* — the Obersalzberg/L-3 document has a
  genuine authenticity debate; cite with the standard scholarly caveat, never bare.
- *Bengal Lancer* — 📚 **upgraded**: Hitler praised the film to Lord Halifax in 1937 (seen three
  times), with the "compulsory viewing for the SS" line in the reported conversation; *Das
  Schwarze Korps* reviewed it warmly. Exact wording carries a caveat in the literature.
- Birdwood — attendance ✅ **verified**: he represented Britain at the Ankara funeral, 21 November
  1938; too ill to march, he saluted the coffin from the Halkevi balcony, held upright on his
  feet. The "refused his boots, civilian shoes on the march" version is embellishment (Teyit.org
  has the photo-lore analysis).
- Piłsudski / the empty chair for the Polish ambassador — beloved legend; the non-recognition of
  the partitions is the defensible kernel.
- Christie writing in "Room 411 of the Pera Palace" — hotel-marketing legend tier.
- Einstein's 1933 letter — ✅ **verified, and better than the memo**: dated 17 September 1933,
  prompted by Sami Günzberg, addressed to PM İnönü; the ministry's annotations reject the OSE
  proposal, and İnönü's own handwritten note reads "their salaries will be unaffordable for us."
  The migration succeeded through the parallel Schwartz–Malche channel. The true story beats the
  myth — use it.
- Village Institutes — founded 1940, under İnönü. Dewey's 1924 report is real; the institutes are
  not an Atatürk-era institution. Fix the implication.
- Karabekir's "I and my army are at your command" salute scene; the Mussolini
  returned-in-uniform anecdote — memoir tier, label as such.
- Jabotinsky and Ambedkar nodes — thin sourcing; verify before leaning on them.
- Dilaçar as proposer of the surname "Atatürk" — contested attribution.
- UNESCO 1981 — ✅ **the resolution is real**: the 20th General Conference (1978) declared 1981
  the Atatürk centennial year, with the "eminent personalities… international understanding" text
  as quoted in UNESCO Türkiye / ATAM materials. The "adopted unanimously by all 152 members, a
  first in UNESCO history" framing is the Turkish-telling layer — quote the resolution, caveat
  the framing.

**The missing flagship the transcript never mentions:** the ANZAC **"Johnnies and Mehmets"**
speech (attributed 1934). Its provenance is seriously disputed — **checked**: no contemporary 1934
source is known; the attribution chain runs through interior minister Şükrü Kaya's 1953
recollection; Cengiz Özakıncı spent a decade in the archives without finding a 1934 delivery; and
Honest History (David Stephens) traces the key "no difference between the Johnnies and the
Mehmets" clause to an Australian hand in 1978 (the Brisbane *Gallipoli Fountains of Honour*
project). Paul Daley covered the dispute in *The Guardian*, April 2015. It is arguably the single
best exhibit for the entire
thesis: a grieving country needed a reconciliatory Atatürk and appears to have co-authored one,
and the words are now carved on memorials on two continents. Build it as a full case study, not
a caption.

explicitly labeled). And add no new people *from the transcript's unsourced pool* until the ledger
exists; the transcript's "more" loop is infinite, and the project's value now is depth, not
breadth. *(Amended Aug 2026: a verified-expansion layer is allowed — nodes added with sources
attached and tagged `verified`. Fourteen such nodes exist: seven people — Armstrong, Rıza Nur,

## 5. The 10 Kasım lens — the original, bounded contribution

**The date does double duty.** Atatürk died the morning of 10 November 1938, at 9:05 —
**the morning after Kristallnacht** (9–10 November 1938). The synagogues were still smoldering
when Ankara radio made the announcement, and Ihrig documents the intensity of German press
coverage of the death — the "star in the darkness" canonization is his account. (The sharper claim
that the coverage *eclipsed* Kristallnacht's is **not in Ihrig** (checked Oct 2026: p. 139 says it pushed the Putsch-anniversary coverage off front pages; Kristallnacht returns no hit) — the juxtaposition is our framing, not his finding.) Within the same frame: the dictator who mourned him loudest, and the
Jewish academics his republic was sheltering from that dictator. That coincidence is the
project's opening scene, its title's justification, and its ethical thesis in one image.

**Core corpus — nobody has assembled this for a general audience:** obituaries and death/funeral
coverage, **10–22 November 1938**, from 25–40 countries. Each obituary is one Rorschach card:
same event, radically different figure seen. Bounded, verifiable, original.

Where to pull it:
- Times Digital Archive; NYT TimesMachine; British Newspaper Archive
- Gallica (*Le Temps*, *Le Figaro*); ANNO (Austrian papers)
- **Trove** (Australia — free, rich, and the ANZAC gaze matters here); Papers Past (NZ)
- Chronicling America (US regional press) — *NB (Aug 2026): the old API endpoint is retired
  (404); the loc.gov replacement and UNESDOC are both bot-gated to automated clients — use a
  browser session or a keyed API from outside a sandbox*
- *Völkischer Beobachter* via Ihrig's citations / German libraries
- Pravda/Izvestia; *al-Ahram*; *Times of India* (ProQuest); Irish and Latin American archives
- Funeral delegation lists — **first pass done (Aug 2026)**: 28 countries represented, dignitaries
  of 17 nations marching, plus a League of Nations delegation, at the 21 Nov 1938 funeral. Named:
  Metaxas in person for Greece; von Neurath for Germany; Sarraut for France; Potemkin for the
  USSR; Dudley Pound and Birdwood for Britain; Aloisi for Italy; Nedić for Yugoslavia — and the
  exiled Amanullah Khan among the mourners. Coffin route: Dolmabahçe → torpedo boat →
  battlecruiser TCG *Yavuz* (19 Nov) → funeral train from İzmit (20 Nov) → Ankara; temporary rest
  at the Ethnography Museum until the Anıtkabir transfer on **10 November 1953** — the fifteenth
  anniversary, the ritual date reproducing itself. Full detail in the vault:
  `Death and State Funeral of Atatürk`. The rank each capital sent is a measurable weight of
  regard; the obituary corpus is this event refracted through 40 newspapers.

**Secondary layer:** 10 Kasım as evolving ritual inside Turkey — when the 9:05 siren practice
began, how commemoration changed decade by decade, and the modern flashpoints (*Mustafa* 2008,
Disney+ 2023). Ends the project on Turkey's own changing gaze.

## 7. Guardrails

- **Never launder uncertainty.** "Reportedly" and "alleged" survive into final copy; confidence
  tiers surface in the eventual UI rather than silently disappearing.
- **Two hagiographies to dodge**, not one: official/pop-Kemalist glorification *and*
  Islamist/nationalist counter-revisionism. Triangulate every contested episode across both
  Turkish and Western scholarship.
- **Law 5816** (1951, protection of Atatürk's memory) exists; be aware of it if publishing
  from/for Turkey. Documented history with visible sourcing is the defensible ground in every
  direction — framing does more work than avoidance.
- **Use the Turkish-language advantage**: ATAM publications (official, hagiographic, but
  document-rich), TBMM records, Cumhuriyet archive, TÜSTAV (left history), SALT Research
  (Istanbul). Doubles the corpus most English-language treatments never touch.
- ✅ Done: the Nobel nomination archive confirms the Venizelos nomination (entry #7872, letter
  dated 12 January 1934; the 1934 prize went to Arthur Henderson). The easy wins are real — keep
  taking them first.
