# Can Dündar

His documentary *Mustafa* opened around Republic Day 2008 and became the fastest-grossing Turkish
film of its year — over a million admissions, three weeks on top of the box office — by doing
something structurally simple and culturally explosive: treating Atatürk as a man. Depression,
heavy drinking, loneliness, women; the film calls him by his first name and cast a Greek child
actor for his boyhood, both of which became charges in themselves.

The Kemalist establishment's reaction is the datum: critics said the film's scenes degraded Atatürk's historical significance and served the interests of
Islamists (a paraphrase of the reported criticism; ⚠️ an earlier version of this note put it in quotation
marks as "diminishes the image of Atatürk in the minds of young Turks", which could not be found
verbatim in any source) — although Dündar is no critic
of Atatürk. Even *sympathetic* humanization breached the icon's protocol. A perfect exhibit of the
domestic gaze policing its own mirror, sixty years after the death. (Dündar was later prosecuted
and exiled in an unrelated press-freedom case — the chronicler of the republic's founder ending up
outside the republic.)
