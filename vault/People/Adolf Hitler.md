# Adolf Hitler

The hinge of this whole vault. Stefan Ihrig's *Atatürk in the Nazi Imagination* argues Hitler saw the
founder of the Turkish Republic not as a useful ally but as a direct role model — unlike his
tactical tolerance of Arab nationalists or his dismissal of [[Subhas Chandra Bose]], the admiration
here was personal and genuine.

**The blueprint.** Two defeated empires, two humiliating treaties ([[Treaty of Sèvres]] and
Versailles). Germany languished under Weimar; Atatürk fought his way to a rewrite. The 1923 Beer Hall
Putsch was modelled largely on the Ankara rebellion, and in 1933 Hitler called Atatürk "a star in
the darkness" — a line the German press reprinted into what Ihrig calls a "minor cult" around him
(p. 115). In 1938: *"Atatürk was the first to show that it is possible to mobilize and
regenerate the resources that a country has lost… Mussolini was his first and I his second student."*

**Ethnic cleansing as völkisch ideal.** The Nazi press celebrated the "New Turkey" as a pure
ethno-national state precisely because it had purged itself of foreign elements — the [[Armenian Genocide]] and Greek expulsions read as successful policy, not tragedy. Hence the alleged 1939 line to
his commanders before Poland: *"Who, after all, speaks today of the annihilation of the Armenians?"*

**Secularism.** He admired the one-party state and the crushing of the clergy — *"How fast Kemal
Atatürk dealt with the priests is one of the most amazing chapters of history!"* (1942) — rating it
above Mussolini's Italy, where the Pope still held sway.

**Racial placement.** Turks were not classed as backward Asiatics but ascribed real racial value,
near or on a par with Germanic peoples — consistently elevated, unlike the [[Nazi Outreach to the Muslim World]] where categories were merely suspended for utility. He kept a bust of Atatürk among
his cherished possessions (a contemporary account quoted in Ihrig, *Atatürk in the Nazi
Imagination*, p. 129). Atatürk's death in November 1938 dominated German front pages, pushing even
the directed coverage of the Hitler-Putsch anniversary to pages 2–3 (Ihrig p. 139). *(The
transcript's "eclipsed Kristallnacht" is not in Ihrig; the juxtaposition is the project's framing,
not his finding.)* See [[Hitler's Atatürk, 1933–1938]].

**On Islam and Arabs** (Speer, via Motadel): admiration for Islam as a martial political religion,
contempt for Arabs. Had Charles Martel lost at Tours in 732, he mused, Germanic peoples would have
converted and built a far greater civilisation — the Arabs, "because of their racial inferiority,"
could not have held it, so "not Arabs but Islamized Germans could have stood at the head of this
Mohammedan Empire."

*Note the triangle: the Nazis idolised Atatürk while their chief Arab collaborator [[Haj Amin al-Husseini]] considered him Islam's great betrayer.*
