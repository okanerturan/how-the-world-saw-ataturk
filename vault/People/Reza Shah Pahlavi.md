# Reza Shah Pahlavi

Idolised Atatürk. In 1934 he made the only state visit outside Iran of his life, to Ankara — the two
former soldiers conversed directly in Turkish, which he spoke fluently. He went home determined to
copy everything: top-down modernisation, state-led industrialisation, Western dress codes, and in
1936 the *Kashf-e hijab*, banning the veil in public, a more aggressive version of Atatürk's clothing
reforms.

**Where the copy failed.** Atatürk had spent a decade building a republican party (the CHP) and an
intellectual infrastructure to carry secularism. Reza Shah tried to enforce it through the brittle
apparatus of an absolute monarchy. When the British and Soviets deposed him during WWII the project
fractured instantly — setting up the religious backlash that culminated in 1979. Compare [[Amanullah Khan]] and [[King Zog I]].
