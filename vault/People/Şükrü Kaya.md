# Şükrü Kaya

Atatürk's Interior Minister for the last eleven years of his life — the operational enforcer of the
homogenizing state: the Settlement Law, the Surname Law machinery, the language campaigns, and
the interior ministry through the Dersim Rebellion years. His ministry ended on 11 November
1938 — the day after Atatürk's death — as İsmet İnönü took over.

His strange afterlife is in reception history. The official narrative has him delivering Atatürk's
Martyrs' Day message at Gallipoli on 18 March 1934; his 1953 recollection in the newspaper *Dünya*
is the seed from which the world-famous [[Johnnies and Mehmets]] text grew. The vault borrows the title of Reuben
Silverman's essay on Kaya for the paradox — call it **the Şükrü Kaya problem**: the most beloved
words of borderless reconciliation ever attached to Atatürk reach us through the voice of the
regime's chief homogenizer, and through no contemporary document at all. ⚠️ Silverman's essay
itself (checked 2026-10-29) is about Kaya as Interior Minister and Turkifier and says nothing about the
Martyrs' Day message, the 1953 *Dünya* interview or "Johnnies and Mehmets"; the paradox is the
vault's framing, and the 1953 interview comes from Özakıncı's *Bütün Dünya* article (the *Dünya*
issue of 10 November 1953).
