# Bakr Sidqi

The soldier who made the first Arab military coup, and a man who read Kemalist Turkey as an army
story. Britannica's entry (⚠️ unreachable on the 2026-10-29 re-check; wording not re-confirmed) says that after the coup of 29 October 1936 he "was not interested in
practical politics and devoted himself to reorganizing the army along the lines of that of
Kemalist Turkey." Karol Sorby adds the political bond: his "ability deeply impressed" [[Hikmat Sulayman]], and the two "were both inspired by Kemalist Turkey, which had been able to maintain
her independence only through the reorganization of her army." Sidqi, he writes, had made his
reputation during the Assyrian affair and posed as "the only soldier who could command the respect
of the entire army."

The documented act is a journey: in August 1937 Sidqi was **on his way to Turkey** (⚠️ Wikipedia's AP-sourced reports say he was about to board a flight to Turkey; the "Iraqi
military mission" and "invitation from the Turkish government" are not found in any source reachable on
2026-10-29) when he was shot at
the air-force base in Mosul on 11 August, together with the RAF commander Muhammad Ali Jawad (this
last detail is from a Wikipedia article and the trip's Turkish end is not independently sourced
here — T-tier). Whatever Atatürk made of the invitation, the pairing says what the officers wanted
from Turkey: the army-led republic.

His Kemalism, on this evidence, was borrowed machinery rather than a creed. For the other pole of the Iraqi mirror, see [[Faisal I of Iraq]].
