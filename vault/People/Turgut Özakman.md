# Turgut Özakman

Playwright, TRT figure, and screenwriter of the *Kurtuluş* television epic — then, in 2005, author
of *Şu Çılgın Türkler* ("Those Crazy Turks"), a "documentary novel" of the War of Independence
built, by his account, on 57 years of collected material. It became the best-selling book in the
history of the republic — 622,000 copies within roughly its first year, printings running into the
hundreds (sources conflict on the "311th printing" date: mid-2006 in one account, 2013 in his
obituary; ledger row exists) — record-breaking sales for a Turkish book.

His place in the deck: the **modern Turkish self-gaze in book form** — the war retold as national
scripture for readers who felt the founding story slipping. It matters to this project's method
too: the book sits inside the provenance chain of the apocryphal [[David Lloyd George]] "genius of
centuries" quote, which it cites from an ATAM journal article and helped fix in popular memory. A
reception machine, not just a reception artifact.
