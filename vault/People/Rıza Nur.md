# Rıza Nur

Second delegate at Treaty of Lausanne under İsmet İnönü and a minister of the early
republic — then a bitter enemy in Paris exile. There he wrote *Hayat ve Hatıratım* ("My Life and
Memoirs"), covering his life to 1935, and staged its afterlife like a time bomb: he personally
deposited the manuscript at the British Museum on 4 June 1935 with the condition that it stay
sealed until 1960, with further copies lodged at the Bibliothèque nationale and Leiden.

Rediscovered by researcher Cavit Orhan Tütengil in 1964 and published in Istanbul in four volumes
(1967–68), the memoirs caused a storm: a view of Mustafa Kemal and his circle so hostile, personal
and frequently unreliable that the book was banned — and became, precisely because of that, the
founding text of the domestic counter-narrative. Scholars handle it with tongs; no serious account
of the era can skip it.

His place in the deck: the **hostile internal mirror** — the man who sat beside İnönü at Lausanne,
telling the story with all the venom the official [[Nutuk]] narrative leaves out. See
[[Historiography of Atatürk]].
