# Albert Einstein

Not a political admirer but a direct participant. In 1933, as the Nazis purged Jewish academics, he
wrote to the Turkish government as honorary president of the OSE pleading for asylum for 40 prominent
Jewish scientists. (Source check, Nov 2026: the letter of 17 September 1933 is addressed to Prime Minister
İsmet İnönü and says nothing about Atatürk or the reforms; the anecdote that Einstein called Atatürk "the
greatest leader of the century" traces only to recent web retellings.) Atatürk's government enthusiastically took them — and hundreds more. Those
German-Jewish exiles became the foundational professors of the reformed Turkish university system.
The vacancy they filled had been engineered by the [[1933 University Reform]]; the logistics were run
by [[Philipp Schwartz]]. See [[Ernst Reuter]], [[Alexander Rüstow]], [[Bruno Taut]].
