# Leon Trotsky

The great irony of 1929: Stalin exiled the world's most famous communist revolutionary, and
practically every European democracy refused him. The one leader who granted a visa was a fiercely
anti-communist dictator who had banned the Communist Party outright.

Trotsky spent four years on Büyükada (Prinkipo) off Istanbul under Turkish police protection, fishing
in the Sea of Marmara and writing *History of the Russian Revolution* and *My Life* there. He was
immensely grateful and intellectually respectful: the Kemalist revolution was strictly "bourgeois,"
he wrote, but the demolition of Sultanate and Caliphate was a massive progressive leap that shattered
the region's medieval stagnation. For Ankara it was a flex of sovereignty — proof of total
independence from Moscow's dictates.
