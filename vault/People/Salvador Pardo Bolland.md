# Salvador Pardo Bolland

**A Mexican diplomat reading the Democrat Party's Turkey through Atatürk.** Mexico had no resident
representative in Ankara between the early 1940s and 1951 (its Turkey post had been run from Madrid
until the break with Franco). In 1951 Bolland arrived as provisional chargé d'affaires and stayed
until 3 December 1953, writing the reports to the Mexican Foreign Ministry that Ayşe Yarar mined in
*Cumhuriyet Tarihi Araştırmaları Dergisi* (2025). Two things in them are about Atatürk.

First, the **posthumous cult under attack**. Bolland reported to Mexico City that a secret group of
religious fanatics wanting the Caliphate restored was working in Turkey, and that its activity was
limited to toppling statues of Atatürk and "small terrorist acts" — in his view nothing to
take seriously. Yarar notes the context he does not name: the Ticani (Tijani) order's attacks on
Atatürk busts in 1950–51 and the Democrat government's Law to Protect Atatürk (25 July 1951) that
answered them. (Yarar's own comment is that the Mexican underrated the danger of such groups.)

Second, a **historical verdict**. Explaining why Ankara refused every invitation to join a Muslim or
Asian bloc (Pakistan's foreign minister Zafrulla Khan had just proposed a Morocco-to-Indonesia
union, February 1952), Bolland wrote — in Yarar's paraphrase — that Turkey chose "the West and not
the Asian Muslim political-cultural group" and that "in Bolland's phrase, Kemal Atatürk's work had
already begun to bear fruit": Turkey had left the Asian-Muslim camp to become a Western link. A
footnote adds that his report contains further comments on Atatürk's "thought and practice" which
Yarar leaves for another study — a lead, not yet read.

What makes him interesting for the deck: he is a Mexican, i.e. a citizen of the other great
twentieth-century secularising revolution (compare Plutarco Elías Calles), and a *critical* observer
of Washington's hold on the Menderes-era government — yet his Atatürk is a plain success story. The
limit: this is a historian's summary of dispatches, not the dispatches (Mexico's Archivo Histórico
Genaro Estrada, SRE).
