# Pope John XXIII

As Archbishop Angelo Roncalli he served as Apostolic Delegate to Turkey from 1934/35 to 1944 —
arriving at the absolute peak of aggressive secularisation, when the state was closing religious
institutions and, from 1934, banning clerical garb in public for clergy of any religion.

You would expect a bitter critic. Instead he bought a civilian suit, a bowler hat and an umbrella and
walked Istanbul looking like a European businessman; learned fluent Turkish; and, in a striking break
with Catholic practice, delivered parts of the Mass and official prayers in Turkish rather than
Latin. On Atatürk's death in 1938 he expressed genuine sorrow, telling Rome the world had lost a
great man. When he became Pope the Turkish press affectionately dubbed him the "Turkish Pope." He
later modernised the Church itself through Vatican II.
