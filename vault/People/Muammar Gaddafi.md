# Muammar Gaddafi

Built his regime on a volatile mix of Arab nationalism, socialism and Islamic populism, and read
Atatürk not as an anti-imperialist hero but as a catastrophic traitor. By abolishing the Caliphate,
imposing the Latin alphabet and turning Turkey's back on the Middle East to face Europe, Atatürk had
in his view abandoned the Islamic world to Western imperialism. He used Kemalism as a rhetorical
punching bag: the definitive example of a nation losing its soul by mimicking its colonisers. Compare
[[Gamal Abdel Nasser]], who drew the opposite conclusion.

(Source check, Nov 2026: this characterisation is inherited from the transcript, and his documented
record cuts both ways. In May 1981 he sent Kenan Evren a centenary message saying "Atatürk was a great
leader not only for the Turkish nation but for the whole Islamic world and for the Arabs" (Turkish text
in *Milliyet*, 21 May 1981, p. 9; see [[World Leaders on Atatürk — Round 30 Sweep]]). His 2002 essay
"Turkey, Europe and the Bin-Ladenists" attacks the alphabet reform but names Atatürk only once,
neutrally. No source for "traitor" has been found. See [[Postwar and Third World Leaders on Atatürk in Their Own Words]].)
