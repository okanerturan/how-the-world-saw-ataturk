# Imam Yahya of Yemen

A highland imam who needed Turks twice: as the last Ottoman governor's ally against the British
and Idrisi in 1918–23, and as a distant great power to be handled afterwards. The record is
relationship, not rhetoric — and what Yahya said of Mustafa Kemal himself survives only in
paraphrase.

- **The treaty.** The Treaty of Da'an (1911) had made Yahya, "who frequently rebelled against the
  central authority", an ally of Istanbul (Ü. Gülsüm Polat, *Atatürk Araştırma Merkezi Dergisi*
  33/96, 2017). After the Mudros armistice he stayed on as the Ottomans' principal local ally;
  Thomas Kuehn's article in *Contemporary Levant* (2024, abstract) says he "received the political
  backing of the Ottoman resistance movement in Anatolia under Mustafa Kemal", until "in 1923
  Mustafa Kemal and Yahya turned to state-building projects emphasising post-Ottoman forms of
  sovereignty".
- **The letter to Faisal.** Polat, following Öke & Karaman, reports that around 1920–21 Yahya —
  "probably at the instigation of" the ex-governor Mahmud Nedim Bey, who stayed in Sanaa —
  wrote to [[Faisal I of Iraq]] expressing satisfaction at his **cooperation with Mustafa Kemal**
  and stressing the importance of cooperating against the Westerners. Faisal answered that the
  Ottomans "have passed"; the Arabs should look after their own interests. Both men were
  hedging.
- **The Ankara government's reply.** Polat's judgment is that Ankara did not (could not) grant
  Mahmud Nedim's requests for support: the Grand National Assembly remembered Yemen as the grave
  of Turkish soldiers, and in the caliphate debate an MP shouted that the title "has eaten ten
  million Turks in Yemen." Correspondence until Lausanne was mostly congratulations on
  victories and replies from Ankara.
- **The abolition did not matter.** Polat reports that the abolition of the sultanate and the
  caliphate "caused no reaction in Yemen": the Zaydis of Sanaa already recognised their living
  imam as caliph; Mahmud Nedim even wrote Ankara his pleasure at the end of the sultanate.
  Compare the outrage in [[Shakib Arslan]] or [[Rashid Rida]]'s Cairo.
- **1926 onward.** Yahya signed a ten-year friendship and trade treaty with Mussolini's Italy
  (September 1926) and took arms and technical aid; Turkish diplomats tracked the Italian
  advance; Turkey's 1929 friendship treaty with [[Ibn Saud]] did not include Yemen, and the
  actual Turkish presence after Lausanne "did not have the character to disturb the great
  powers". Former Ottoman officers helped modernise the Yemeni army.

Verdict: an instrumental gaze. Mustafa Kemal was useful in 1919–23, irrelevant to Zaydi
legitimacy after the caliphate went, and a piece of the great-power board after 1926. What is
still missing is any direct Yahya statement about Atatürk in 1924–38.
