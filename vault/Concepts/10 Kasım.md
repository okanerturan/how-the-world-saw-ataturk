# 10 Kasım

The project's namesake. Every 10 November at 9:05 — the minute of the death at Dolmabahçe in 1938
— sirens sound across Turkey and the country halts: traffic stops on the bridges, pedestrians
freeze, then the national anthem. The silence lasts two minutes; every other Turkish ceremony
observes one. Schools run Atatürk Week (10–16 November); flags fly at half-mast.

The date organises the whole memory system:
- **1938** — the death and [[Death and State Funeral of Atatürk]].
- **1953** — the transfer to [[Anıtkabir]], deliberately staged on the fifteenth anniversary.
- **2008** — [[Can Dündar]]'s *Mustafa* premieres into the anniversary season and the humanization
  row erupts.
- **2023** — the centennial year's [[2023 Atatürk Series Controversy]].

**The origin question — answered (Aug 2026).** The first commemoration was held on 10 November
1939, the first anniversary, run off a standardized national program issued by the government on
**5 November 1939** and distributed through the Halkevleri: ceremonies opened at 9:05 with a
siren, followed by a **five-minute** silence "in the spiritual presence of Atatürk," a talk on his life, and a
wreath at the town's Atatürk monument — the core ritual grammar of siren, silence and wreath in place from year
one (Akça's study of Muğla's ceremonies, 1939–1949). The mourning anthem played by the Community Center
band and the black Atatürk portrait on the front page of Muğla's *Halk* newspaper are local Muğla practice
in Akça, not items of the national program. Today's silence is
two minutes: the shortening is itself an unstudied evolution thread.

The scholarly frame for the ritual's meaning is Nazlı Ökten, "An Endless Death and an Eternal
Mourning: November 10 in Turkey," in Esra Özyürek (ed.), *The Politics of Public Memory in Turkey*
(Syracuse UP, 2007), 95–113. The observance's later privatization and image-boom is Özyürek's
*Nostalgia for the Modern* territory — Turkey's own gaze, changing decade by decade.
