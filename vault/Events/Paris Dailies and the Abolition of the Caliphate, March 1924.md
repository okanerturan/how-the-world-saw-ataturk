# Paris Dailies and the Abolition of the Caliphate, March 1924

The Grand National Assembly abolished the caliphate on 3 March 1924. The Paris dailies of 4–7 March are mostly Havas copy; this card keeps the places where
a paper speaks in its own voice about Kemal. It extends [[Paris Press on the Caliphate, the Civil Code and the Decade, 1924–1933]], which reads La Croix of 6 and 8 March, and it corrects that card: a La Croix item first attributed to 9 March is in fact in the **7 March** issue. Context: Abolition of the Caliphate. Every quotation below is a Gallica `ContentSearch` fragment copied exactly as the OCR gives it (so "Moustapha", "Mustapha", "Kernal" for Kemal and other OCR slips are kept, marked [OCR] where they matter). Fragments are short and are cut with "(...)"; "PAG_n" is Gallica's view number (the n-th image of the issue, not always the printed page). Only what a fragment shows is claimed.

**La Croix** (Catholic), 7 March 1924, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k261789f)) — the second article of a series (the text refers to "le premier article dans la Croix du 4 mars"). The fragments run: a protest against *"cette intolérance jeune-turque qui, incapable de mettre sur pied des écoles, s'efforce de détruire celles que la France a multipliées dans le monde ottoman"*; then *"Je sais bien les excuses que donnent à cette politique sectaire des journaux comme le Temps, toujours prêts à saluer la « laïcité »"*; then *"Mustapha Kemal ne ferme pas les écoles chrétiennes, disent(...)Et, puisque Mustapha Kemal prétend inaugurer(...)"* — a Catholic paper doubting the line that Kemal spares Christian schools and, by name, reproaching *Le Temps* for applauding Turkish secularism. This is the quotation of the withdrawn 9 March record, now found in its right issue; the truncation after "prétend inaugurer" means the paper's full sentence is not shown.

**Le Temps**, 5 March, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k2453995)), leader on the Turkish decisions: *"La politique laïque et nationale qui se développe ainsi"*, and, in contrast with the Caliph's protest, *"En revanche, ces principes sont compris et maintenus par Moustapha Kemal pacha"* [OCR "Moustapha' Kemal paclia"]. The same view shows the counter-voices it reports: that the suppression "indigne les musulmans de l'Inde" and that "on va trop loin". Which principles the leader means is cut off in the fragments.

**Journal des débats**, 5 March, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k490711v)): a long account of the fall of the Caliph names *"Moustafa Kémal le Victorieux"* as the man whose "contrôle le plus strict" over the new elections removed most hodjas and brought in partisans of the lay regime, says he "vivait à l'occidentale" with a wife unveiled, and that he "aurait voulu qu'on ne bannit pas [OCR: "ho bannit"] les membres de la famille impériale" and had put down a motion ("présenté à cet effet une motion") to that effect.

**Le Petit Journal**, 5 March, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k628753x)): "Le raisonnement de Mustapha Kemal pacha et de ses partisans est des plus simples" and a clause that Kemal and the Assembly "n'avaient pas osé bou[le]verser" — an explaining voice, no judgment in the fragment.

**L'Humanité**, 5 March, view 3 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k401251s)), under "Après la suppression": the speech Kemal gave on Saturday "n'était que le prélude des décisions importantes que vient d'adopter l'Assemblée nationale turque". **Le Populaire**, 4 March, view 3 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k818709n)): "Le discours de Kemal ... Mustapha Kernel a relevé la volonté ferme de la nation turque d'assurer des bases solides au régime républicain" [OCR]. Both report; neither judges.
