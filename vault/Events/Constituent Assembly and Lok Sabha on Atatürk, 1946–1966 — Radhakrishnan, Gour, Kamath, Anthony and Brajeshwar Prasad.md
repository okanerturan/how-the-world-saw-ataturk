# Constituent Assembly and Lok Sabha on Atatürk, 1946–1966 — Radhakrishnan, Gour, Kamath, Anthony and Brajeshwar Prasad

Found by archive.org full-text search over the Lok Sabha Secretariat's digitised debates. In the Constituent Assembly Kemal is a weapon in the argument over religion and the state (Gour, Radhakrishnan, against Jinnah's Pakistan) and over strong government (Brajeshwar Prasad); after independence he becomes an example in Lok Sabha: the ragged army (Kamath 1949), whipping and flogging (Anthony 1955, Kamath 1963). Nehru's own view: Jawaharlal Nehru.

## S. Radhakrishnan (1947-01-20)

> Allenby's liberating campaigns in Egypt, Lawrence's adventures in Arabia, Kemal Pasha's defiant creation of a secular Turkey, point out that the days of religious States are over.

Constituent Assembly of India Debates, vol. II, 20 Jan 1947 (Objectives Resolution), p. 272. Speaker identified from the nearest preceding speaker line ('Sir S. Radhakrishnan (United Provinces: General)'); the page header 272 precedes the passage. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Hari Singh Gour (1946-12-19)

> Kemal Pasha Ata-Turk saw the futility and unwisdom of combining politics with religion. The first thing he did was to put an end to Pakistan and establish the Republic of Turkey.

Constituent Assembly of India Debates, vol. I, 19 Dec 1946, p. 149 (page header 150 follows the passage). Gour's 'Pakistan' here means the old Ottoman Caliphate-Sultanate arrangement (his own use of the word); the vault does not adopt his framing of Muslim politics. Speaker header 'Dr. Sir Hari Singh Gour (C. P. and Berar' precedes the passage. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Brajeshwar Prasad (1948-11-09)

> The system of government set up by Hitler, Mussolini, Kemal Ataturk and Stalin represent the indirect forms of democracy. The whole of Germany, Italy and Turkey were behind the dictators.

Constituent Assembly of India Debates, vol. VII, 9 Nov 1948, pp. 373-374. Speaks for 'philosopher-kings' and one-party rule; the same speech says 'Germany, Italy and Turkey rose to grand heights under Hitler, Mussolini and Kemal Ataturk' and 'the records of Mushtapha Kemal and Mussolini are as good as that of the leaders of parliamentary democracy' (p. 374). Sensitive framing (fascist comparison) is the speaker's own. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Hari Vishnu Kamath (1949-05-16)

> At the close of World War I, Kemal Ataturk with his ragged army stood alone against many of the powerful countries of Europe and beat them back.

Constituent Assembly of India Debates, vol. VIII, 16 May 1949, p. 23. Speaker taken from the running header '[Shri H. V. Kamath]' on the preceding page; he returns to Kemal on 7 Sept 1949 (the Roman-script reform, as an argument against prejudice towards English). Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Frank Anthony (1955-11-30)

> Mustafa Kemal Ata Turk stamped out black-marketing in foodgrains because he introduced whipping.

Lok Sabha Debates, 30 Nov 1955, Abolition of Whipping Bill, c. 804. Used for Anthony's own argument; an unverified claim about Kemal's policy, recorded only as his view. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Hari Vishnu Kamath (1963-08-22)

> Remember that Kemal Ataturk of Turkey as soon as he came to power, for a small reform like stopping spitting in the streets prescribed flogging

Lok Sabha Debates, 22 Aug 1963 (no-confidence motion), cols. c. 2174-75 (OCR 2175). Kamath repeats the story on 17 Apr 1963 and 25 Aug 1966 (flogging bill debates), always as a model of drastic penalty. Column from OCR page header '2175 Motion'. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.
