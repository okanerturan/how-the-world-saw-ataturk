# Venizelos Warns Britain of Kemal, The Times 8 July 1921

In July 1921, with the Greek army committed in Anatolia and Venizelos out of office (he had lost the November
1920 election and was in exile), the Liberal MP Harold Spender sent *The Times* notes of a recent interview.
The paper printed them on page 10 on 8 July 1921 under "Why Greece Is Fighting", beside the report that
General Harrington had been authorised to meet Mustafa Kemal on the Black Sea coast.

What Venizelos says of Kemal is framed as an argument to a British audience for continued Allied support of
Greece. These are Spender's notes of the exchange, not a stenographic record.

- On the 1920 Kemalist advance he says the Kemalists "defied the Allies and attacked them both in the
  Constantinople area and Cilicia" in June 1920 and "placed in jeopardy the freedom of the Straits".
- On the 1921 position: *"For if Kemal reaches Constantinople the freedom of the sea is involved, and your
  supremacy in the Mediterranean."* He also says "the Russian Bolshevist Government are supplying the
  Kemalists" and that if "Kemal and the Bolshevists" took Constantinople "they would defy all the Christian
  Powers" (the reprint prints "got the Constantinople"; transcription error, flagged).
- He calls the situation "the old war still unfinished" and says the Treaty (Sèvres) "has never been enforced".

**Reading.** Kemal appears as a military and geopolitical threat, not a character; there is no personal
description. The 1930 rapprochement and the 1934 Nobel nomination are the same man's later stance (see
[[Venizelos Nominates Kemal for the Nobel Peace Prize, 1934]] and [[Eleftherios Venizelos]]). Context:
[[Greco-Turkish War]].
