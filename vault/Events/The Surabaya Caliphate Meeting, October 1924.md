# The Surabaya Caliphate Meeting, October 1924

Seven months after Ankara abolished the caliphate, Muslims of the Dutch East Indies were still arguing about who should hold it, and one answer on the table was Mustafa Kemal.

**Before the meeting.** Chiara Formichi (*Islam and Asia: A History*, Cambridge 2020, p. 128) writes that until a Central Caliphate Committee was set up in Surabaya, "not earlier than October 1924", public opinion in the Indies "had been overall unconcerned with the Ottomans' defeat, and was supportive of Mustafa Kemal's actions"; the Indies press had reported his military bravery since the late 1910s. Martin van Bruinessen (*Studia Islamika* 1995) says the same: his resistance movement "enjoyed widespread sympathy among newspaper-reading Indonesian Muslims". The earlier, louder side of that sympathy is in [[Sarekat Islam and the Cheribon Telegram, 1922–1923]].

**The meeting, 4–5 October 1924.** Leaders of the Sarekat Islam, Muhammadiyah and Al-Irshad met in Surabaya to decide whether to send a delegation to the caliphate congress in Cairo. Van Bruinessen tells it from the report of the Dutch adviser on native affairs, R.A. Kern, to the governor general (23 October 1924). The standing committee of the Al-Islam Congress, elected at Garut in May, sent a letter asking the meeting to nominate Mustafa Kemal as the new caliph. The proposal was discussed seriously. Tjokroaminoto said he approved of Turkey's attitude but that it was too early to judge what Kemal's secularization aimed at. Haji Fakhruddin of Muhammadiyah carried the meeting with the view that the caliph should sit in Mecca. The meeting set up the Centraal Comité Chilafat.

**Boundary.** Nobody's own words survive here: the letter and Tjokroaminoto's answer are known from Kern's summary as a historian retells it. Kern's report (KITLV, Kern collection H 797 no. 316) has not been seen.
