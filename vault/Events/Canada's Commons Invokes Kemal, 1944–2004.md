# Canada's Commons Invokes Kemal, 1944–2004

After the Chanak debates of 1922–1923 ([[Canada Says Not Yet at Chanak, September 1922]], [[Canada's Commons on Chanak, February 1923]]) and [[Rodney Adamson]]'s "absolute dictator" of 1943, Kemal comes up in the Canadian House of Commons as an example to argue with. LiPaD (University of Toronto), which holds the Hansard from the 1900s to 2019, answers scripts with a bot check. Its search was run in a browser on 18 Nov 2026: "Ataturk" returns 6 speeches, "Atatürk" 7 and "Kemal" 22, some of them the same.

- **Jean-François Pouliot, 17 February 1944.** The government was briefing only the party leaders in secret. That reminded him "of Kemal Pasha who, in Turkey, chose his own leader of the opposition; and he was considered a very great man-both of them." `pouliot-1944-02-17`
- **Stockwell Day and Francine Lalonde, 20 April 2004**, in the debate on a private member's motion to recognise the Armenian genocide. Both invoke the founder against today's Turkish government. Day: "Mustafa Kemal Ataturk himself recognized and commented on it." Lalonde: Kemal "repeatedly, dozens of times, condemned the massacres". These are their claims; the archive records them as said. `stockwell-day-2004-04-20`, `francine-lalonde-2004-04-20`

Not entered: Republic Day and Anzac statements by members (Obhrai 2011, Vellacott 2010 and 2015, Nicholls 2012 and 2013) that repeat textbook lines; Julian Reed 1999 and Eleni Bakopanos 1995/1996 in passing; index entries of 1923.
