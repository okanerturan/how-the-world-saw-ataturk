# Turkish Writers and Contemporaries on Kemal — Round 33

Turkish writers, thinkers, comrades, family and opponents, in their own documented words about Kemal or in documented first-hand contact with him (round 33). Many had vault notes built from the project's original unsourced transcript; these records are what survives verification.

## Nâzım Hikmet, 1921
*Young poet who had crossed to Anatolia to join the national struggle* — witness, meeting, evidence S.

Received in Ankara with Vâlâ Nureddin as 'two young poets'; Kemal at once turned the conversation to poetry.

> None

Mediated by: Göksu & Timms, *Romantic Communist* (1999), section 'Ankara: poetry with a purpose' (from p. 19) ← Vâlâ Nureddin's memoir, p. 95.

⚠️ First-hand contact rule (world-famous writer). Read only as archive.org search-inside snippets of a lending-only book ('When they were presented to the great man as two young poets, Kemal immediately turned the subject to poetry'); page not shown, and the 'Va-Nu, 56-67' citation seen attaches to the neighbouring sentence, so the exact source of the meeting passage is not confirmed. Nâzım's own words at the meeting are not given. Date from the context (1921, the poets' arrival in Ankara). Weak: needs the book page or Vâlâ Nureddin's memoir. Check: snippet runs on to Kemal's advice 'I recommend you to write poetry with a purpose' (Va-Nu, 95); the poets sailed from Istanbul on 1 Jan 1921.

## Yahya Kemal Beyatlı, 1921
*Poet and essayist, writing for the Istanbul press during the National Struggle* — admiring, article, evidence S.

Argues that since the Greek landing at İzmir Mustafa Kemal is no longer an individual but the symbol of the risen nation.

> Bu eser bir şahsın değil bir milletindir. Mustafa Kemal'i bir şahıs zannedenler aldanıyorlar.

Mediated by: Enginün, Yeni Türk Edebiyatı: Tanzimat'tan Cumhuriyet'e 1839-1923 (Dergâh), pp. 124-125 ← Eğil Dağlar (1966), pp. 31-33, article 'Eser'.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'This work belongs not to one person but to a nation. Those who take Mustafa Kemal for one person are mistaken.' He goes on: 'Mustafa Kemal İzmir'e efzunlar çıktığı günden evvel bir fertti. O günden beri artık bir fert değil bir timsaldir.' Same wording (with 'Efzunlar' and 'bir fertdi') in the essay 'Atatürk - Yahya Kemal dostluğu' in Kanbak (comp.), Yahya Kemal Beyatlı, pp. 78-79, apparently from Atatürk Araştırma Merkezi Dergisi. DATE INFERRED: Eğil Dağlar collects his 1919-1922 articles; 'Eser' speaks of the movement creating its symbol 'within one year' of the May 1919 landing, so c. 1920-21; the exact paper and date are not established. In 'Onun Sesi' he praises a speech of Kemal's as free of bombast, egotism and ambition (Eğil Dağlar p. 35). Contacts: a reconciliation dinner at Çankaya on 21 Dec 1933, later table invitations and his election as deputy (Kanbak pp. 82-84); Afet İnan recalls him reading poems at Atatürk's table (Hatıralar ve Belgeler, p. 396). His 1938 funeral writings were not found.

## Ziya Gökalp, 1922-10
*Sociologist and poet, theorist of Turkism; editor of Küçük Mecmua (Diyarbakır)* — admiring, poem, evidence S.

In verse petitions to the Gazi, hails him as genius and saviour from the Greeks, begging him to save Turks from ignorance and backwardness.

> Sen dâhisin, buna çoktan inandık.. / Mefküresiz rehberlerden pek yandık..

Mediated by: Kanbak (comp.), Ziya Gökalp (archive.org, 2025), pp. 20-21, 73-74 ← Devrim Yazarlarının Kalemiyle Milli Mücadele ve Gazi Mustafa Kemal (Kültür Bakanlığı, 1981), p. 1020 ← Küçük Mecmua nos. 20-21 (Oct 1922); dating by Cavit Orhan Tütengil, Türk Dili no. 302 (Nov 1976), repr. Kanbak pp. 72–74: Küçük Mecmua nos. 20–21, Oct 1922.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'You are a genius, we long believed it.. / We have suffered enough from guides without an ideal..' Poem 'İstid'â — Gazi Paşa Hazretleri'ne', each stanza ending 'Kurtar bizi...!' (Save us!), including from 'the Black Sultan' (Vahideddin). The essay reprinted in the same compilation dates the two İstid'âs to Küçük Mecmua nos. 20 and 21 (October 1922). In the second ('İkinci İstid'â') Gökalp argues against Kemal's stated wish to become a private citizen after the peace: 'Bu milletin hâli olur pek yaman, / Kılavuzu olmazsa bir kahraman'. His 1923 Altın Işık adds 'Mustafa Kemal'dir baş kahramanı!'. Küçük Mecmua itself not seen. Contact: the essay, citing E. B. Şapolyo, says Kemal summoned Gökalp to Çankaya, after which Gökalp wrote Doğru Yol on the party's nine principles. The vault note's 'My flesh and bones belong to my parents...' line is Kemal's saying about Gökalp, not Gökalp's gaze, and was not checked.

## Abdülmecid II, 1924
*Last Ottoman Caliph, deposed and expelled in March 1924* — hostile, quote, evidence P.

In exile at Territet, rebuts Kemal's insinuation that Islam bars progress: those who fight faith are not servants of progress.

> Those who combat its influence cannot be numbered among the servants of progress.

Mediated by: Ferdinand Tuohy, The Cockpit of Peace (1926), pp. 314-315.

⚠️ Interview by the British journalist Ferdinand Tuohy at the ex-Caliph's hotel in Territet, c. June 1924 (his wives had not left their rooms 'for three months'). Kemal is named in Tuohy's framing ('Kemal's insinuations that the Moslem religion was a bar to progress, seemed mostly to pre-occupy Abdul'), not in the quoted words, which defend Islam ('The spirit of Islam not only doesn't hinder real progress, but ... demands it'). Tuohy also reports him saying Kemal had given him £1,200 and a ticket to the frontier. English as printed (Abdülmecid probably spoke French). His formal 1924 protest declaration and his diaries were not found in free sources.

## Sabahattin Ali, 1933-04-14
*Turkish novelist and poet; German teacher held in Konya prison* — instrumental, letter, evidence P.

Jailed for a poem said to insult the Gazi, writes to him denying it, regrets his great name serving personal revenge, and asks pardon.

> Beni en çok üzen yediğim ceza değil, sizin büyük isminizin şahsî intikam vasıtası olarak kullanılabilmesi ve buna müsamaha edilmesi keyfiyetidir.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'What grieves me most is not the sentence I received, but that your great name could be used as an instrument of personal revenge, and that this is tolerated.' Typed petition headed 'Reisicumhur Gazi Mustafa Kemal Hazretlerine', dated '14- IV - 1933', signed 'Konya Hapisanesinde mevkuf, Konya Muhtelit Ortamektep almanca muallimi Sabahattin Ali', with a revenue stamp, office annotations and the stamp 'Cumhurbaşkanlığı Arşivi'. Read from the facsimile (Wikimedia Commons, source given as a Habertürk image of 10 Aug 2018) and the proofread tr.wikisource transcription (quality level 4), which agree. He was serving a one-year sentence for 'implicitly and allusively insulting' the President in a poem; he asks first to be believed ('Ben böyle bir şey yapmadım'), then for pardon ('Beni affedecek kadar büyük ve iyi kalpli olduğunuzdan eminim'). Flattery in a plea for pardon, hence instrumental. Whether Kemal answered is not established here (his release in the 1933 amnesty is tier M, unverified). The poem itself was not found.

## Mehmet Âkif Ersoy, 1935-08
*Poet of the İstiklâl Marşı, former Burdur deputy, living in Cairo* — hostile, quote, evidence P.

Reportedly said in Antakya that the Gazi owed Rauf his life, honour and position many times over, yet worked to ruin him.

> Zira Gazi paşa Rauf beyi çekemez. Çünki Gazi paşa def'atla hayatını, şerefini ve mevkiini Rauf beye medyundur.

Mediated by: TBMM, İstiklâl Marşı ve Mehmet Âkif Ersoy: Belgeler (2021), doc. 8.3 (from p. 84) ← İstanbul Valiliği to the Interior Minister ← informant '117', report no. 5 of 6 Sept 1935.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'For the Gazi cannot bear Rauf Bey. Because the Gazi has owed his life, his honour and his position to Rauf Bey time and again.' Hearsay in a police informant's report, a hostile and interested source; the report presents it as what 'Şair Akif ... Atatürk hakkında demişdir'. OCR normalised ('mevki- ini' → 'mevkiini'). Âkif was in Antakya (then French mandate Sanjak) for about three weeks and left on 31 Aug 1935. The same reports say he called the abolition of the Caliphate 'en büyük bir dinsizlik' and praised Abdülmecid; those are about the regime, not Kemal personally. The same TBMM volume prints Kemal's 1920 telegram arranging Âkif's election for Burdur (a contact from Kemal's side). Âkif's letters from Egypt were not found in free sources.

## Mahmut Esat Bozkurt, 1940
*Former Minister of Justice (1924-30), professor at the Institute of the Turkish Revolution* — admiring, book, evidence P.

Presents Atatürk as a man of great self-denial who resigned his command to enter Erzurum as a plain citizen and became the nation's saviour.

> Bu Türk oğlu, bu sade vatandaş, yarınki hür ve müstakil Türk milletinin, Türk ulusunun kurtarıcısı olacaktı. Ve oldu.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'This son of the Turks, this plain citizen, was to become the saviour of the free and independent Turkish nation of tomorrow. And so he did.' Atatürk İhtilali, I (İstanbul Üniversitesi Yayınları, İnkılâp Enstitüsü lectures; İstanbul: Burhaneddin Matbaası, 1940), p. 129 (archive.org OCR). The same page begins 'Atatürk büyük ferağet sahibi idi' and adds first-hand: 'Atatürkten bir çok defalar işittim', quoting Kemal on feeling himself the strongest man once he became a plain citizen. The vault note's 'realities of life, not revelations from the sky' (Civil Code) is not about Kemal and was not checked.

## Nâzım Hikmet, 1945-11-10
*Turkish poet and communist, then a prisoner in Bursa* — admiring, poem, evidence P.

In verse from his War of Independence epic, shows Kemal on Kocatepe before the 1922 offensive as a blond wolf with flashing blue eyes.

> Sarışın bir kurda benziyordu / Ve mavi gözleri çakmak çakmaktı

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'He looked like a blond wolf / And his blue eyes flashed like flint.' Printed in the weekly Gün, no. 2 (10 Kasım 1945, the Atatürk anniversary issue), under 'İstiklal Savaşı Destanından: Atatürk', signed 'Nazım HİKMET' (archive.org OCR; the line break 'çak- maktı' joined here). This is the Kocatepe passage of the epic later published as Kuvâyi Milliye; that it was written in Bursa prison c. 1939-41 is tier M, not checked. Dated by this first printing found, while he was still in prison. The unsigned prose on Atatürk that follows the signature in Gün is not his and is not used. His critical lines on Kemal (prison letters, Moscow-era statements) were not found in free sources.

## Nihal Atsız, 1947-11
*Pan-Turkist writer, editor of Kür Şad, tried in the 1944 racism-Turanism case* — conflicted, article, evidence P.

Says a lawyer dropped his 1944 defence when Falih Rıfkı called him an enemy of Atatürk, and implies he is no admirer.

> O, Atatürk'e hayransa, ben de değilsem, bu, beni yarı yolda bırakmayı mazur gösterir mi?

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'If he admires Atatürk and I do not, does that excuse leaving me halfway?' Signed 'ATSIZ', in his reply to Hasan Âli Yücel ('Sıfır'), Kür Şad no. 4-5 (1947; the issue marks the 23rd anniversary of Gökalp's death and carries the date 3 Kasım 1947), archive.org OCR ('ben de de- gilsem' normalised). The lawyer is Hâmit Şevket İnce. The sentence is conditional: it implies but does not state that Atatürk is not his hero. Kür Şad also mocks officials who strike 'Atatürk poses' on Atatürk Boulevard. The vault note's claims (blood nationalism, attacks on 'Peace at home', Nazi sympathies) are about the state, not Kemal, and were not checked here.

## Afet İnan, 1959-05-31
*Historian, Atatürk's adopted daughter, founding member of the Turkish Historical Society* — admiring, memoir, evidence P.

Sees Atatürk not only as commander and founding president but as the thinker who planned his revolutions over a lifetime.

> Savaşta kumandan, devlet kurmada siyasi bir şahsiyet, inkılap hareketlerinde ise devrimci bir fikir adamı olarak karşımıza çıkıyor.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'He appears before us as a commander in war, a political figure in founding the state, and a revolutionary man of ideas in the movements of reform.' Önsöz (p. xvi) of Atatürk Hakkında Hatıralar ve Belgeler (1st ed. Ankara 1959), read in the İş Bankası edition revised by her son Arı İnan (2007; 8th printing 2009), whose language was modernised, so the 1959 wording may differ slightly. First-hand witness of his last 13 years; the book prints his letter to her from the Savarona of 14 June 1938. The vault note's framing ('engineered academic') is interpretation, not tested here. Check: quote verified only in the 2007 edition, whose editor (Arı İnan) modernised the language; the 1959 wording is unverified.

## Ahmet Ağaoğlu, 1969
*Liberal deputy and publicist, co-founder of the Free Republican Party (1930)* — conflicted, memoir, evidence S.

Recalls telling the Gazi at his Yalova table in 1930 that a pre-arranged opposition party was too artificial; Kemal grew angry, then laughed.

> Gazi'ye karşı takınılmasını daima bir namus ve şeref borcu, bir Vatan ve Türklüğün icabı olarak kabul etmiş olduğum doğruluktan ve açıklıktan bu kerre de ayrılmadım. "Bu biraz fazla sunidir!.." dedim

Mediated by: Soyak, Atatürk'ten Hatıralar (1973), vol. 2, pp. 416-417 ← Ağaoğlu, Serbest Fırka Hatıraları, pp. 4, 10.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'I did not depart this time either from the honesty and frankness that I had always held, towards the Gazi, to be a debt of honour and a duty to the Fatherland and Turkishness. I said: "This is a bit too artificial!"' Event: Yalova, August 1930, after Fethi's and Kemal's letters were read. DATE: Soyak calls the memoir 'kısmen neşredilen'; its first edition year 1969 is tier M (not verified); Ağaoğlu died 1939, so it was written 1930-39. Through Fethi he reports Kemal wanted 'biraz hürriyet havası' but a party that would be a wing of the RPP. Soyak is a Kemal loyalist and adds his own notes. Read from archive.org OCR; quotation marks normalised and the line-break 'suni- dir' joined. Check: a 1969 Baha Matbaası edition is cited, but an undated Nebioğlu edition also exists; 1969 may not be the first edition.

## Sabiha Gökçen, 1982
*Military pilot, Atatürk's adopted daughter* — admiring, memoir, evidence S.

Recalls persuading Atatürk to let her fly in the 1937 Dersim operation; he consented and handed her his own pistol to guard her honour.

> erişilmeze erişmiştim [on meeting him in 1925; translation: 'I had reached the unreachable']

Mediated by: Altınay, in Vatan Millet Kadınlar (İletişim, 2000), pp. 268-269 ← Gökçen, Atatürk'le Bir Ömür (1996 printing), pp. 111, 117-118, 122.

⚠️ TRANSLATION (this record's): 'Should such bad luck happen, do not worry, I will not surrender myself to them alive.' Her reply to Kemal, who warned that a forced landing among the rebels would be worse than death. The memoir is 'Oktay Verel'in kaleminden' (ghost-written); bibliographies give printings of 1984 and 1994 as well, so the first date is not settled. The chapter title is 'Dersim Harekâtı ve Namusumu Koruyacak Silah!'. NUANCES vault claim: the vault says he 'deployed her'; in her account she learned of the operation late and asked him for permission, which he gave subject to Marshal Çakmak's consent. She says she does not dwell on the operation's causes or results (p. 125). Check: first edition *Atatürk'ün İzinde Bir Ömür Böyle Geçti*, written by Oktay Verel (Türk Hava Kurumu, 1982); later printings 1996. Quote as cited by Altınay 2000, p. 262; the Dersim episode is narration about herself.
