# Westminster on Atatürk, 1941–1957 — Eden, Selwyn Lloyd, Noel-Baker, Wigg, Davies and Hollis

Found through TheyWorkForYou's search of the historic Commons record (a transcription of Hansard). In wartime, Eden uses Atatürk's 'genius' to court Turkey (Churchill's 2 Aug 1944 line on Kemal's Russian policy states no view and is not counted); in the Cyprus years (1955-56) Davies, Selwyn Lloyd and Noel-Baker cite the Atatürk-Venizelos reconciliation as a model, and in 1957 Wigg uses Britain's failure to topple Kemal in 1922 as a lesson. Existing person note: [[Winston Churchill]] (earlier Commons records are in [[Parliament Meets Kemal, February–March 1920]]).

## Anthony Eden (1941-08-06)

> In that world the Turkey re-created by the genius of Ataturk will have a noble part to play and, in doing so, Turkey will decide her own course and she will choose her own collaborators.

Orders of the Day — War Situation, HC Deb 6 Aug 1941, c2039. Speech reassuring Turkey that Britain would not trade her interests away; praise is of the state Atatürk 're-created', in a diplomatic setting.

## Anthony Eden (1942-01-08)

> It was a cardinal principle of the policy of modern Turkey, as laid down by Ataturk, the great leader of modern Turkey, that there should be close understanding between Russia and Turkey. The wisdom of that policy becomes daily more apparent amid the vicissitudes of war.

War Situation, HC Deb 8 Jan 1942, c171. Used to reassure Turkey after Anglo-Soviet talks; Atatürk is invoked for the Russian policy, not assessed as a whole.

## Clement Davies (1955-12-05)

> Certainly, ever since the Turks established their great tradition under that amazing man, Ataturk, one has had great admiration for these people.

Cyprus, HC Deb 5 Dec 1955, c91. Cyprus debate (sensitive thread): the praise is a preface to an argument against Turkish interference; recorded as the speaker's framing.

## Christopher Hollis (1953-10-28)

> We all know the history of how the ancient enmity between Greece and Turkey was ended by an agreement between those two countries for exchange of populations brought about by the statesman Kemal Ataturk.

European Affairs and Trieste, HC Deb 28 Oct 1953, c2835. Sensitive thread (the 1923 population exchange): this is Hollis's framing, as a precedent for Trieste; the vault does not adopt it.

## Selwyn Lloyd (1956-07-19)

> Everyone will bear in mind the work of Kemal Ataturk and Venizelos in building up a new relationship, friendship and alliance.

Cyprus, HC Deb 19 Jul 1956, c1403. Cyprus debate (sensitive thread): speaker's framing. Mirror label is the closest of the ten (the reconciling former enemy); not forced beyond that.

## Philip Noel-Baker (1956-09-14)

> Thirty years ago, after the far more grievous troubles of which I have spoken, Venizelos and Mustapha Kemal made a historic reconciliation.

Cyprus, HC Deb 14 Sep 1956, c355. Cyprus debate (sensitive thread): speaker's framing. Noel-Baker was an eyewitness of the 1922-23 Lausanne population-exchange talks, which he mentions in the same speech (Nansen).

## George Wigg (1957-02-05)

> Today, hon. Gentlemen speak in terms of great respect of Kemal Ataturk. There was a time when we tried to knock down Kemal Ataturk.

Army Supplementary Estimate, 1956-57, HC Deb 5 Feb 1957, c332. Used as a precedent against removing Nasser by force (Suez aftermath): Wigg's point is that the Middle East choice is 'authoritarian regimes based upon the army and chaos'. Wigg, who served in Constantinople in 1923, mentions Kemal in six contributions 1946-57 (24 May 1946, 16 Mar 1953, 29 Jul 1954 on the Turks' goodwill, 24 Jan 1956 'another Kemal or ... Enver Pasha' of Nasser, 8 Nov 1956); this is the fullest statement.
