# Statesmen and Royals in Contact with Kemal, 1919–1938

Contemporary records for major statesmen and royals: their own words about Kemal, or a documented first-hand contact with him (a letter, a meeting or audience, a substantive exchange). Under the census's first-hand contact rule (5 Nov 2026) such contacts count for major figures even without a stated opinion; form telegrams and protocol do not. Most texts come from the editors' notes in Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri and from Şimşir's published British documents.

## James G. Harbord, 1919-10-16
*US Major General, head of the American Military Mission to Armenia* — admiring, report, evidence P.

Met Kemal at Sivas in Sept 1919; reported him a distinguished Dardanelles commander, 'a young man of force and keen intelligence'; wrote him about Armenians.

> The leader, Mustapha Kemal Pasha, is a former general officer in the Turkish Army, who commanded, with distinction, an Army Corps at the Dardanelles, and appears to be a young man of force and keen intelligence.

⚠️ Harbord's report to the Secretary of State, 16 Oct 1919, FRUS 1919 vol. II, doc. 828, p. 858. Exhibit D is his letter to Kemal of 9 Oct 1919 thanking him for courtesies and urging wider assurance that the movement was not hostile to Christians. Praise is measured and sits beside Armenian apprehensions he relays; the Sivas meeting was 22 Sept 1919.

## Nariman Narimanov, 1920-08-19
*Chairman of the Revolutionary Committee of Soviet Azerbaijan* — instrumental, letter, evidence P+S.

Sent Kemal a letter, read in the Grand National Assembly, pledging Muslim communists' full strength to his aims against British-led European imperialism.

> Müslüman komünistleri sizin maksadınıza çatmak yolunda var kuvvetlerini sarf edeceklerdir.

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 9 (Kaynak 2002) p. 219 ← TBMM Zabıt Ceridesi, Devre 1, vol. 3 (19 Aug 1920 sitting) p. 29.

⚠️ Translation: 'The Muslim communists will spend all their strength on the road to reaching your goal.' Azeri-Turkish wording; OCR 'makstdunuza çatmak!' normalised to 'maksadınıza çatmak'. Carried by Halil Pasha; Kemal read it out and the Assembly voted a reply. Alliance language serving the Soviet line.

## Ahmed Sharif as-Senussi, 1920-11-25
*Head of the Senussi order, guest of the Grand National Assembly* — admiring, meeting, evidence J+S.

Came to Ankara as the Assembly's guest; at the banquet Kemal thanked him for urging the nationalists on after 16 March 1920 and serving since.

> None

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 10 (Kaynak 2004) pp. 117–119 ← Hâkimiyeti Milliye 27 Nov 1920; vol. 11 p. 202 ← Hâkimiyeti Milliye 14 June 1921.

⚠️ His support is attested by Kemal's banquet speech (25 Nov 1920) and by his own Eid (Ramazan Bayramı) greeting telegram of June 1921 praying for the struggle's success, summarised in Kemal's reply; his own words are not printed. Kemal recalled seeing the Senussis' work in Libya himself; a sword he gave hung in Kemal's study (Vakit, 10 Jan 1922, ABE vol. 12).

## Horace Rumbold, 1921-01-20
*British High Commissioner at Constantinople (later Ambassador in Berlin)* — conflicted, telegram, evidence P.

Told Curzon Kemal was no longer a brigand chief: Angora governed Anatolia with average efficiency and Muslim support, holding native Christians in a stranglehold.

> It is useless to regard Mustapha Kemal any longer as a brigand chief.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. III No. 15, pp. 59–60 ← Rumbold to Curzon, tel. No. 43, Constantinople, 20 Jan 1921.

⚠️ A realist reassessment rather than praise: the same telegram calls the population 'sheep-like' and notes Angora's 'strangle hold on native Christians'.

## David Lloyd George, 1921-01-25
*British Prime Minister, at the Allied conference in Paris* — hostile, speech, evidence P.

Told the Allies that Kemal could only reject peace from vain ambition to revive the old Empire and become the first of new Sultans.

> He (Mr. Lloyd George) could not imagine that Mustapha Kemal was hostile to a peaceable solution unless indeed he was filled with the vain ambition of resurrecting the old Turkish Empire and constituting himself the first of a new line of Sultans.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. III (TTK 1973) No. 20, p. 73 ← British Secretary's Notes of an Allied Conference, Quai d'Orsay, 25 Jan 1921, 4 p.m. (DBFP 1st ser. vol. XV pp. 28–39 No. 4).

⚠️ Reported speech in British minutes; conditional in form. Same meeting: he agreed with Briand that the Allies must not seem to sue Kemal for peace. Differs from the existing Lloyd George records (Dec 1920, Aug 1922, 1938).

## Horace Rumbold, 1921-02-14
*British High Commissioner at Constantinople* — conflicted, report, evidence P.

Forwarding missionary reports of the Hadjin massacre, judged that Kemal did not himself encourage massacres of Christians but was powerless to stop extremists.

> At the same time I am of opinion that Mustapha Kemal Pasha does not himself encourge or countenance the massacre of Christians, but rather that he is powerless, even if desirous of doing so, to protect from massacre the Christian population of the areas in which military operations are in progress

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. III No. 58, p. 134 ← Rumbold to Curzon, despatch No. 173, Constantinople, 14 Feb 1921.

⚠️ Sensitive thread (Hadjin, 1920): Rumbold's judgement of personal responsibility, recorded as his view only; the massacre account itself comes from missionary sources he forwarded. 'encourge' is as printed. Rumbold wrote that he had 'no reason to doubt' the missionary memorandum.

## Aristide Briand, 1921-02-22
*French Prime Minister and Foreign Minister, at the London Conference* — conflicted, speech, evidence P.

Refusing to sit with Feisal, called Mustafa Kemal an open enemy who fought France in the recognised manner, an honourable enemy he would meet.

> Mustapha Kemal was an open enemy, who carried on warfare against the French in the duly recognised manner.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. III (TTK 1973) No. 68, p. 176 ← British Secretary's Notes of an Allied Conference, 10 Downing Street, 22 Feb 1921, 11 a.m. (DBFP 1st ser. vol. XV pp. 160–165 No. 18).

⚠️ Reported speech in British minutes, not Briand's own text. Said in reply to Lloyd George's remark that Feisal had not shed as much French blood as Kemal; the next sentences say Briand was 'quite ready to meet and discuss matters with honourable enemies'. Scan: archive.org item ingiliz-belgelerinde-ataturk, file #3 (djvu text).

## Raymond Poincaré, 1921-12-23
*French senator and former President (Prime Minister from Jan 1922)* — witness, letter, evidence S.

Received a letter from Mustafa Kemal thanking him for his large share in defending Turkey's cause, whose first fruit was the Ankara Agreement.

> None

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 12 (Kaynak 2005) p. 158 ← Şimşir, Atatürk ile Yazışmalar I (1981) pp. 162–163 ← Bibliothèque nationale de France (French original).

⚠️ Contact record: the text is Kemal's letter to Poincaré, so the claim that Poincaré championed the Turkish cause is Kemal's characterisation; no reply from Poincaré was found. Same day Kemal wrote to Marshal Lyautey via Berthe Georges-Gaulis.

## Mikhail Frunze, 1921-12-25
*Ukrainian SSR envoy extraordinary to Ankara, Red Army commander (later Soviet war commissar)* — instrumental, meeting, evidence S.

In talks with Kemal, cast Turkey's struggle as national-democratic, headed toward state socialism, and told him a communist revolution would cost the propertyless Pasha nothing.

> Hâkimiyetten söz ederken, sizi —Paşa'yı— göz önüne alıyorum ve sizin hiçbir mal ve mülkünüzün olmadığını ve kendi hizmetiniz ve emeğinizle geçindiğinizi biliyorum. Buradan, komünist ihtilal olsa bile sizin hiçbir şey kaybetmeyeceğiniz sonucu çıkmaktadır.

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 12 pp. 177–179 ← Soviet record of the Abilov–Frunze talk with Mustafa Kemal, 25 Dec 1921 (Azerbaijan State Archive of Recent History, f. 28, op. 1, d. 68, ll. 10–16) ← Y. Aslan, Mustafa Kemal–M. Frunze Görüşmeleri (2002) pp. 35–46.

⚠️ Turkish translation of a Russian record. Translation: 'When I speak of power I have you, the Pasha, in mind, and I know you have no property and live by your own service and labour. It follows that even if there were a communist revolution you would lose nothing.' A second talk followed on 4 Jan 1922.

## Mikhail Frunze, 1922-01-04
*Ukrainian SSR envoy extraordinary to Ankara, Red Army commander* — instrumental, report, evidence S.

Before the farewell talk, agreed the line: back Kemal, keep him under Soviet influence, steer his personal dictatorship toward a party one, publicise him.

> Mustafa Kemal'i desteklemek ve onun daima bizim etkimiz altında kalmasına çalışmak

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 12 (Kaynak 2005) pp. 203–206 ← Abilov's Russian record of the second Abilov–Frunze talk with Mustafa Kemal, 4 Jan 1922 (Azerbaijan State Archive of Recent History, f. 28, op. 1, d. 68, ll. 1–7) ← Y. Aslan (2002) pp. 46–56.

⚠️ Translation: 'to support Mustafa Kemal and work to keep him always under our influence'. The line was agreed jointly by Abilov, Frunze and Mikhailov and recorded by Abilov, so Frunze is one of three voices. In the talk Kemal described the Assembly's groups and his army controls. Distinct from frunze-1921-12-25 (the first talk).

## Hubert Lyautey, 1922-09
*Marshal of France, Resident-General in Morocco* — admiring, letter, evidence S.

Corresponded with Kemal (his letter of 20 March 1922 answered Kemal's of December 1921) and congratulated him on the September 1922 victories.

> None

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 12 pp. 157, 375 and vol. 13 (Kaynak 2004) p. 325 ← Şimşir, Atatürk ile Yazışmalar I (1981) pp. 205, 291 ← Turkish Foreign Ministry archive; Kemal's Dec 1921 letter ← F. Pekin, Belleten 20 (1956) pp. 654–655.

⚠️ Lyautey's own wording is not printed in the volumes read; the congratulations are known from Kemal's reply of 25 Sept 1922 (via Ferid Bey in Paris), which calls him 'the dearest friend of the Islamic and Turkish world'. Polarity rests on the act of congratulating on the victories.

## Hubert Lyautey, 1923-01
*Marshal of France, Resident-General in Morocco* — conflicted, quote, evidence S.

Told Berthe Georges-Gaulis he had admired the Kemalists' struggle and told them so, but no longer understood men breaking with five centuries' history.

> Mücadelelerinde onlara hayran oldum ve bunu kendilerine bildirdim. Fakat bugün onları anlıyamıyorum

Mediated by: F. Pekin, 'Atatürk ve Lyautey', Belleten XX/80 (Oct 1956) p. 642 ← B. Georges-Gaulis, La question turque (1931).

⚠️ Turkish translation of remarks Gaulis recorded years later. Translation: 'I admired them in their struggle and told them so. But today I do not understand them.' The same conversation praises their sincerity, energy and military worth but says such men 'grow weak once they succeed'. The PDF text layer drops Turkish letters (printed as 'onlar~~anhyam~yorum'); quote restored from the page.

## Ronald Lindsay, 1926-02-08
*British Ambassador to Turkey (later Ambassador in Washington)* — hostile, report, evidence P.

Complained of a a campaign against foreigners against foreign firms and schools, a 'Chinese wall' policy pursued with Kemal's characteristic remorseless pertinacity and popular support.

> This policy is being pursued with the remorseless pertinacity characteristic of Mustafa Kemal and it receives the cordial support of the whole population.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VI No. 4 ← Lindsay to Austen Chamberlain, despatch No. 59, Constantinople, 8 Feb 1926.

⚠️ Quote normalised from poor OCR ('purnued', 'remorseles pertinaciy', 'charactertic'). In despatch No. 258 of 31 Mar 1925 (Şimşir V) he wrote that the Sheikh Said revolt was probably being used to crush opposition to 'the radical autocracy of Mustafa Kemal'.

## Faisal I of Iraq, 1927
*King of Iraq* — admiring, report, evidence S.

Told Turkey's ambassador in London he followed its reforms, could only revere the Gazi's genius, and that Turkey would be every Asian nation's model.

> Bütün Asyaî milletlere nümune-i imtisal olacağınız şüphesizdir.

Mediated by: Saygı, Yalova Sosyal Bilimler Dergisi 7/15 (2017) p. 215 ← Balkaya 2015 p. 572 (Turkish diplomatic report of the London conversation).

⚠️ Translation: 'There is no doubt that you will be a model to be followed by all Asian nations.' Same passage: 'it is not possible ... not to revere [the Gazi's] genius' and he had not expected such results at the start of the national movement. Words reach us through a Turkish envoy's report, at two removes.

## Joseph C. Grew, 1927-10
*US Ambassador to Turkey (1927–1932)* — admiring, memoir, evidence P.

After presenting credentials, told the Foreign Minister the Gazi had made a profound impression, struck by the strength and determination of his face.

> He had the face of a man who could overcome all difficulties in reaching his goal.

⚠️ Turbulent Era: A Diplomatic Record of Forty Years, 1904–1945 (Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1952), vol. II, chapter 'Arrival in Turkey' (from p. 729; exact page not seen); read only as archive.org search-inside snippets of a lending copy, which also give 'I said to the Minister that the President had made a profound impression on me'. The vault card's all-night dinners and 'terrifyingly intelligent' line are not found. Page unknown (chapter 'Arrival in Turkey').

## George Clerk, 1928-02-20
*British Ambassador to Turkey* — witness, meeting, evidence P.

At a Foreign Minister's ball the Ghazi addressed him for half an hour on honourable enemies turned friends; Clerk found it platitudinous but sincerely friendly.

> None

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VI No. 7 ← Clerk to Austen Chamberlain, Angora, 21 Feb 1928 (F.O. 424/268).

⚠️ OCR is poor, so no verbatim quote. Clerk notes the speech was 'admirably phrased' but 'somewhat platitudinous generalities', and that Kemal told the French attaché to have his 'lying papers' report he was not dying. Clerk's funeral-era view is already in data.

## Faisal I of Iraq, 1931-07-06
*King of Iraq, on a state visit to Ankara* — admiring, meeting, evidence J+S.

On his Ankara state visit, when Kemal mentioned ceded Mosul, answered that Turkey had won all Iraq, its streets full of the Gazi's admirers.

> Fakat buna karşılık bütün Irak'ı kazandınız. Bugün Bağdat caddelerini hep Gazi'nin hayranları ve sevenleri dolduruyorlar!

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 25 (Kaynak 2009) p. 188 ← Vakit 8 July 1931 p. 1, Cumhuriyet 9 July 1931; British despatch Clerk to Henderson, July 1931 (Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VII).

⚠️ Translation: 'But in return you have won all of Iraq. Today Baghdad's streets are filled with admirers and lovers of the Gazi!' Press report of a ball at Ankara Palas conversation; flattery between hosts. On leaving (14–15 July) he told reporters Turkey's progress, owed to Atatürk's genius, was an example (Saygı 2017 pp. 218–219).

## Maxim Litvinov, 1931-10-29
*Soviet People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs, guest at the Republic Day in Ankara* — witness, meeting, evidence S.

Reported to Moscow long talks with Kemal, who pressed him on Manchuria, feared Japan would turn on the USSR, and explained Turkey's Balkan policy.

> Kemal Paşa'yla, daha da fazla İsmet Paşa'yla uzun görüşmeler yaptım.

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 25 pp. 267–268 fn. ← M. Perinçek, Atatürk'ün Sovyetler'le Görüşmeleri (2007) pp. 411–412 ← Litvinov's telegram to the NKID, 29 Oct 1931 (Dokumenty vneshnei politiki SSSR vol. 14 p. 622 ff.).

⚠️ Turkish translation of a Russian telegram. Translation: 'I had long talks with Kemal Pasha, and even more with İsmet Pasha.' He judged that Turkey valued Soviet friendship. Vakit (31 Oct 1931) adds that Kemal spoke with him in French until morning at the ball.

## Douglas MacArthur, 1932-09-27
*US Army Chief of Staff, on a European tour* — witness, meeting, evidence P+S+J.

Received by the Gazi at Dolmabahçe on 27 Sept 1932; recalled talking with him about rebuilding Turkey's armed forces.

> In Turkey, I talked with that indomitable leader, Mustapha Kemal Ataturk

⚠️ Quote from MacArthur, Reminiscences (1964) p. 98 (archive.org reminiscences00maca, search snippet), written 32 years later; the adjective is his later view. Date and timing (17:00 to 18:20, during the Language Congress) from Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 26 p. 37 fn., which shows the long 'Atatürk–MacArthur conversation' text first printed in 1951 is a fabrication. Army and Navy Journal 10 Sept and 1 Oct 1932 report the visit.

## Nicolae Titulescu, 1933-10
*Romanian Foreign Minister* — witness, meeting, evidence P.

Received by the President during a three-day visit to Angora; two hours reviewing European politics, finding Turkish and Romanian views in complete agreement.

> None

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VIII (TTK 2006) No. 3, annual report on Turkey for 1933, para. 94 ← Morgan to Sir John Simon, Angora, 20 Jan 1934 (F.O. 371/17959/E.596).

⚠️ Contact record (first-hand rule): the agreement is the British chargé's summary, not Titulescu's words. Treaty of friendship signed 17 Oct 1933. The vault card's Titulescu quotes (Atatürk 'greatest statesman in Europe', a 1938 eulogy) were not found. PDF file #8, p. 277.

## Charles H. Sherrill, 1933-10-26
*US ambassador to Turkey 1932–33, retired general; author of A Year's Embassy to Mustafa Kemal (1934)* — admiring, quote, evidence P+S.

After a year of talks with Kemal, called Mussolini and Kemal Pasha the world's two great leaders; his book likened Kemal to Moses.

> two great leaders of the world today

Mediated by: Arthur Brisbane, 'Today' column, St. Louis Post-Dispatch 26 Oct 1933, Part Three (reporting Sherrill); Özdemir 2014 (ATAM), pp. 75–79 <- Turkish translation of Sherrill's book (Gazi Mustafa Kemal, tr. Alp N., Tercüman 1001 Temel Eser), pp. 10–11, 105–106.

⚠️ The English original of the book (Scribner 1934) is search-only on HathiTrust (uc1.$b57581) and was not read. The quote is Brisbane's report of his remark on returning, not the book. The Moses, heart and Ankara-move passages are read only in Özdemir's Turkish quotation, e.g. p. 77: 'Mustafa Kemal aynı zamanda büyük bir kalp adamı olduğunu da ispat ediyordu' [translation: Mustafa Kemal was proving at the same time that he was a man of great heart]. First-hand: Kemal drew sketch maps for him at Ankara and Yalova (Özdemir pp. 78–79). A Washington Post editorial of 28 Oct 1933 also reports his view that Kemal 'has a splendid army and uses it as an instrument for peace' (the paper's words).

## Edward VIII, 1936-09-04
*King of the United Kingdom, private visit on the yacht Nahlin* — witness, meeting, evidence P+J.

Received by Atatürk at Istanbul, exchanged visits, drove with him through the city, invited him to London, and telegraphed thanks from Sofia.

> Türkiye'de ilk ikametimden pek zevkli bir hatıra muhafaza edeceğim.

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 28 (Kaynak 2010) pp. 281–283 ← Edward's telegram, Sofia 7 Sept 1936 (Şimşir, Atatürk ve Yabancı Devlet Başkanları vol. 2 pp. 342–343) and Cumhuriyet, Akşam, Kurun 7 Sept 1936.

⚠️ Translation of the Turkish rendering of his French telegram: 'I shall keep a most pleasant memory of my first stay in Turkey.' At Sirkeci station (6 Sept) he told Atatürk in French he hoped he would one day honour London. Corroborated by the British annual report for 1936 (para. 61) and NZ wire reports of 5–7 Sept 1936; his memoir A King's Story (1951) recalls Atatürk greeting him ashore and leaving his bullet-proof car.

## Franklin D. Roosevelt, 1937-04-06
*President of the United States* — admiring, letter, evidence S.

After watching Julian Bryan's film of Turkey at the White House, wrote Atatürk of his enthusiasm at astonishing changes made so quickly; hoped to meet.

> Nispeten kısa bir zamanda meydan getirdiğiniz pek çok hayret verici hususları görünce, hissettiğim şevk ve heyecanı size arz etmek istedim.

Mediated by: Atatürk'ün Bütün Eserleri vol. 29 (Kaynak 2011) p. 228 fn. 2 ← Şimşir, Atatürk ve Yabancı Devlet Başkanları vol. 1 (1993) pp. 259–260, 718–719 (English original, Turkish Foreign Ministry archive).

⚠️ Turkish translation of an English letter; 'meydan' is as printed (read 'meydana'). Translation: 'Seeing the many astonishing things you have brought about in a relatively short time, I wanted to tell you of the enthusiasm I felt.' He also enjoyed the film of Atatürk playing with his small daughter and the stamps Atatürk sent. Atatürk replied 25 May 1937. Check: Şimşir's English text of the reply is dated 6 June 1937.

## Jamil Mardam Bey, 1938-03
*Prime Minister of Syria* — admiring, quote, evidence S.

Told a Cumhuriyet correspondent that Atatürk was not only the Turks' hero but father of the East, whose path all Eastern nations follow.

> إنّ البطل أتاتورك ليس للترك فحسب بل هو أبو الشرق أيضاً. [translation: The hero Atatürk belongs not only to the Turks; he is the father of the East as well.]

Mediated by: al-Zawba'a no. 48 (unsigned SSNP article, in Saadeh's Complete Works vol. 6) ← an-Nahar ← Cumhuriyet interview.

⚠️ Quoted by a hostile source (Saadeh's party organ) to accuse Mardam of treason over Alexandretta; Turkish original not seen. Translation is the census's. Check: the date is an-Nahar's translation date; the interview was on or before 12 Mar 1938.

## Sir Percy Loraine, 1938-06-02
*British Ambassador to Turkey (1933–1939)* — admiring, letter, evidence P.

Pressing London for an honorary degree for Atatürk, argued he was technically no dictator, his position not that of Hitler or Mussolini.

> But is he a "dictator"? I think myself that technically he is not.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VIII (TTK 2006) No. 211, p. 511 ← Loraine to Sir Lancelot Oliphant, private letter, British Embassy Istanbul (FO 371/21930).

⚠️ Date read as '2nd June 1938' by fresh OCR of the scan (archive.org djvu text reads '2'); PDF file #8, p. 766. Attached a summary: Atatürk 'has deserved well of the whole learned world by raising the cultural and social level of a whole nation'. Check: dated 2nd June 1938 on p. 511 (F.O. 371/21930/E.3437); summary on p. 512.

## Sir Percy Loraine, 1938-11-25
*British Ambassador to Turkey (1933–1939)* — admiring, report, evidence P.

Obituary despatch: Atatürk treated him as a personal friend; a duality of character; a very great leader in war and peace, but no true dictator.

> I am persuaded that this is a misnomer. A very great leader, yes, both in war and in peace: but a true dictator, no.

Mediated by: Şimşir, İngiliz Belgelerinde Atatürk vol. VIII (TTK 2006) No. 273, pp. 559–562 (quote p. 561) ← Loraine to Halifax, despatch No. 608, Angora, 25 Nov 1938 (FO 424/282).

⚠️ Quote from fresh OCR of the scan (PDF file #8, pp. 814–817); archive.org djvu text is garbled here. 'This' refers to the label dictator. Confirms the vault's friendship claim in Loraine's words; poker and weeping at the funeral are not in this text.
