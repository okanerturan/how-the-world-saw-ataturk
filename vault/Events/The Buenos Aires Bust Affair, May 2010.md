# The Buenos Aires Bust Affair, May 2010

**A modern reception flashpoint, and a contested one.** In May 2010, as Prime Minister Recep Tayyip
Erdoğan toured South America with seven ministers and some 300 businessmen, Turkey's embassy in
Buenos Aires was pressing for the unveiling of a bust of Atatürk in a park (Figueroa Alcorta/Sarmiento avenues;
the Armenian Mirror-Spectator alone places it near Jorge Newbery airport, so treat that detail as partisan-sourced). The Armenian community of Argentina — led by Archbishop
Kissag Mouradian, with a paid statement in *Clarín*, *La Nación*, *Página/12* and the *Buenos Aires
Herald* — protested — in a paid advertisement of 27 May, the wording the Mirror-Spectator reports —
that Atatürk "continued the extermination plan initiated on April 24, 1915, by the Ottoman Government"
and inaugurated its denial (the quoted words are the community's advertisement, not a personal statement by the archbishop), and on 28 May the city's public-space minister, Diego Santilli, withdrew the permission.
On 30–31 May Erdoğan cancelled the Argentine leg. Turkey's Foreign Ministry said the trip was
cancelled because written permission "was reversed as a result of initiatives by the Armenian
lobby."

The facts are disputed even by the participants. The Argentine foreign ministry (under the Kirchner
government) blamed the Buenos Aires city government of Mauricio Macri; city hall said no ceremony
had ever been authorized, that the embassy had asked to *replace an existing bust*, and that
**there had never been a bust of Atatürk there** — the missing monument had been of an Egyptian
human-rights figure, which in turn irritated Argentina's Arab community. The Turkish ambassador,
Hayri Hayret Yalav, said the paperwork had run since November 2009 without trouble. Armenian-press
accounts (Armenian Weekly, Mirror-Spectator) are partisan; Reuters, the Buenos Aires Herald and the
Turkish ministry's own statement are the baseline.

Read it as the mirror of everything the vault holds on the genocide thread: a statesman honored
by one diaspora's calendar and indicted by another's, on the soil of a third country that had
itself legislated on the matter (Argentina recognized the genocide by law in 2007). Neutral note:
what is verified is the dispute and its sequence; the historical charge against Atatürk is argued
in [[Armenian Genocide]] and is not adjudicated here. For the Turkish counter-image of such
monuments see [[Anıtkabir]].
