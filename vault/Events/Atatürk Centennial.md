# Atatürk Centennial

UNESCO's 20th General Conference (1978) resolved to mark 1981, the hundredth anniversary of
Atatürk's birth, as a commemorative year — the resolution praising "an exceptional reformer" and
"the leader of the first struggle given against colonialism and imperialism" (the English text as
reproduced at ataturktoday.com; the Turkish rendering on the same page says "one of the first leaders" of
such struggles, and the official UNESCO text was unreachable on 2026-10-29, so the exact wording is
unsettled). The resolution is real (27 Nov 1978); the standard Turkish framing ("adopted unanimously by all 152
members, a first in UNESCO history") is the embellished layer to caveat. Archival locus, pinned
Aug 2026: *Records of the General Conference, 20th session, Paris 1978*, Vol. I: Resolutions,
**p. 69** — UNESDOC ark pf0000114032 (bot-gated; the exact resolution number needs one browser
click).

The domestic wrinkle worth the project's attention: the centennial year arrived ten months after
the **12 September 1980 military coup**, so the worldwide celebration of Atatürk unfolded inside
Turkey under a junta that wrapped itself in his authority — the state-managed gaze at maximum
volume, exactly as Kemalism was being rewritten from above. Pair with [[10 Kasım]] and the
memory-studies shelf in [[Historiography of Atatürk]].
