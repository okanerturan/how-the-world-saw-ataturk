# America's Immigrant Press Reads Kemal, 1922–1938

Chronicling America holds the foreign-language press of the United States. Searching it for Kemal (`fa=language:<lang>`, 1919–38)
gives, by hit count: German 262 pages, Polish 201, Czech 136, Lithuanian 91, Slovenian 48, Hungarian 16, Italian 17, Slovak 9, Finnish 20,
Yiddish ~480 (Hebrew-script query), Spanish (see [[Spanish-Language Newspapers Across the Americas Read Kemal, 1922–1935]]).
**Greek, Armenian, Arabic and Hebrew return zero**: the Greek-American, Armenian-American (*Hairenik*, *Asbarez*)
and Syrian-Lebanese (*al-Hoda*, *Meraat-ul-Gharb*) papers are not on loc.gov for these years. Most hits are agency copy from the
1922 Smyrna and Chanak weeks; this card keeps only **items with a stated view**.

What the communities saw, in short:

- **Hungarians** saw a kinsman. *Amerikai Magyar Hirlap* (Youngstown, 23 Nov 1922) reprinted a Budapest *Pesti Hírlap* greeting, "Üdvözlet Angorába", comparing the Angora Assembly to Kossuth's 1849 Debrecen parliament; by 1938 the same paper could joke about "the Turkish dictator".
- **Poles** split by paper, not by creed. The Cleveland women's weekly *Jedność Polek* (1927–28) made Kemal the Turkish Piłsudski, a "regenerator"; the Connecticut Catholic weekly *Przewodnik Katolicki* (Dec 1922) read him through oil politics and Polish interest, and blamed the American press's change of tune on Standard Oil.
- **Czechs** in Cleveland first printed a hostile-but-awed Plain Dealer editorial ("brigand", yet a hero to Muslims and Hindus) at Smyrna time, then a respectful essay on Lausanne as the start of a new Turkish era.
- **Italians** used him. The Baltimore weekly praised him as an "incorruptible patriot" (1922) and credited him with the Italo-Turkish pact (1929); Connecticut papers of 1934–35 used Kemal as a stick or a comparison in arguments about Mussolini's own dictatorship.
- **Slovaks** (Youngstown, 1930) and **Slovenes** (Chicago, 1922) offer a mild praise and a Balkan wariness.
- **Yiddish**: the New York *Morgen Zhurnal* (25 Jul 1934) wrote of "the sufferings of our brothers under the Turkish dictatorship" after the Thracian pogroms. This is the hardest-edged Jewish voice found; the Yiddish Daily Tageblatt's English wire pages (1921–26) carry the Daily Mail's "Sephardic Jew from Salonica" rumour and Cilicia reports but no view of the paper's own.

- **Il Risorgimento Italiano nel Maryland**, 1922-10-21, p. 1 (admiring). *Profile: Kemal is no longer a rebel but 'the recognised dictator'; unlike Enver and Talaat he is an incorruptible patriot saving his country.* Original (it, OCR-verbatim): “un patriota che lavora, indomito ed incorrotto, per salvare quanto è salvabile del suo povero paese” — English (vault translation): “a patriot who works, indomitable and incorruptible, to save whatever can be saved of his poor country”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn89060332/1922-10-21/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned front-page item (the same page calls him 'il dittatore riconosciuto'); OCR has minor artefacts._
- **Il Risorgimento Italiano nel Maryland**, 1929-01-12, p. 4 (instrumental). *Welcoming the Italo-Turkish pact: Turkey's rebirth and return to European history owed above all to Kemal's energy, intuition and will.* Original (it, OCR-verbatim): “rinascita e vitalità dovute sopra tutto all’energia, all’intuito, alla volontà di Kemal Pascià” — English (vault translation): “rebirth and vitality owed above all to the energy, the intuition, the will of Kemal Pasha”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn89060332/1929-01-12/ed-1/?sp=4). _Caveat: Praise framed to celebrate Italy's pact policy (Fascist-era Italian diplomacy), hence instrumental; unsigned._
- **La Verità (Waterbury)**, 1935-05-03, p. 2 (admiring). *Praises Lady Astor's speech hailing 'dictator' Kemal for freeing Turkish women from the harem; credits him with restoring Turkey's political unity.* Original (it, OCR-verbatim): “ha riconosciuto i meriti indiscutibili del capo supremo della nuova Turchia” — English (vault translation): “has recognised the indisputable merits of the supreme head of the new Turkey”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn95063190/1935-05-03/ed-1/?sp=2). _Caveat: Unsigned column; the paper's loyalty to Italian Fascism is not shown in this passage. The quoted clause is the paper's comment on Astor's speech._
- **La Verità (Waterbury)**, 1935-08-16, p. 3 (instrumental). *Defending Italy against dictatorship charges: Russia, Germany and Kemal's Turkey, which regulates dress and writing, are worse; Italy has no race struggle.* Original (it, OCR-verbatim): “se nella Turchia di Kemal vi e’ persino la legge che regola il modo di vestire e di scrivere” — English (vault translation): “if in Kemal's Turkey there is even a law regulating the manner of dressing and of writing”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn95063190/1935-08-16/ed-1/?sp=3). _Caveat: Fascist-apologist comparison, not an assessment of Kemal; Kemal is cited as a harsher dictatorship than Italy._
- **La Sentinella (Bridgeport)**, 1934-09-13, p. 4 (instrumental). *On a Stamford 'strong mayor' charter: hopes for results like those of Mussolini, Stalin, Kemal, Piłsudski and other 'reformist dictators'.* Original (it, OCR-verbatim): “risultati come quelli di Mussolini, Stalin, Kemal, Piluski e di altri dittatori riformisti” — English (vault translation): “results like those of Mussolini, Stalin, Kemal, Piłsudski and other reformist dictators”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84020351/1934-09-13/ed-1/?sp=4). _Caveat: Local-politics argument; Kemal used as one example of a reformist dictator; OCR 'Piluski', 'Tesperimento' in the sentence's opening._
- **Amerikai Magyar Hirlap**, 1922-11-23, p. 4 (admiring). *Greeting to Angora reprinted from Budapest's Pesti Hírlap: Kemal's sword proved a truth; the Angora Assembly likened to Kossuth's 1849 Debrecen parliament.* Original (hu, OCR-verbatim): “Kemal villogó kardja alaposan bizonyította be a világnak azt az igazságot” — English (vault translation): “Kemal's flashing sword thoroughly proved to the world the truth”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn88078388/1922-11-23/ed-1/?sp=4). _Caveat: Unsigned; OCR damaged in places. Hungarian-Turkish kinship framing and the Kossuth analogy are the paper's own._
- **Amerikai Magyar Hirlap**, 1938-04-28, p. 7 (conflicted). *Wry line after Kemal's flu recovery: the Turkish dictator pleases us because his name reaches the papers only when he is ill.* Original (hu, OCR-verbatim): “A török diktátor tetszik nekünk” — English (vault translation): “The Turkish dictator pleases us”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn88078388/1938-04-28/ed-1/?sp=7). _Caveat: One-line joke in a column; the implied contrast is with noisier dictators (Hitler is the topic of the preceding paragraph)._
- **Svět (Cleveland)**, 1923-09-07, p. 2 (admiring). *Essay 'Obrozování Turecka': Lausanne, from which Turkey emerges victor, opens a new era; Kemal's victories drive the Angora national renaissance.* Original (cs, OCR-verbatim): “znamená počátek nového období tureckých dějin” — English (vault translation): “means the beginning of a new period of Turkish history”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045068/1923-09-07/ed-1/?sp=2). _Caveat: Essay is headed with an author's initials in the OCR ('Dr. F. Š.'?), not resolved; page header and column order damaged by OCR._
- **Svět (Cleveland)**, 1922-09-16, p. 2 (conflicted). *Translating a Plain Dealer editorial: brigand-like Kemal holds Europe's fate, yet is a dazzling hero to Muslims and Hindus.* Original (cs, OCR-verbatim): “zbůjnický Turek, drží ve svých rukou osud Evropy” — English (vault translation): “brigand-like Turk, holds the fate of Europe in his hands”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045068/1922-09-16/ed-1/?sp=2). _Caveat: Mediated: the editorial is the Plain Dealer's, printed in translation by Svět as its own take on the day; OCR interleaves columns, so the quoted clause is joined from two lines._
- **Jedność Polek (Cleveland)**, 1927-12-20, p. 4 (admiring). *Defends Kemal's 36-hour party speech against charges of self-glorification: without him Turkey would be a protectorate; he laid a lasting new foundation.* Original (pl, OCR-verbatim): “Burząc stary porządek rzeczy, dał trwałe, niewzruszone podwaliny pod nowy.” — English (vault translation): “By tearing down the old order of things he gave lasting, unshakable foundations to the new.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83035481/1927-12-20/ed-1/?sp=4). _Caveat: Unsigned column; compares with Kerensky's failure in Russia. OCR damaged in the passage before the quote._
- **Jedność Polek (Cleveland)**, 1928-10-09, p. 1 (admiring). *Turkey Europeanises and modernises, undoubtedly Kemal's merit: Swiss law, Latin alphabet, spread of literacy.* Original (pl, OCR-verbatim): “Zasługa to niewątpliwie Mustafa Kemal Paszy.” — English (vault translation): “The credit for this undoubtedly belongs to Mustafa Kemal Pasha.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83035481/1928-10-09/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned news-and-comment column._
- **Jedność Polek (Cleveland)**, 1928-12-18, p. 4 (admiring). *Reports a rumour of crowning Kemal king, which the Turkish press rejects; says Poles grasp his work through Piłsudski's, 'our Polish Washington'.* Original (pl, OCR-verbatim): “Odrodzicielem kraju i narodu w Turcji jest Kemal Pasza.” — English (vault translation): “The regenerator of the country and the nation in Turkey is Kemal Pasha.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83035481/1928-12-18/ed-1/?sp=4). _Caveat: Unsigned column; the Piłsudski analogy is the paper's, anchored to a remark it attributes to a US envoy in Warsaw._
- **Przewodnik Katolicki (New Britain)**, 1922-12-15, p. 9 (instrumental). *Lausanne editorial: US press dropped its 'cruel Turks' outcry on Standard Oil's orders; Kemal sang the trust a sweet oil tune; Poland should favour Turkey.* Original (pl, OCR-verbatim): “Kemal basza i rząd turecki w Angorze zaśpiewał trustowi amerykańskiemu bardzo miłą piosenkę” — English (vault translation): “Kemal Pasha and the Turkish government in Angora sang the American trust a very pleasant song”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn88043117/1922-12-15/ed-1/?sp=9). _Caveat: Unsigned editorial. It also asserts that the Greeks, not the Turks, burned Smyrna; that attribution is contested and is recorded only as the paper's view._
- **Youngstownské Slovenské Noviny**, 1930-01-31, p. 1 (admiring). *Item on Turkey banning child labour in mines: new Turkey has an excellent president in Kemal Pasha, unlike the US South.* Original (sk, OCR-verbatim): “Nové Turecko má výborného prezidenta v osobe Kemal pašu” — English (vault translation): “New Turkey has an excellent president in the person of Kemal Pasha”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn88078386/1930-01-31/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned filler item; the contrast with the US South is the paper's comment._
- **Glas svobode (Chicago)**, 1922-12-01, p. 5 (conflicted). *After Mudanya the sultan is gone and the caliphate lies open to Kemal; he has proved his energy and ability, but rivals loom.* Original (sl, OCR-verbatim): “Da Kemalu ne manjka energije in sposobnosti, je zadostno pokazal.” — English (vault translation): “That Kemal does not lack energy and ability, he has shown sufficiently.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045203/1922-12-01/ed-1/?sp=5). _Caveat: Unsigned; also on p. 5 a 'Balkanska zveza' feature wary that Kemal will want to protect Muslims everywhere; the paper's stance is partly inferred from that._
- **Der Morgen Zhurnal (Jewish Morning Journal)**, 1934-07-25, p. 7 (hostile). *Editorial on 1934 Thracian pogroms: Turkey made Jews second-class citizens; Jewish bodies must investigate 'sufferings of our brothers' under Kemal Pasha's dictatorship.* Original (yi, OCR-verbatim): “ליידען פון אונזערע ברידער אונטער דער טערקישער דיקטאטור” — English (vault translation): “sufferings of our brothers under the Turkish dictatorship”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045838/1934-07-25/ed-1/?sp=7). _Caveat: Yiddish OCR is poor ('דו' for 'די'); the quote is exactly as OCR renders it. Page layout suggests the editorial page but no byline appears._

**Dug, nothing usable:** *Washington Journal* (German, DC) and *Der Nordstern* (German, St Cloud) — German-language news and
a Jan 1929 reprinted feature on Turkish reforms ("dieser diktierte Umschwung"), no editorial voice; *Dziennik Chicagoski*,
*Draugas*, *Lietuva* (Lithuanian), *Auttaja* (Finnish): daily Smyrna-week wire copy not read to the bottom; *Tabor Independent* (Czech, S. Dakota) not read.
