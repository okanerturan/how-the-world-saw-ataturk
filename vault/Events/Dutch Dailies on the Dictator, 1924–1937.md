# Dutch Dailies on the Dictator, 1924–1937

Between the caliphate and the funeral ([[Dutch Press, November 1938]]), the Dutch word for Kemal was *dictator*. What the
dailies did with the word divides them. This card follows ten items in the KB/Delpher index, each of them an article with a stated or
implied view, and each from a paper that is not an obvious "Atatürk paper".

**Algemeen Handelsblad** (Amsterdam liberal daily), 29 October 1924 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010656761:mpeg21:a0174)), a special correspondent on
"De Turksche dictatuur": Kemal, the correspondent reports, argues that the People's Party saved Turkey and so has the right to exercise its dictatorship, though the constitution speaks of the whole people's sovereignty. *"heeft Moestapha Kemal veel van de extreme fascisten overgenomen"*, the correspondent concludes, the difference being that the fascists openly despise parliament while Kemal "verbergt deze echter achter de comedie van een parlement." The same article asks whether it is right to base a policy on one man's authority.

**De Volkskrant** (Catholic daily), 20 October 1926 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMKB12:000137094:mpeg21:a00067)), "Turkije's nieuwste aanwinst": the statue of "den Tunkschen dictator Moestafa Kemal" in Constantinople, in a coat ("gekleede jas"), went up despite religious and artistic protest; *"de krachtfiguur van het moderne Turkije staat thans in een regenjas"* (on his pedestal). It is copied from the *Handelsblad* and the irony is mild.

**De Grondwet** (regional daily; confessional character not checked), 24 June 1929 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMGARO01:000174605:mpeg21:a0025)), "Het oude en het nieuwe Turkije": asked whether Angora could bear the full truth, "in een staat waar een volledige dictatuur heerscht", the paper says the uprisings and plots are made by people attached to their fathers' faith, *"die in het hervormingswerk van den dictator een vernietiging zien van den Islam"*, and doubts a whole cultural revolution is possible in the time the dictator allows. Four years later ([28 Oct 1933](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMGARO01:000177892:mpeg21:a0079), "Kemal Pascha tien jaar dictator") it writes that *"heeft president Kemal pasja, de dictator van het land, een volkomen nieuw rijk gevormd"* — and, in the same article, that democrats, Kurdish leaders and orthodox believers "werden allemaal als misdadigers ... opgehangen."

**De Tijd** (Catholic "godsdienstig-staatkundig dagblad") three times. 16 June 1930 ([verse](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010532214:mpeg21:a0148)), a comic poem signed Martin Berden: *"Kemal zwaait den dirigeerstok Als dictator in zijn land, Doch critiek wil hij niet hebben"* (verse lines run together), with Turkish journalists dancing to the cell. 30 March 1933 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010535257:mpeg21:a0051)), in an essay on what marks fascism, a one-party state is "de dictatuur van Kemal in Turkije op de Republikeinsche Partij", set beside Moscow and Rome. 28 June 1934 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010535720:mpeg21:a0003)), *Cosmopolitica*, "De appel van Kemal": waiting and tea-drinking is the motto of the "Turkendictator", and *"een der uitstekendste heerschersdeugden van den ghazi"* is patient waiting for the apple to fall.

**Voorwaarts** (social-democratic daily), 23 August 1930 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:010212863:mpeg21:a0040)), "Het nieuwe Turkije": Kurdish bitterness has grown under the regime Mustafa Kemal has run since the start of the dictatorship he began exercising in 1923, which has denied every old social and religious form and, "nadat hy al zün tegenstanders met kogel of strop onschadelijk had gemaakt," driven Turkey toward a modern order with gendarmes' bayonets. The line is *"tegenstanders met kogel of strop onschadelijk had gemaakt"*.

**De Telegraaf**, 18 July 1931 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=ddd:110570965:mpeg21:a0001)), "Een geslaagde dictatuur", by Count Carlo Sforza: *"SLECHTS één van de na-oorlogsche dictators, Moestafa Kemal, schijnt een succes te zijn, althans tot dusver."* Kemal alone kept to a policy of self-restraint and gave up the caliphate's world pretensions. Sforza adds that he came to this conviction in 1919 as Italian High Commissioner in Constantinople. The view is Sforza's and is recorded on his note ([[Carlo Sforza]]) as a new, dated, primary utterance; the paper is its carrier.

**Haagsche Courant**, 15 September 1937 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMKB04:000149289:mpeg21:a0019)), "Het nieuwe Turkije": Atatürk's democratic experiment of 1930 failed, he rules "volgens dictatoriale principes", yet *"Atatürk's dictatuur een zegen voor het land is geweest"* — he was not a dictator who despised democracy, and rules for his land and people. (The item may be syndicated copy.)

**Not recorded as gazes** (dictator label only, wire or caption): *De Standaard* 29 Dec 1932 (Kemal ordering the prayers in Turkish; "groote ontstemming" in conservative Muslim circles) and 25 Sept 1930 (Ismet "de rechterhand van den Turkschen dictator"); *Haagsche Courant* 30 Dec 1930, which says the reactionaries are "fel tegen Kemal pasja gekant, wegens diens leekenpolitiek"; *De Volkskrant* 25 Oct 1935 ("Op last van den dictator" the census house-arrest). *De Maasbode* did not surface among the roughly 700 articles retrieved.

Dutch quotations are copied exactly as the KB/Delpher OCR gives them (the OCR of 1920s newspaper print is noisy; I quote only runs that read cleanly). Confessional labels (Catholic, Protestant, social-democratic) come from the paper's own KB subtitle where it has one and otherwise from general knowledge of the Dutch press, and are marked as such.
