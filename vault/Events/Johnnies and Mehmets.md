# Johnnies and Mehmets

**The flagship case study.** The most beloved Atatürk text in the world — *"There is no difference
between the Johnnies and the Mehmets where they lie side by side here in this country of ours…"* —
carved at Anzac Cove and on the Atatürk Memorial in Canberra — both dedicated on Anzac Day, 25
April 1985, the two ends of a documented exchange: Turkey officially recognised the name "Anzac
Cove," Australia raised the memorial on Anzac Parade — recited every Anzac Day, the emotional
foundation of modern Turkish-Australian friendship.

Its provenance, verified layer by layer:
1. **1934** — the official narrative: an ailing Atatürk writes a Martyrs' Day message that Interior
   Minister [[Şükrü Kaya]] delivers at Gallipoli on 18 March. **No contemporary record of the famous
   text has been found**; Cengiz Özakıncı spent a decade in the archives looking.
2. **1953** — the earliest traceable source: Kaya's recollection in an interview with the newspaper
   *Dünya*, two decades after the fact.
3. **1978** — the "no difference between the Johnnies and the Mehmets" clause itself enters the
   text via an Australian hand, in Brisbane's *Gallipoli Fountains of Honour* project (Honest
   History's finding — David Stephens calls the quote "an Anzac confidence trick").
4. **1985** — the composite is carved in stone on two continents and becomes diplomacy.

Why it leads the project: a grieving Australia and a reconciling Turkey **co-authored the Atatürk
both needed**, then installed him at their most sacred sites. And the Şükrü Kaya problem (the vault's label, after the title of Reuben
Silverman's essay on Kaya): the words of borderless tolerance reach us through the regime's chief homogenizer, in
the middle of the deportation-law era. No single artifact demonstrates "Atatürk as Rorschach"
better. See [[Gallipoli Campaign]], [[William Birdwood]], [[Historiography of Atatürk]].
