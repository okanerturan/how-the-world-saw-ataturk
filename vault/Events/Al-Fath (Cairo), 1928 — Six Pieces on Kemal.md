# Al-Fath (Cairo), 1928 — Six Pieces on Kemal

Continuing the round-7 card on *al-Fath* (Muhibb al-Din al-Khatib's Cairo weekly), this card reads further pages of the 1928 run (IA batch of nos. 1-125, [`efath-moheb-elden-elkheeb`](https://archive.org/details/efath-moheb-elden-elkheeb)). Issue dates are from the mastheads (no. 79 = 12 Jan 1928; no. 86 = 1 Mar 1928; no. 96 = 17 May 1928; no. 102 = 28 Jun 1928; no. 112 = 6 Sep 1928; no. 119 = 25 Oct 1928). Printed page numbers; pdf page in brackets.

**1. "Memory of the abolition of the Caliphate" (no. 86, 1 Mar 1928, p. 569 [pdf 1369]), unsigned.** A "page from notes written on announcement of the abolition, 3 March 1924", printed four years on. A disappointed admirer: *"Mustafa Kemal, whose name we used to extol, for whom we prayed and whom we blessed, and from whom we hoped for the raising and honouring of Islam, acts thus towards Islam"* (مصطفى كمال الذي كنا نشيد باسمه وندعو له ونبارك عليه ونرجوه لرفعة الإسلام وإعزازه يفعل هكذا بالإسلام). The same page says the Anatolian soldier fought only out of Islamic motive and that the Indians and Afghans helped for the sake of the Caliphate Kemal then removed. Conflicted: the liberator's credit is conceded, the secularist condemned. *(English translation mine.)*

**2. "The religious fate of Turkey" (no. 96, 17 May 1928, pp. 721-723 [pdf 1521-1523]).** The paper credits it to "a great mujahid, the best-informed of Arabic writers on Turkey's affairs"; no name is printed (the description fits Shakib Arslan, but that is my inference only). It answers the claim that Turkey owes its revival to Kemal: *"it is not true, their claim that Mustafa Kemal is the one who freed Turkey; those who freed Turkey were a band of which Mustafa Kemal was one"* (فالذين حرروا تركيا هم عصابة كان مصطفى كمال واحداً منهم, p. 721). *(English translation mine.)*

**3. "The deluded" (no. 79, 12 Jan 1928, p. 452 [pdf 1252]).** Reprinted "from an article in *al-Akhbar* by one of the princes of eloquence, whose pen gives him away" (unnamed). After beating the Greeks Kemal *"thought himself to have become the man of the earth in length and breadth, and that all he says has become verses sent down from heaven"* (ظن نفسه أصبح رجل الأرض في الطول والعرض، وأن كل ما يقوله صار آيات منزلة من السماء). *(English translation mine.)* The piece also mocks his lecture on Rousseau.

**4. "The childhood of the Turkish leader, the false Robespierre" (no. 112, 6 Sep 1928, p. 172 [pdf 1778]), signed Muhammad Ali Gharib.** *"We do not much mind Mustafa Kemal's rancour against the Islamic religion and his hatred of it"* (ونحن لا نهتم كثيراً بحقد مصطفى كمال على الدين الإسلامي وكراهته له): he is an ordinary man, history will remember him for his childishness, not his genius. *(English translation mine.)*

**5. "Mustafa Kemal and Islam" (no. 102, 28 Jun 1928, pp. 17-18 [pdf 1617-1618]), signed Muhibb al-Din al-Khatib.** *"Mustafa Kemal has finished traversing the stages of the first leg of what he took upon himself, the demolition of the landmarks of Islam in Turkey"* (انتهى مصطفى كمال من طيّ مراحل الشوط الأول فيما أخذه على عاتقه من تقويض معالم الإسلام في تركيا). He lists the abolition of the sharia courts, the hat and prayer, and ends: "close your ranks". *(English translation mine.)*

**6. Jerusalem, no. 119 (25 Oct 1928, p. 301 [pdf 1897]): "Propaganda for the Turks in Palestine", signed al-Ri'awi al-Saghir (Jerusalem).** He reports a lecture "Renewal in Turkey and Islam" by Hasan Sidqi al-Dajani, who opened: *"What al-Ghazi Mustafa Kemal Pasha has done of brilliant works, which are a clear victory over time and a tremendous revolution and great development"* (إن ما قام به الغازي مصطفى كمال باشا من الأعمال الباهرة التي هي فوز مبين على الدهر، والتي هي انقلاب هائل وتطور عظيم), and claimed to have his news from the Turkish consul. The correspondent answers: *"this lecture is a stain of shame and filth with which the place where it was given was defiled"* (إن هذه المحاضرة لطخة عار وقذارة لوّث بها المكان الذي ألقيت فيه). Two voices, one admiring (Dajani, mediated by *al-Fath*) and one hostile. *(English translation mine.)*

**Caveats.** Items 1-3 are unsigned; item 2's author is not named. Quotes are the Arabic as read from page images (poor OCR, so no machine check).
