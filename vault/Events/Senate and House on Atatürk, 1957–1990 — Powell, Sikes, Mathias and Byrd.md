# Senate and House on Atatürk, 1957–1990 — Powell, Sikes, Mathias and Byrd

Four statements by members of the US Congress (eleven were staged; the independent check dropped seven that state no view of Atatürk or were misattributed: Simon's line is Riegle's) from the bound edition of the *Congressional Record*, 1957–1990, found through the GovInfo full-text search ("Ataturk", collection CRECB) and read in the PDF text layer of 111 granules (Senate, House and Extensions of Remarks sittings; the 1957–1992 range left over from [[Congressional Record Pens on Atatürk — Connally, Pepper, Levin, Moynihan, Byrd, Lantos and Others]]). Each is the member's own words; articles, letters and staff papers that members had printed in the Record are not counted (Kennedy 1959, Sparkman 1958, Jenner 1958, Dole's AHEPA letter 1985 and the Bowles lecture were checked and dropped). **Evidence P** (the official record). The scans are two-column OCR with some interleaving, so page numbers are approximate (from running heads) and quotes were rebuilt from the interleaved lines. Six of the eleven are Armenian-question debates (Coelho 1985, Aspin 1985, Simon 1986, Levin, Wilson and Dole 1990): those are the speakers' framing, not the vault's. The 1981 centennial statement (Mathias) is the fullest.

Names: [[Adam Clayton Powell Jr.]], [[Robert L. F. Sikes]], [[Charles McC. Mathias]], [[Robert C. Byrd]].

## Adam Clayton Powell Jr. — 1957-08-27

Sends Republic Day greetings and sketches Atatürk's revolution, calling the transformation of Turkey since 1919 admirable and its modernisation 'the goal of Kemal Ataturk'.

> No one can fail to admire the courage with which this country has tackled its huge tasks. Turkey, under Mustafa Kemal Ataturk, set out to be a modern nation and became a healthy republic with amazing vitality.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks (House), 27 August 1957, Congressional Record vol. 103 (bound), pp. ~16181-16182 (granule CRECB-1957-pt12-4-3) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1957-pt12/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1957-pt12-4-3.pdf>

**Caveat.** Delivered as one of his greetings on the anniversaries of the Bandung Conference states ('I announced that I would address the United States Congress each time there was an anniversary'); the account of the reforms reads like embassy-supplied material, so it is a courtesy text as well as a view. Page numbers from the running heads (OCR).

## Robert L. F. Sikes — 1975-10-02

Arguing to lift the Turkish arms embargo, credits 'the strong leadership of Ataturk' with giving the Turks a new concept of national responsibility and progress.

> Following World War I, the Turks developed a new concept under the strong leadership of Ataturk-Mustafa Kemal-of national responsibility and progress.

**Source.** House of Representatives, 2 October 1975, Congressional Record vol. 121 (bound), p. ~31479 (granule CRECB-1975-pt24-5-2) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt24/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1975-pt24-5-2.pdf>

**Caveat.** One sentence in the Cyprus arms-embargo debate, a defence of Turkey against the 'Terrible Turks' talk; the praise serves the argument. State from his own colleague's reference ('the distinguished gentleman from Florida') and the standard record. Page from running head.

## Charles McC. Mathias — 1981-07-30

Marks the centennial: Atatürk shaped Turkey 'from the ruins of a decaying empire', a charismatic rationalist and organiser whose legacy is Turkey's challenge.

> His extraordinary determination, dedication, and dynamic leadership combined with an unwavering vision of Turkey's role in the world drove him to a relentless reform of Turkey's social and political structure.
>
> From a defeated, divided dynasty torn between East and West, he created a modern democratic republic with an unwavering commitment to the West.

**Source.** Senate, 30 July 1981, 'Reflections on Ataturk Centenary', Congressional Record vol. 127 (bound), pp. 18675-18676 (granule CRECB-1981-pt14-2-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1981-pt14/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1981-pt14-2-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** A full statement of his own (about 1,000 words) headed 'Reflections on Ataturk Centenary', quoting Atatürk and Toynbee. State and party from the standard record, not the passage. The OCR misreads some letters ('Kemv.l'); quotes taken from clean lines.

## Robert C. Byrd — 1990-02-21

In a long speech against the Armenian resolution, praises Atatürk: a secular Western-oriented republic with no model in 1923, 'but succeed it did'.

> It did not come about simply by coincidence. When Ataturk founded the Turkish Republic in 1923, there was no single example in the world to suggest that the adventure on which she was launched might succeed, but succeed it did.

**Source.** Senate, 21 February 1990 (S.J. Res. 212 debate), Congressional Record vol. 136 (bound), pp. ~2156-2160 (granule CRECB-1990-pt2-4-2) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1990-pt2/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1990-pt2-4-2.pdf>

**Caveat.** Sensitive thread: he is speaking against the Armenian commemorative resolution (Dole's S.J. Res. 212) and uses Atatürk to argue Turkey's value to the West; this record states his view of Atatürk only. A second, earlier statement than his 2007 one already in the dataset. Page from running head (OCR).
