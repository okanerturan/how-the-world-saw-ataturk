# Australian Editors on Kemal after Chanak, October 1922

A fortnight after [[Billy Hughes]] had warned Mustapha Kemal what Australia's entry into a war would mean, a goldfields daily wrote that Kemal had been the one keeping the peace.

**Kalgoorlie Miner, 14 October 1922, p. 4, leader "MUSTAPHA KEMAL PASHA."** (read on the page image, 18 Nov 2026). Written after the Mudanya armistice: "We shall probably be very near the mark if we ascribe the Mudania success and the preservation of peace so far to the personality of Mustapha Kemal Pasha." The leader casts him as "a restraining influence" on his colleagues and soldiers, says he "could have had war at any moment he pleased" and that "to say that Kemal wanted war is silly", and calls him "no dictator, but a part — a large part certainly — of a constitutional Government". His aims it judges "from his actions, and, as we learn them on the authority of an Englishman who has long known him well". Record: `kalgoorlie-miner-1922-10-14`.

Compare the New Zealand papers of the same weeks in [[New Zealand Editors on Kemal, September 1922 – 1923]].
