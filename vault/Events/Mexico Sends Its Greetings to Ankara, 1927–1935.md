# Mexico Sends Its Greetings to Ankara, 1927–1935

**A friendly, distant, mostly formal gaze — plus one nickname.** Mexico and republican Turkey had
almost nothing to trade and little to say, but the paper trail shows how Mexico City addressed
Atatürk across eight years. The friendship treaty was signed in Rome on 25 May 1927 by Turkey's
ambassador to Italy, Suat (Suad) Bey, and Mexico's minister in Rome, Carlos Puig Casauranc —
under Plutarco Elías Calles, whom Mexicans nicknamed "el Turco" (Carlos Martínez Assad, in his prologue to Orgaz, 2021,
credits his clipped moustache or a surname people took for Lebanese, and adds that he governed at the
same time as Atatürk). Mexico, newly in the League, voted for Turkey's admission in 1932. On
17 October 1933 Atatürk received Mexico's Genaro Estrada — the poet-diplomat — at credential presentation, the two
speaking French.

The warmest words are recorded in Turkish diplomatic reporting (Ankara's Prime Ministry Republican
Archive, BCA). On 12 October 1934 Turkey's Washington ambassador Mehmet Münir presented credentials
to President Abelardo Rodríguez, who received him in Chapultepec and asked him to carry **his
"greetings and best wishes to Gazi Mustafa Kemal Atatürk."** In March–April 1935 the Turkish chargé
Tahsin Ömer called on Foreign Minister Emilio Portes Gil, who, in his report, spoke "in terms of
appreciation about the Turkish nation and Atatürk" and "with longing" of what Atatürk's "high genius,
determination and will" had done for his people in so short a time. (Both are the Turkish diplomat's
account of Mexican courtesies — real, but to be read as diplomatic politeness and as filtered by the
man who reported them.) The same year Mexico's minister in Paris, Marte R. Gómez, relayed that
Ambassador Suat expressed "frank sympathy for our country"; Atatürk, for his part, is said to have
nicknamed that Turkish chargé "Mayatepek" over his theory that Maya and Turkish were cognate
(an episode of Atatürk's own gaze, noted here only for the pairing).

Verdict on the gaze: **admiring but institutional**. The two regimes are so often compared (see the
Mexican Revolution parallels developed by Orgaz, 2021) that it is useful to see how little contact
there actually was — Turkey's Mexico post was run from Washington until 1947, Mexico's from Madrid —
and that the contemporary Mexican words on Atatürk we can verify are ministers' courtesies, not an
essayist's judgement. For that, the earliest Latin American text remains [[José Carlos Mariátegui]]'s.
