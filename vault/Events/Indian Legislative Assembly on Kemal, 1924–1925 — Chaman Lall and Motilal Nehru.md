# Indian Legislative Assembly on Kemal, 1924–1925 — Chaman Lall and Motilal Nehru

Two Legislative Assembly speeches. Chaman Lall (25 March 1924) in the debate on passports for the Khilafat delegation to Angora: Kemal's sword, not British diplomacy, had won revision of Sevres. Motilal Nehru (11 February 1925) used Kemal's £40 salary as head of the administration in the debate on the President's pay. Earlier Indian reactions: [[Gaya to Angora — Indian Voices on Kemal, 1922–1924]]; later: Jawaharlal Nehru. (A third voice of the 1924 debates, Harchandrai Vishindas on 6 March 1924, said the abolition of the Caliphate was 'a lesson' the Government of India should take, and is noted in the candidates file.)

## Diwan Chaman Lall (1924-03-25)

> it was not the Government of India but the Sword of Mustapha Kemal Pasha.

Legislative Assembly Debates, vol. IV pt. III, 25 Mar 1924, p. 2214. Said as the Chair stopped him from turning the debate into one on foreign policy; the Khilafat delegation to Angora was to present congratulatory addresses to 'Ghazi Mustafa Kamal Pasha'. Text read from the OCR of the official report; obvious OCR slips are silently corrected in the quote.

## Motilal Nehru (1925-02-11)

> Now that is a government of the people by themselves, and if a person of the distinction of Mustapha Kemal Pasha and his position in the country as the head of the administration, can keep all the dignity attached to that position on £40 a month

Legislative Assembly Debates, vol. I pt. II, 11 Feb 1925, President's Salary Bill, p. 975. He prefaces it 'I do not know how far it is correct' (a newspaper report). Instrumental use of Kemal as a model of frugal republican leadership; 'Pasha' OCR-corrected from 'Fasha'. Father of Jawaharlal Nehru.
