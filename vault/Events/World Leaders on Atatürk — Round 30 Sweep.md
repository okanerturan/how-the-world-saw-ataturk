# World Leaders on Atatürk — Round 30 Sweep

Heads of state and government, ministers and party leaders found by the round-30 Monid sweep (Exa web search and SerpApi Google Books over 546 names not yet in the census) and verified at the source: official transcripts, presidency and UN sites, the press of record. Wreath-laying, protocol formulas and labels were rejected; each record below is the speaker's own stated view of Atatürk. Translations are marked.

## Vladimir Lenin, 1921
*Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars, Soviet Russia* — admiring, memoir, evidence P.

Briefing his envoy, calls Mustafa Kemal no socialist but a good organiser, talented commander and clever statesman leading a bourgeois-national revolution.

> — Конечно, Мустафа Кемаль-паша не социалист,— говорил Владимир Ильич,— но, по-видимому, хороший организатор, талантливый полководец, ведет буржуазно-национальную революцию, прогрессивно настроенный человек, умный государственный деятель. [English translation: Of course, Mustafa Kemal Pasha is not a socialist,' Vladimir Ilyich said, 'but he is evidently a good organiser, a talented commander, leading a bourgeois-national revolution, a progressive-minded man, a clever statesman.]

Mediated by: S. I. Aralov, Vospominaniya sovetskogo diplomata 1922–1923 (Moscow: IMO, 1960), pp. 34–35, recalling his briefing by Lenin.

⚠️ Recollected about 38 years later in a Soviet-era memoir; Lenin's words are Aralov's reconstruction. The meeting took place in Moscow between Aralov's recall in early November 1921 and his departure for Ankara at the end of December 1921 (pp. 23, 33–36). The scan reads 'не социалист' as '—: е социалист' (OCR). Lenin goes on to say he is sure Kemal 'will knock the arrogance out of the imperialists' and that Russia must help the Turkish people. Read in the archive.org open text.

## Theodor Heuss, 1929-01
*German liberal publicist and Reichstag deputy, later first Federal President* — hostile, article, evidence S.

After a Balkan journey, judges Kemal's Turkey a 'parliamentary-clothed dictatorship' that arranges elections and holds power through police and courts.

> parlamentarisch umkleidete Diktatur

Mediated by: Mathie 2023, Der Türken- und Türkeidiskurs in Schulbüchern 1919–1945 (Klinkhardt, open access), quoting Heuss, 'Balkanreise', Die Hilfe 35.1 (1929), pp. 20–22, here p. 21.

⚠️ Only the phrase and the paraphrase ('Wahlen arrangiere', held up by 'Polizei und Gericht') are given in the secondary source; Heuss's article itself was not read. Mangold-Will (Begrenzte Freundschaft, 2013, p. 253 snippet) says Heuss's 1928 assessment of Turkey was positive overall despite this. A judgement of Kemal's regime rather than of the man; hence conflicted.

## Douglas MacArthur, 1953-11-10
*General of the Army, former Supreme Commander in the Far East; message to the Turkish nation for the 15th anniversary of Atatürk's death, issued through the Turkish press directorate* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

'My old friend', a statesman of world stature underrated even by some Turks; had he lived, the world scene would be fundamentally different.

> [translation] Bugün 10 kasım, eski dostum Kemal Atatürkün ölüm yıldönümüdür. O zamandan beri on beş sene geçmiş olmasına rağmen onun ufulüne kendimi henüz alıştıramıyorum. Eğer sağ olsaydı milletlerarası sahnenin bugünkü hercümerc hali esaslı bir şekilde değişik olurdu. Atatürk, Türkiyede daima memleketin bugünkü inkişafının modern atası olarak anılacaktır. Fakat, onun dünya çapında devlet adamı olarak yüksek kudreti belki de bazı vatandaşları tarafından bile tam mânasiyle anlaşılamamaktadır. O tanıdığım diğer insanlar gibi, hür insanlığın köleliğe karşı durması meselesinin cihanşümul mahiyetini idrak ediyordu, onun dünyayı sarsan mücadelenin vahim bir anında vefatı, telâfisi gayri kabil olmakta devam etmektedir. [English translation: Today, 10 November, is the anniversary of the death of my old friend Kemal Atatürk. Although fifteen years have passed, I still cannot accustom myself to his passing. Had he lived, the present chaos of the international scene would be fundamentally different. In Turkey Atatürk will always be remembered as the modern father of the country's present development. But his great power as a statesman of world stature is perhaps not fully understood even by some of his compatriots. Like the other people I have known, he grasped the universal nature of free humanity's stand against slavery; his death at a grave moment of the world-shaking struggle remains irreparable.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 10 Nov 1953, p. 7, box 'Mac Arthur'un mesajı' (Ankara, A.A., 9 Nov): Anatolian Agency's Turkish text of the message.

⚠️ English original not seen. The Anatolian Agency dateline is Ankara, 9 Nov; the message itself opens 'Today, 10 November'. 'O tanıdığım diğer insanlar gibi' ('like the other people I have known') is as printed and may stand for 'like few other people I have known'. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive). See also macarthur-1932-09-27 and macarthur-1963-11-10.

## Konrad Adenauer, 1954-03-18
*Chancellor of West Germany; reply to Prime Minister Menderes's dinner speech, Ankara Palas, on his official visit* — admiring, speech, evidence J.

The 'genius leader, the unforgettable Atatürk', who with the Turkish nation worked wonders out of a state sunk in hopelessness; the German nation's deep admiration.

> Müsaade buyurursanız, ben de Alman milletinin asil Türk milleti ve onun dahi önderi unutulmaz Atatürk'ün nice sonsuz zorluklar ve siyasi ümitsizlikler içinde bulunan bir devletten ne gibi harikalar yaratmağa muktedir olduğunu görmekle duyduğu derin hayranlığı ifade etmek isterim. [English translation: If you will allow me, I too would like to express the deep admiration the German nation feels at seeing what wonders the noble Turkish nation and its genius leader, the unforgettable Atatürk, were able to create out of a state caught in endless difficulties and political hopelessness.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 19 Mar 1954, p. 7 ('Almanya Başvekili dün Ankaraya geldi', section 'Adenauer şerefine verilen ziyafet'): the paper's Turkish rendering of a speech given in German.

⚠️ Opening of his reply to Menderes at the dinner on his arrival day; he goes on to Turkey as a factor of stability in the Near East and its place in the Western alliance. Earlier that day he laid a wreath at Anıtkabir and stood in silence (Milliyet p. 1, reporter's narration). The German original (Bulletin des Presse- und Informationsamtes der Bundesregierung, March 1954) was not seen. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Josip Broz Tito, 1954-04-13
*President of Yugoslavia; reply toast at President Bayar's dinner, Çankaya, Ankara, during his state visit* — admiring, speech, evidence J.

Ankara as the dignified expression of a national strength inspired by the memory of 'the legendary personality of the great Atatürk'.

> Türkiyenin hükûmet merkezinde bulunmaktan derin bir memnunluk duymaktayım. Bu Türk milletinin büyük Atatürkün efsanevî şahsiyeti hatırasından mülhem olan kuvvetinin ve canlı millî şuurunun vakur bir ifadesidir. [English translation: I feel deep satisfaction at being in the seat of government of Turkey. It is the dignified expression of the Turkish nation's strength, inspired by the memory of the legendary personality of the great Atatürk, and of its living national consciousness.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 14 Apr 1954, p. 7 ('Türk-Yugoslav siyasî görüşmeleri başladı', section 'Titonun nutku'): the paper's Turkish rendering of a toast given in Serbo-Croatian.

⚠️ Opening lines of the reply toast; the rest is about Yugoslav sympathy, the Ankara Pact and Balkan peace. The same day he laid a wreath at Anıtkabir (Milliyet p. 1); no book entry is printed. The original Serbo-Croatian text (Borba / Politika, 14 Apr 1954; Govori i članci vol. IX) was not seen. The Instagram line 'Sizi hayatım boyunca daima ilham kaynağı olarak yaşattım' attributed to his Anıtkabir entry has no source. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Camille Chamoun, 1955-04-02
*President of Lebanon; reply to the Governor and Mayor of Ankara, Kemal Aygün, at the Foreign Ministry residence, during his state visit* — admiring, speech, evidence J.

Ankara as the place where Atatürk prepared the War of Independence and 'how successfully he brought it to a conclusion'.

> Ankara şehrinin tarihini ve Atatürk'ün burada İstiklâl Harbini nasıl hazırladığını ve nasıl muvaffakiyetle neticelendirdiğini yakinen bilmekteyim. [English translation: I know closely the history of the city of Ankara, and how Atatürk prepared the War of Independence here and how successfully he brought it to a conclusion.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 3 Apr 1955, p. 7 (continuation of 'Türk-Lübnan ticaret anlaşması yapıldı', section 'Şamoun'un mukabelesi'): the paper's Turkish rendering.

⚠️ Borderline under the strict test: the only judgement is 'muvaffakiyetle' (successfully). He adds that he had earlier spent five days in Ankara. The Anıtkabir visit is narration only. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive) by a reader agent; not re-read by the main agent.

## Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, 1956-05-16
*Shah of Iran; speech on receiving the honorary citizenship of Ankara, Türk Ocağı, during his state visit* — admiring, speech, evidence J.

'The great Atatürk', whose deep friendship with his father Reza Shah he reveres; the two 'great guides' who built Iranian–Turkish friendship on a solid foundation.

> Merhum hükümdar pederimle büyük Atatürk arasındaki derin dostluk râbıtaları, benim için daima şâyânı hürmet olmuştur. Onun için şimdi burada bu iki büyük rehberin hâtıralarını, derin bir heyecanla meşbû olarak yâdediyorum. Onların sağlam bir temel üzerine kurmuş oldukları büyük eseri, İran - Türk dostluğunu bizler ebedi kılmalıyız. [English translation: The deep bonds of friendship between my late father the sovereign and the great Atatürk have always commanded my respect. So here and now I remember these two great guides with deep emotion. We must make eternal the great work they built on a solid foundation, Iranian–Turkish friendship.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 17 May 1956, p. 1 ('Şâh, Ankara hemşehrisi oldu'): the paper's Turkish rendering.

⚠️ Short speech after Governor Kemal Aygün presented the honorary-citizenship certificate. The day before (15 May), in a statement to Ankara Radio in Persian, he spoke of a friendship founded 'under the guidance of the enlightened ideas and strong hands of the late Reza Shah and the late Atatürk' ('Merhum Rızâ Şâh'ın ve merhum Atatürk'ün aydınlık fikirleri ve kavi ellerinin rehberliği ile'; Milliyet 16 May 1956 p. 5, 'Şahın demeci'). His Anıtkabir visit (15 May) is narration only. The census's Reza Shah records concern his father. Read on the page images (Milliyet's subscriber archive) by a reader agent; re-checked on the crop by the main agent.

## Theodor Heuss, 1957-05-06
*Federal President of West Germany* — admiring, speech, evidence S.

On the first German state visit since the Kaiser, likens post-war Germany to Turkey after 1918, which began a new chapter through Atatürk's great statesmanship.

> Wir werden mit ihnen fertig werden, wie die Türkei nach 1918 mit den Lasten einer Vergangenheit fertig wurde und in dem großen Staatsmanntum von Kemal Atatürk ein ganz neues Blatt der Staatsgeschichte zu schreiben begonnen hat. [English translation: We will cope with them, as Turkey after 1918 coped with the burdens of a past and, in the great statesmanship of Kemal Atatürk, began to write a wholly new page of its state history.]

Mediated by: Mathie 2023 (open access), quoting Heuss, 'Erwiderung des Herrn Bundespräsidenten auf die Ansprache des Türkischen Staatspräsidenten bei dem Abendessen am 6. Mai ds. Js. in Ankara', 1957, Archiv der Stiftung Bundespräsident-Theodor-Heuss-Haus, B122, 924.

⚠️ Reply to President Bayar's toast at the state dinner, Ankara, 6 May 1957; 'ihnen' = the consequences of the war (bracketed in the source). Read in the archive.org open text of the book's introduction; the archive typescript itself was not seen.

## Konstantinos Karamanlis, 1959-05-07
*Prime Minister of Greece; statement on arrival at Esenboğa airport, Ankara, three months after the Zurich–London agreements on Cyprus* — instrumental, quote, evidence J.

'The great Atatürk', who with Venizelos laid the foundations of a Turkish–Greek friendship that served both countries; invoked to cast renewed cooperation as historic necessity.

> Büyük Atatürk ve Elefterios Venizelos, Türk-Yunan dostluğunun temellerini attıkları zaman bu dostluk her iki memleket için faydalı olmuştur. Bugün ise Türk-Yunan müşterek çalışmaları bir tarihi zaruret halini almıştır. [English translation: When the great Atatürk and Eleftherios Venizelos laid the foundations of Turkish–Greek friendship, this friendship was beneficial to both countries. Today Turkish–Greek joint work has become a historic necessity.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 8 May 1959, p. 5 (continuation of 'Karamanlis Ankara'da', section 'Karamanlisin beyanatı'): the paper's Turkish rendering of his airport statement.

⚠️ Arrival statement to the press, 7 May 1959; he visited Anıtkabir the same afternoon (narration only). A political invocation rather than a personal portrait. The census already holds his nephew Kostas Karamanlis (PM 2004–09); this is the elder Konstantinos (1907–1998). Greek original not seen. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive) by a reader agent and re-checked on the crop by the main agent.

## Alec Douglas-Home, 1963-11-10
*Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (from Oct 1963); message for the 25th anniversary of Atatürk's death* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

Known in Britain first as a brilliant commander, then as a statesman among the great national leaders; the Anglo-Turkish partnership is largely his work.

> [translation] Atatürk'ün adı bizce, hemen hemen 50 yıl önce parlak bir Türk askeri kumandanı olarak biliniyordu. Barışı takiben ona büyük milli liderler arasında tarihteki daimi yerini kazandıran devletçilik sıfatlarıyle Atatürk'ü tanıdık. […] Mutlu işbirliği; büyük anlamda Atatürk'ün çalışmalarının neticesidir. Atatürk'ün ölümünün 25. yıldönümünde, O'nu kahraman asker olarak saygı ile yâd eder, modern Türkiye'nin Ata'sını, devlet adamı Atatürk'ü, takdir ve şükranla anarız. [English translation: To us Atatürk's name was known almost fifty years ago as that of a brilliant Turkish military commander. After the peace we came to know Atatürk through the qualities of statesmanship that won him his permanent place in history among the great national leaders. […] This happy cooperation is in a large sense the result of Atatürk's work. On the 25th anniversary of his death we remember him with respect as a heroic soldier, and we honour the father of modern Turkey, the statesman Atatürk, with appreciation and gratitude.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 10 Nov 1963, p. 1 ('Bütün dünya anıyor…'): the paper's Turkish rendering of a message for the 25th anniversary of Atatürk's death.

⚠️ 'Almost fifty years ago' points to Gallipoli, 1915. One of ten leaders' messages Milliyet printed on 10 Nov 1963 for the 25th anniversary (seven on p. 1, three on p. 7). Kennedy's (kennedy-1963-11) was a recorded message requested by the Turkish government, so the others were probably solicited the same way. Original-language text not seen. ## Habib Bourguiba, 1963-11-10
*President of Tunisia; message for the 25th anniversary of Atatürk's death* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

The 'Gazi' who refused defeat when all seemed lost, restored his country's sovereignty and built a modern state: an inspiration for newly sovereign nations.

> [translation] Milletimiz «Gazi» nin ölmez eseri için en büyük hayranlığı duymakta, hâtırasına da en büyük hürmeti beslemektedir. Biz O'nun şahsında, savaş meydanlarında, büyük bir asker olduğunu ispat ettikten sonra, herşeyin tamamen kaybolduğu zannedildiği bir anda, milletinden ümidini kesmeyi ve mağlûbiyeti kabul etmeyi şiddetle reddeden Tanrının seçtiği büyük insanı tebcil ediyoruz. […] Atatürk böylece, ölümü esarete tercih eden bir milletin neler yapabileceğini hayretler içinde bulunan dünyaya göstermiştir. Bu örnek unutulmayacaktır. Memleketinin eğemenliğinin mimarı olarak, biz Atatürk'ün şahsında, parçalanmak yolunda olan bir İmparatorluğun enkazı üstünde ilerlemeye yönelmiş, müessir ve modern bir devlet kurmuş devrimciye hayranlık duymaktayız. O'nun ölmez eseri, egemenliklerini elde etmiş milletlerin kaderlerine hükmedenler için ışıklı bir örnek ve bir ilham kaynağı olarak kalacaktır. [English translation: Our nation feels the greatest admiration for the immortal work of the 'Gazi' and holds his memory in the highest respect. […] Atatürk thus showed an astonished world what a nation that prefers death to slavery can do. This example will not be forgotten. As the architect of his country's sovereignty, we admire in Atatürk the revolutionary who, on the ruins of an empire breaking apart, built an effective, modern state turned towards progress. His immortal work will remain a shining example and a source of inspiration for those who govern the destinies of nations that have won their sovereignty.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 10 Nov 1963, p. 1 ('Bütün dünya anıyor…') with continuation on p. 7: the paper's Turkish rendering of a message for the 25th anniversary of Atatürk's death.

## Hayato Ikeda, 1963-11-10
*Prime Minister of Japan; message for the 25th anniversary of Atatürk's death* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

The 'great' and 'famous leader of the Turkish nation', for whom he expresses deep admiration.

[English translation: If you come to my office in Jakarta, if you come to my house in Jakarta, there is a statue of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk in my office, in my house.
