# Writers and Thinkers on Atatürk — Round 30 Sweep

Writers found by the round-30 sweep and verified at the source.

## Dagobert von Mikusch, 1929
*German writer; Gasi Mustafa Kemal (1929), quoted from the English translation Mustapha Kemal: Between Europe and Asia (Heinemann, 1931)* — admiring, book, evidence P.

Creator of modern Turkey, ranked with Lenin and Mussolini: 'a kind of dictator in a democratic toga', yet 'a statesman of an entirely modern type'.

> [translation] The history of our times abounds in outstanding men. Mustapha Kemal, the creator of modern Turkey, stands alongside Lenin, the great revolutionary, and Mussolini, who has given actual form to a new conception of the State. […] He thus possessed both legislative and executive authority, and was therefore a kind of dictator in a democratic toga. But he never disregarded the national representatives or underrated their importance. […] In the exercise of this rare political art he was a statesman of an entirely modern type.

⚠️ Quoted from the English translation by John Linton (London: Heinemann, 1931), preface (dated Munich, spring 1929) and p. 248, read in the open DLI scan; the German original was not seen.

## Hanns Froembgen, 1937
*German writer; Kemal Ataturk: a biography (London, 1937), the English translation of his German biography of 1935* — admiring, book, evidence P.

The greatest soldier of the Ottoman army; like Edward VIII a man of action 'filled with a new, live spirit', free of obsolete conceptions.

> [translation] […] none other than Mustafa Kemal, the greatest soldier in the Ottoman Army and Enver's most unrelenting opponent. […] men of action, both filled with a new, live spirit, free from all prejudices and burdens and obsolete conceptions.

⚠️ Quoted from the English translation by Kenneth Kirkness (London, 1937), pp. 61 and 250; the German original was not seen. On p. 61 the phrase reports whom a conspirator named as Enver's successor. The p. 250 passage describes Kemal's meeting with Edward VIII in September 1936 and calls him 'his former Majesty', so it was written after the abdication of December 1936.

## G. Ward Price, 1939
*Daily Mail foreign correspondent, who first visited Angora in 1923 and attended Atatürk's funeral in November 1938; Year of Reckoning (Cassell, 1939)* — hostile, book, evidence P.

At the funeral: a career of 'savage violence and moody self-indulgence', yet mourned by weeping crowds ten days after his death.

> It was a curious experience to watch the coffin of Kemal Ataturk carried to the unusual resting-place of the Ankara Ethnographic Museum, surrounded by international honours, and saluted by his old British antagonist of the Dardanelles. His career had been one of savage violence and moody self-indulgence, yet his people mourned him by hysterical weeping beside his coffin even ten days after his death.

⚠️ Year of Reckoning (London: Cassell, 1939), p. 343, read in the open DLI scan. In his memoir Extra-Special Correspondent (Harrap, 1957; lending copy, snippets only) he credits Kemal with 'such a national reconstruction as contemporary history had never witnessed'. His own Daily Mail interviews with Kemal were not reached (British Newspaper Archive is paywalled).

## Ze'ev Jabotinsky, 1939
*Founder of Revisionist Zionism* — hostile, article, evidence S.

On the South Tyrol transfer, counted Kemal Pasha among rulers who rid themselves of minorities: the Armenians by massacre, the Greeks by deportation in 1923.

> Кемаль Паша тоже однажды избавился от армянского меньшинства… перебив их. [English translation: Kemal Pasha too once got rid of the Armenian minority… by slaughtering them.]

Mediated by: Russian translation by 'B. Ts.' (Wikisource, from Khroniki Ierusalima) ← 'A Talk with Zangwill', Hadegel (Revisionist journal, Manchuria/China), 1939.

⚠️ Sensitive: the attribution of the Armenian massacres to Kemal is Jabotinsky's, recorded, not adopted. A translation of a translation. The essay then quotes, apparently as Zangwill's recalled words, a view that the 1923 exchange proved beneficial. Check: the view that the 1923 exchange proved beneficial is a 1936 Geneva interlocutor's ('Ваше Сиятельство'), not Zangwill's; Jabotinsky himself calls it a 'зверским изгнанием' (brutal expulsion).

## George Orwell, 1943-10-22
*English writer and Tribune columnist* — hostile, article, evidence P.

Cites the Left's praise of Mustapha Kemal in 1935–39 as an example of its readiness to accept 'very doubtful allies' against Fascism.

> In the years 1935-9, when almost any ally against Fascism seemed acceptable, left-wingers found themselves praising Mustapha Kemal and then developing a tenderness for Carol of Rumania.

⚠️ 'Who Are the War Criminals?', review of 'Cassius' (Michael Foot), The Trial of Mussolini, Tribune, 22 Oct 1943; Collected Essays, Journalism and Letters vol. 2, item 54 (archive.org open text). The judgement is implied: the sentence follows 'the Left has also been willing to shut its eyes to a great deal and to accept some very doubtful allies'. The lead's attribution to 'Notes on Nationalism' (1945) is wrong; that essay does not mention Kemal.

## Irfan Orga, 1950
*Turkish air-force officer and writer in England; Portrait of a Turkish Family (1950), a memoir that describes meeting Atatürk* — conflicted, memoir, evidence P.

A 'lean, fanatic's face' with a dazzling smile and an 'impatient, autocratic temperament'; Orga, who had met him, grieved 'as if a friend had gone'.

> I remembered how the lean, fanatic's face could light with a smile so dazzling that even his enemies forgave him much and how, more frequently, the pressed-in lips smiled when the eyes remained dark and sombre, challenging. […] the tall stern leader with the hard eyes that could so suddenly soften, the welcoming hand on the shoulder and the amused, harsh voice that said: 'Well, Lieutenant, still running the Air Force?' The lack of ceremony surrounding him upon formal occasions had not perhaps been in keeping with his impatient, autocratic temperament, yet sometimes he had affected simplicity.

⚠️ Chapter 28, on November 1938; read in a digitised text of the Eland edition, which has no page numbers. His later Atatürk (with Margarete Orga, Michael Joseph, 1962) drew a Turkish Embassy protest against 'the words used to describe Ataturk's character' (Evening Standard, Jan 1962), per Ateş Orga's afterword.

## Sati' al-Husri, 1950
*Arab nationalist theorist, former Ottoman educator* — admiring, article, evidence P.

Rebutting Hifni Mahmud Pasha: Kemal succeeded by active nationalism, a Turkish state on language and history, wisely renouncing the Arab provinces.

> لا شك في أنه أظهر حكمة وكياسة بعدم الطمع في استرجاع الولايات العربية، بعد اجلاء المحتلين عن الولايات التركية. [English translation: There is no doubt that he showed wisdom and tact in not coveting the recovery of the Arab provinces, after expelling the occupiers from the Turkish provinces.]

⚠️ 'Al-istishhad bi-tarikh al-Atrak', reply to Hifni Mahmud Pasha's article in al-Musawwar (date OCR-garbled, c. Mar 1950), in Ara' wa ahadith fi al-qawmiyya al-'arabiyya (1951; 1985 reprint), pp. 79–82, quote p. 81; Arabic OCR, punctuation normalised. Uses Kemal to argue for Arab unity.

## Bernard Lewis, 1961
*British-American historian of the Middle East; The Emergence of Modern Turkey (1961)* — admiring, book, evidence P.

A tough, brilliant soldier, hard drinker and wencher of immense will; a dictator, but one who respected decency and legality, without terror or camps.

> A tough and brilliant soldier, a hard drinker and wencher, he was in all things a man of immense will and abounding vitality. ... His was a dictatorship without the uneasy over-the-shoulder glance, the terror of the door-bell, the dark menace of the concentration camp.

⚠️ The Emergence of Modern Turkey, ch. VIII 'The Kemalist Republic', p. 290 in the 2nd edition (OUP/RIIA 1968), read in the open archive.org text (THEEMERGENCEOFMODERNTURKEY). The same wording is in the 1961 first edition (archive.org search-inside of emergenceofmoder0000bern_d0k7 and others); its page number is not confirmed. The paragraph also says 'By his contemporaries he was often called a dictator, and in a sense he certainly was', and that he showed 'a respect for decency and legality, for human and political standards'. The admiration is qualified: political activity against the regime was banned and the press was strictly controlled.

## Lord Kinross, 1964
*British historian; Atatürk: The Rebirth of a Nation (Weidenfeld & Nicolson, 1964)* — conflicted, book, evidence P.

His biographer on the private man: 'without love in his nature', one who 'could not endure to be loved too much'.

> In human terms, Kemal was a man without love in his nature. He understood people. But he did not love them. […] Kemal was a man wedded to his career and his country and the society of other men, to whom women meant little save as a source of distraction, an outlet for his appetites, and a stimulus to his masculine vanity. […] Kemal liked, in his vanity, to be loved, and throughout his life chose women who took the initiative in showing their feelings. But he could not endure to be loved too much […]

Mediated by: archive.org full-text search snippets of the 1964 London edition; pages from Rustow 1968, n. 28.

⚠️ Assembled from full-text search snippets of lending copies of the biography; pages not seen by us (Rustow, 'Atatürk as Founder of a State', n. 28, gives pp. 164, 259 and 391). Rustow quotes the last sentence as 'could not bear to be loved too much'; Kinross printed 'endure'. See also lord-kinross-1968-11-10.

## Lord Kinross, 1968-11-10
*British historian, author of Atatürk: The Rebirth of a Nation (1964); guest at the 30th-anniversary ceremonies in Istanbul* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

'The most beloved of the Turks', 'the greatest statesman and politician of the 20th century'; saving Istanbul at Gallipoli was his greatest achievement.

> «Türklerin en sevgilisi, büyük dosta... 20. yüzyılın en büyük devlet ve siyaset adamının hâtırasına.» […] «Birinci Dünya Savaşı, Osmanlı İmparatorluğu için bir son, Atatürk için bir başlangıçtı. Gelibolu harekâtı sırasında, üstün kuvvetler karşısında, İstanbul ve Boğazları kurtarması, Atatürk'ün en büyük başarısıdır. Mustafa Kemal, 1918'de bile hâlâ mağlûp edilmemiş bir kumandandı. İstiklâl Savaşı için, Anadolu'nun bağrında halkı birleştiren bu büyük adam, bütün zorlukları da yenmesini bilmiştir. İstiklâl mücadelesi, sadece askerî yönü ile değil, politik yönü ile de büyük önem taşır. Mücadele, Sakarya ile tam bir zafere ulaşmıştır.» [English translation: To the most beloved of the Turks, the great friend… to the memory of the greatest statesman and politician of the 20th century. […] The First World War was an end for the Ottoman Empire and a beginning for Atatürk. His saving of Istanbul and the Straits against superior forces during the Gallipoli campaign is Atatürk's greatest achievement. Even in 1918 Mustafa Kemal was still an undefeated commander. This great man, who united the people in the heart of Anatolia for the War of Independence, knew how to overcome every difficulty. The struggle for independence matters greatly not only in its military but also in its political aspect. With Sakarya the struggle reached complete victory.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 11 Nov 1968: p. 7 ('Ulu Ata'yı andık', İstanbul'da) for his entry in the book at the Taksim Republic Monument ceremony; p. 1 ('Kinross Atatürk'ü anlattı') and p. 7 for his address to officers at the Third Army Corps headquarters, Harbiye, summarised by the paper ('Özetle şunları söylemiştir').

⚠️ The speech is the paper's summary in quotation marks ('Özetle şunları söylemiştir'); the book entry is quoted in Turkish, and the paper does not say in which language he wrote it. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Dankwart A. Rustow, 1968
*American political scientist; 'Atatürk as Founder of a State', Daedalus 97, no. 3 (1968)* — conflicted, article, evidence P.

A psychological portrait: an able, withdrawn general who mastered his ambivalence about authority, but whose personality 'began to blur and decline' in his last decade.

> The second phase (1923-27) was that of consolidation of his dictatorship, when Kemal was able to relate the personal experience of his youth to the cultural situation of Turkey as a whole. The third phase (1927-38) included the last decade of his life, when he was the country's unchallenged ruler, but when the resources of his personality, so sharply focused earlier, began to blur and decline.

⚠️ Read in an open DLI scan of the Popular Prakashan (Bombay) reprint of the Daedalus issue 'Philosophers and Kings' (catalogued as 1949). The essay's concluding 'triple accomplishment' sentence (liberator, creator of the Republic, radical reformer) is garbled in the two-column OCR and is summarised, not quoted. Milliyet printed an article by him on 31 Oct 1973 (dankwart-rustow-1973-10-31).

## Arnold J. Toynbee, 1973-10-30
*British historian; signed article 'İngiliz gözüyle' in Milliyet's series for the Republic's 50th anniversary* — admiring, article, evidence J.

In their crisis the Turks 'found a great leader', dynamic and inspiring, who like Churchill in 1940 could promise only victory through sacrifice.

> [translation] TÜRKLER Kurtuluş Savaşında heyecan verici bir zafer kazandılar. Bu zafer, cesaret ve sabırlarının armağanıydı. Ulusal tarihlerinin bu bunalımlı noktasında Türkler büyük bir önder buldular: Mustafa Kemal Atatürk... Hareketli ve ilham verici bir kişiydi Atatürk. Ne var ki, Türkler bedelini ödemekte istekli olmasalardı, yurddaşlarını, Atatürk de zafere ulaştıramazdı. 1919 da Atatürk kendi halkına, ancak aşırı fedakârlık isteyen bir zafer ihtimali vaadedebilirdi. 1940'da Churchill'in yaptığı da buydu. Türk halkı Atatürk'ün çağrısına soylu bir cevap verdi. [English translation: The Turks won a thrilling victory in the War of Liberation, the gift of their courage and patience. At this critical point in their national history the Turks found a great leader: Mustafa Kemal Atatürk… Atatürk was a dynamic and inspiring person. Yet had the Turks not been willing to pay the price, not even Atatürk could have led his compatriots to victory. In 1919 Atatürk could promise his people only the chance of a victory demanding extreme sacrifice. That is what Churchill did in 1940. The Turkish people gave Atatürk's call a noble answer.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 30 Oct 1973, p. 2: 'İngiliz gözüyle — Türk Kurtuluş Savaşı ve dünya', bylined 'Yazan: Prof. Arnold Toynbee' (Turkish translation of his article).

⚠️ Written for the Republic's 50th anniversary, two years before his death; the English original was not seen. A striking shift from his 1922 The Western Question (arnold-toynbee-1922). The next day's article in the series was by Dankwart Rustow. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Dankwart A. Rustow, 1973-10-31
*American political scientist (City University of New York); signed article 'Amerikan gözüyle' in Milliyet's series for the Republic's 50th anniversary* — admiring, article, evidence J.

A born leader who still valued organisation and legality, and a soldier who turned military into civilian rule; not a mere elitist.

> [translation] Atatürk, Türkiye'nin yaşadığı en uzun barış döneminde zaferleri her zaman hatırlanan bir askerdi ve yönetimi askerî rejimden sivil rejime çevirmeyi başarmıştı. […] Doğuştan bir liderdi; buna rağmen örgütlenmeye ve yasal gereklere çok önem veriyordu. Bütün bu kutuplar, tek bir diyalektik gerilimin parçalarını oluşturdu ve Atatürk'ün yaratıcı önderliğinin katkısıyla bir sentez yarattı. Kurduğu Batılı Türk Ulusu ve devletinin gücü, bu sentezin değerini göstermektedir. […] Gerçekte, Atatürk, salt bir elitist değildi; bu ve buna benzer birçok nedenlerde, birtakım birbirine karşı unsurları bir araya getirmiş ve belirli çelişmezlikleri uzlaştırmıştı. [English translation: Atatürk was a soldier whose victories were always remembered in the longest period of peace Turkey has known, and he succeeded in turning the government from military to civilian rule. […] He was a born leader, yet he attached great importance to organisation and legal requirements. All these poles formed parts of a single dialectical tension, and with the contribution of Atatürk's creative leadership they produced a synthesis. The strength of the Western Turkish nation and state he founded shows the value of this synthesis. […] In fact Atatürk was not a mere elitist; for this and many similar reasons he brought together a number of opposing elements and reconciled certain contradictions.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 31 Oct 1973, p. 2: 'Amerikan gözüyle — Batı dengesi, iç isyanlar ve Atatürk…', bylined Prof. Dankwart A. Rustow (Turkish translation); the first two passages as printed in the standfirst box above the headline.

⚠️ English original not seen. Warmer than his 1968 psychological portrait (dankwart-rustow-1968). Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Wolfgang Höpker, 1973-11-03
*German journalist and author (Deutsche Zeitung); signed article 'Alman gözüyle' in Milliyet's series for the Republic's 50th anniversary* — admiring, article, evidence J.

No dogmatist or ideologue but a practical statesman who knew when to wait; yet he misjudged the hold of Islamic tradition on Anatolian peasants.

> [translation] Atatürk, ne bir dogmatik idi ve ne de bir ideolog, bilâkis pratik fikirleri olan bir devlet adamı idi. Zamanının Türkiyesinde yapılması mümkün olanın sınırı aşmakta olduğunu gördüğü anda, durup münasip zamanı beklemesini biliyordu. İşte kendisinin asıl büyüklüğü de buradadır. […] Atatürk bu noktada, âdet ve geleneklerin içlerinde ne kadar büyük dirençler sakladıklarını, Anadolu köylüsünün içine İslâmî geleneklerin ne kadar derin kökler salmış olduğunu, karanlığın kuvvetini tahminde yanıldı. [English translation: Atatürk was neither a dogmatist nor an ideologue, but a statesman of practical ideas. The moment he saw that what was possible in the Turkey of his time was being exceeded, he knew how to stop and wait for the right moment. That is where his real greatness lies. […] At this point Atatürk misjudged how great a resistance customs and traditions hide, how deep Islamic traditions had rooted in the Anatolian peasant — the strength of the darkness.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 3 Nov 1973, p. 2: 'Alman gözüyle — Avrupa'ya yönelen yeni Türkiye', bylined Dr. Wolfgang Höpker (Turkish translation).

⚠️ German original not seen. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Jacques Benoist-Méchin, 1974
*French historian and writer, author of Mustapha Kémal ou la mort d'un empire (1954); À destins rompus (Albin Michel, 1974)* — admiring, book, evidence P.

A man with the energy of the greatest sultans: they wanted to give the world to Turkey, he gave Turkey to the world.

> […] un homme en qui revivaient les instincts guerriers de ses ancêtres et dont l'énergie n'était pas inférieure à celle des plus grands sultans du passé : Kemal Ataturk. […] ceux-ci avaient voulu remettre le monde à la Turquie; Mustapha Kemal, lui, a remis la Turquie au monde. [English translation: …a man in whom the warrior instincts of his ancestors lived again and whose energy was no less than that of the greatest sultans of the past: Kemal Atatürk. … They had wanted to deliver the world to Turkey; Mustapha Kemal delivered Turkey to the world.]

Mediated by: archive.org full-text search snippets of À destins rompus (1974).

⚠️ Seen only as full-text search snippets of a lending copy (no page numbers); the 1954 biography (lending only) gave narrative snippets only.

## Candido Mendes, 1981-05-18
*Brazilian political scientist; paper at the International Atatürk Symposium, Istanbul* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

A leader who 'never looked backward', whose 1923–38 revolutions set him above Europe's leaders and whose legacy is a guide for Latin America.

> [translation] "Atatürk'ün Avrupa liderlerinden üstün niteliği, 1923-1938 yılları arasında yaptığı devrimlerdir. Atatürk, hiçbir zaman geriye dönük bir lider olmamıştır" [English translation: What sets Atatürk above the leaders of Europe is the revolutions he made between 1923 and 1938. Atatürk was never a backward-looking leader.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 19 May 1981, p. 8: 'Uluslararası Atatürk Sempozyumu başladı' (name printed 'Candido Mentes'); paper 'Atatürk çağdaşlaşmasından 1950 sonrası gelişmelere, bir Latin Amerika yaklaşımı'.

⚠️ The paper adds in indirect speech that he said Atatürk's legacy is a guide for Latin American countries too. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Jerzy J. Wiatr, 1981-05-18
*Polish political sociologist; paper at the International Atatürk Symposium, Istanbul* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

Exceptional among soldiers who took power: he set aside his regime's military elements, and his republic was not a military dictatorship.

> [translation] "Atatürk'ün, bilinçli olarak, rejiminin bütünüyle askerî öğelerini önemsemeyerek, iktidara gelen askerî liderler arasındaki müstesna yerini aldığını" […] "Kemalizm sembolü, gelişmekte olan ülkelerin ilerici askerî rejimlerinin yönlerini tesbit için, geri kalmışlık ve dış bağımlılık koşulları hüküm sürdükçe, önemli ideolojik âmil olarak kalacaktır" [English translation: that Atatürk, by consciously disregarding the wholly military elements of his regime, took an exceptional place among military leaders who came to power […] 'As long as conditions of backwardness and external dependence prevail, the Kemalist symbol will remain an important ideological factor in setting the course of progressive military regimes in developing countries.']

Mediated by: Milliyet, 19 May 1981, p. 8: 'Uluslararası Atatürk Sempozyumu başladı', subhead 'Askerî liderler arasında Atatürk'.

⚠️ Partly indirect speech; a Marxist-era Polish scholar speaking eight months after the 1980 coup in Turkey. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).

## Mattei Dogan, 1981-05-18
*French political sociologist (CNRS); paper at the International Atatürk Symposium, Istanbul, for the centenary of Atatürk's birth* — admiring, quote, evidence J.

The first and greatest charismatic leader of the twentieth century, a model for Reza Shah, Gandhi, Nehru, Bourguiba, Kenyatta and Eva Perón.

> [translation] "Napolyon da bir liderdi, ama Atatürk ve de Gaulle gibi krizmatik bir lider değildi. Atatürk ve de Gaulle'ün ortak özellikleri, asker oluşlarıdır. Her ikisinin de etkileyici kişilikleri, başardıklarının kalıcı olmasını sağlamıştır. Atatürk 20. yüzyılın ilk ve en büyük karizmatik lideridir. Rıza Şah, Gandi, Nehru, Burgiba, Kenyatta ve Eva Peron'a örnek olmuştur." [English translation: Napoleon too was a leader, but not a charismatic leader like Atatürk and de Gaulle. What Atatürk and de Gaulle have in common is that they were soldiers. In both, an impressive personality ensured that what they achieved would last. Atatürk is the first and greatest charismatic leader of the 20th century. He was a model for Reza Shah, Gandhi, Nehru, Bourguiba, Kenyatta and Eva Perón.]

Mediated by: Milliyet, 19 May 1981, p. 8: 'Uluslararası Atatürk Sempozyumu başladı' (the paper's Turkish rendering of his paper 'Karizmatik (etkileyici) Kişilikler Yönünden Yoksul Bir Çağ, Napolyon'dan Atatürk'e'; name printed 'Mattei Doğan').

⚠️ Symposium organised by Türkiye İş Bankası. Date is the day before publication. Read on the page image (Milliyet's subscriber archive).
