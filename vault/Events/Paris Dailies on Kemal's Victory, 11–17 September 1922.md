# Paris Dailies on Kemal's Victory, 11–17 September 1922

A second pass over the Smyrna fortnight, complementing [[Paris Reads Smyrna and the Straits, September 1922]] (which reads 13–17 September from
*L'Action française*, *L'Humanité*, *Le Figaro*, *Le Temps*). This one looks at 11, 12, 14, 15 and 17 September and keeps only fragments in which the paper
itself, not a dispatch, judges Kemal. Most of the fragments are Havas or Times relays ("Vive Kemal pacha", the "réception triomphale" at Smyrna). Every quotation below is a Gallica `ContentSearch` fragment copied exactly as the OCR gives it (so "Moustapha", "Mustapha", "Kernal" for Kemal and other OCR slips are kept, marked [OCR] where they matter). Fragments are short and are cut with "(...)"; "PAG_n" is Gallica's view number (the n-th image of the issue, not always the printed page). Only what a fragment shows is claimed.

**Le Temps**, 11 September, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k2448592)), answers the London *Evening Standard*: *"On pourrait répondre [aux] insinuations de l'Evening Standard que Moustapha Kemal et ses officiers d'état-major ne sont pas des « paysans incultes »"*, and cites General Townshend (back from Angora) on "le remarquable effort industriel d'Angora". The semi-official daily is defending Kemal's competence against the British press, four days before it shifts to warning notes on the Straits. On 15 September, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k2448634)), a political paragraph says *"Moustapha Kemal se rend parfaitement compte que les Français, les Anglais et les autres alliés s'opposeraient ensemble à une tentative quelconque [sur] Constantinople"* — a cool analyst's reading of him as a realist (whether the sentence is the paper's or a correspondent's is not shown).

**L'Humanité**, 11 September, view 3 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k400518q)): *"Kemal peut cependant '.atteindre l'impérialisme britannique en Asie"* [OCR], by a rapprochement with the Arabs of Mesopotamia and Palestine or by working Afghanistan — the Communist daily reads the victory as a blow at British imperialism, with no word of admiration for the man.

**Le Populaire** (Socialist), 11 September, view 1 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k8181715)), is wary: *"Que Fehrid bey et Mustapha Kernal écoulent certains conseils qui leur viennent de Moscou, un nouveau conflit s'ensuivra dans les Balkans"* [OCR "Kernal", "écoulent" for "écoutent"], with all central Europe drawn in. View 3 of the same issue adds that "Kemal pacha" was encouraged by the Sublime Porte itself, "à l'exception de certains éléments pro-britanniques amis de Damad Ferid pacha" — the sentence is cut there.

**L'Action française**, 17 September, view 3 ([issue](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k7613473)): a short fragment, its lead-in cut: *"Moyistapha Kémal se méfie et il a rai son"* [OCR: "Moustapha Kémal se méfie et il a raison"] — Kemal is wary, and right to be. What he is wary of, and whether the line is the paper's own view, the fragment does not show. (View 1 of the same issue: "Constantinople par les forces de Mustapha Kemal, constituerait un événement désastreux", a dispatch.) The 15 September issue also relays unnamed papers that say Kemal's victory "est d'un exemple pernicieux" and teaches the Germans that a treaty can be shed by force; the fragment does not show whose view that is, so it is not recorded.

**Dry.** *La Croix* 14 September, view 1 has a headline fragment "Mustapha Kemal pacha ... atrocités grecque[s]" and view 2 the Havas line that Kemal will hold responsible the Greeks who committed atrocities; no editorial voice. *Le Figaro* and *La Croix*: dispatches only. *Le Temps* 17 September, view 3 carries an interview in which someone calls Kemal "un homme toujours bien équilibré" — the speaker's name is not in the fragment.
