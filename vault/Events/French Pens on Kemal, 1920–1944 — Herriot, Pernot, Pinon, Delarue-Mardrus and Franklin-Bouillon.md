# French Pens on Kemal, 1920–1944 — Herriot, Pernot, Pinon, Delarue-Mardrus and Franklin-Bouillon

Five French voices on Kemal found by archive.org full-text search (the Herriot lecture in the Turkish Press Directorate's review, with the open djvu read) and by phrase search of French Wikisource (the two Revue des Deux Mondes pieces and Delarue-Mardrus), plus Franklin-Bouillon's statement of September 1922 as reported by the New York Times. Related: Franklin Bouillon, and the earlier [[Western Statesmen and Churchmen on Atatürk in Their Own Words]].

## Édouard Herriot — 1934

*After a 1933 visit, calls Kemal a remarkable statesman who believes in science and reason, a disciple perhaps unconscious of Comte and Descartes.*

> Le Ghazi croit à la science, à la raison. Je vous l'ai dit : je me le représente comme un disciple, inconscient peut-être, d'Auguste Comte et de Descartes.
> (translation: The Ghazi believes in science, in reason. I told you: I picture him as a disciple, unconscious perhaps, of Auguste Comte and of Descartes.)

[[Édouard Herriot]]. Published in the Turkish Press Directorate's own French-language propaganda review, so the venue is Kemalist; the text is Herriot's lecture, signed. Issue date not printed in the OCR (the review began in 1934), hence year-only. Herriot also calls him 'ce remarquable homme d'État' (p. 27) and says he ruled 'non par des gestes de dictature'. OCR reads 'lai' for 'l'ai' (corrected in the quote). Cited as: Herriot, 'De la vieille à la nouvelle Turquie', La Turquie kémaliste no. 1 (1934), pp. 15–28, quote on p. 27 (a lecture to an audience, 'Mesdames et Messieurs').

## Maurice Pernot — 1922

*Portrays the Angora leader as adored by his soldiers and esteemed by generals, a national hero, while noting opponents' charges of dictatorship.*

> Moustapha Kemal, adoré par les soldats, est estimé par les chefs, même par ceux dont la valeur technique est peut-être supérieure à la sienne.
> (translation: Moustapha Kemal, adored by the soldiers, is esteemed by the generals, even by those whose technical ability is perhaps superior to his own.)

[[Maurice Pernot]]. Wikisource transcription; the issue date within 1922 is not printed in the transcription (tome 7 = early 1922), so year-only. Pernot also calls him 'le jeune dictateur' and reports opponents' charge of 'allures de dictateur'; the 'adoré par les soldats' sentence is Pernot's own judgement, not a quoted source. Mixed: sympathetic portrait of a moderate against Unionist hard-liners, framed for French policy. Cited as: Pernot, 'La Question turque' II, 'Angora', Revue des Deux Mondes, 7e période, tome 7 (1922), pp. 549–579, quote on p. 567 (Wikisource page numbers).

## René Pinon — 1920

*Treats Kemal as the continuator and accomplice of Enver, Talaat and Djemal, and the real head of a Moscow–Constantinople–Berlin plot against the Allies.*

> Mustapha Kemal est le continuateur et le complice des Enver, des Talaat et des Djemal.
> (translation: Mustapha Kemal is the continuator and accomplice of the Envers, Talaats and Djemals.)

[[René Pinon]]. Wikisource transcription; the piece is dated by the tome (March–April 1920, so 1920), the exact issue is not printed. Written before the Ankara Assembly's victories; Pinon's reading is the 'Unionists in disguise' thesis of the Allied press in 1920, which he states as fact. Cited as: Pinon, 'L'Offensive de l'Asie', Revue des Deux Mondes, 6e période, tome 56 (1920), pp. 799–815, quote on p. 808 (the 'masque d'un insurgé' line on p. 813).

## Lucie Delarue-Mardrus — 1944

*Recalls Kemal's unveiling campaign as a wave 'ordered' from above; some Turkish women preferred suicide to the new era. She mocks his cap decree elsewhere.*

> au moment de la vague de libération ordonnée par Kémal-Pacha, certaines dames turques ont préféré se suicider qu'entrer dans cette ère nouvelle qui les épouvantait.
> (translation: at the moment of the wave of liberation ordered by Kemal Pasha, some Turkish ladies preferred to kill themselves rather than enter this new era that terrified them.)

[[Lucie Delarue-Mardrus]]. Wikisource transcription (page numbers are the book's). A passing, ironic view: she says 'on ne nous l'a pas dit' (the suicide claim is hearsay) and in the Constantinople chapter she pleaded for the old customs. Posthumous (1944) recollection of a pre-1914 Orient. She also writes that 'avant Kémal Pacha' any passer-by could scold an unveiled woman (p. 31). Cited as: Delarue-Mardrus, El Arab, l'Orient que j'ai connu (Lugdunum, 1944), ch. 'Tunis', p. 30; also ch. 'Constantinople', p. 137: 'S'ils avaient pu prévoir les futures casquettes cyclistes ordonnées par Kémal-pacha !'.

## Henry Franklin-Bouillon — 1922-09-24

*Before leaving to meet Kemal, says he has confidence in Kemal's discretion and wisdom, from their telegrams and his knowledge of the man.*

> Our exchange of telegrams. our reciprocal friendship and my knowledge of the man leads me to hope
> (translation: (already English: the New York Times's rendering of his French remarks))

Franklin Bouillon. Single source (J): read only as the archive snippet returned by search, the article itself is paywalled; the quotation is the NYT's English rendering of a French statement, and the OCR full stop after 'telegrams' is kept as printed in the snippet. Wire/press report of a statement, not his own text. Diplomatic purpose (selling Allied terms to Kemal) colours it; he adds that dangers remain. Cited as: 'Paris Acclaims Peace in Near East; Will Send Franklin-Bouillon by Fast Cruiser to Advise the Turks', New York Times, 25 September 1922 (Paris dispatch of 24 Sept).
