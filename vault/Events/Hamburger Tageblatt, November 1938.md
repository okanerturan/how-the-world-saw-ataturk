# Hamburger Tageblatt, November 1938

The German card, standing on a curated primary document: **German History in Documents and
Images (GHDI)** presents two *Hamburger Tageblatt* texts from two issues — the obituary of 10 November 1938 (p. 2,
"Vom Rebell zum Vater der Türken"), admiring the national revitalization (military reorganization,
educational reform, the veil, the Latin alphabet), **plus, a month later (10 December 1938, pp. 1–2), an
essay attributed to Atatürk himself, "The Leader and His People"** (the two are not printed together) — the leader who serves his own interest is "only a second-rate man" — printed by a
Nazi paper as leadership doctrine.

**Read on the page (18 Nov 2026).** The obituary is in the Deutsches Zeitungsportal (Hamburger Tageblatt, 10 Nov 1938, p. 2; image from SUB Hamburg). Headline: "Vom Rebell zum „Vater der Türken"", over "Kamal Atatürk heute gestorben — Neuwahl des Präsidenten am Freitag". After the Ankara report: "Der Tod Kamal Atatürks nimmt der Türkei den Wegbereiter und Führer, dem allein sie ihren bedeutenden Aufstieg nach dem Weltkriege zu verdanken hat." Near the end: "Ein Wille aus Granit und eine unerschöpfliche Arbeitskraft waren das Fundament dieses Wirkens." The same page reports the death of the diplomat vom Rath in Paris and bans Jews from owning weapons, the day after the November pogrom.

GHDI's editorial frame matches Ihrig's thesis exactly: Nazi Germany read Atatürk's "new national
consciousness" as a model of the leadership it valued in itself. This card gives the Adolf Hitler node its documentary floor — a real Nazi-era newspaper page, translated and curated by
a scholarly project, doing precisely what Ihrig says the German press did. (The "eclipsed
Kristallnacht" formulation remains a page-level Ihrig check — this card neither proves nor needs
it.)
