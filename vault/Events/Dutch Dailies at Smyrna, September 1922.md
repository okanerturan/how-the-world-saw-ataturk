# Dutch Dailies at Smyrna, September 1922

The Dutch dailies of September 1922 handled Smyrna as wire news. Two items from the KB index show how Kemal appeared
in them; neither is an editorial, and the card is deliberately short. Context: Burning of Smyrna; Paris
on the same days: [[Paris Reads Smyrna and the Straits, September 1922]].

**De Grondwet**, 18 September 1922 ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMGARO01:000172985:mpeg21:a0018)), "Telegrammen. De brand-ramp van Smyrna": a Constantinople wire collecting
British refugees' accounts (the fire set on purpose; Turkish soldiers ringing survivors inside the burning area; the Greeks' own retreat marked by arson). The wire's summary: *"De algemeene indruk, door de verhalen alhier gewekt is, dat Moestafa Kemal de schoonste gelegenheid van zijn leven heeft laten voorbijgaan"* — had he held his troops in check he would have won a moral victory greater than over the Greek army. The view is the unnamed correspondent's and the newspaper carries it; the same page prints the Turkish claim that arrested persons confessed to the arson.

**De Volkskrant** (Catholic), 22 September ([article](https://resolver.kb.nl/resolve?urn=MMKB12:000132218:mpeg21:a00020)): the Catholic angle of the Smyrna news is church property (the archbishop's residence destroyed; the cathedral and other Catholic churches spared) and Christians marched inland. It also reports *"Kemal heeft beloofd dat de banken van die maatschappij door hem zullen worden beschermd"* (of Standard Oil) and the hope that the Allies' advice will bring Kemal to respect the neutral zone. No view of Kemal is stated; recorded as witness. On 16 September the same paper printed Kemal's reported peace terms and the fire's toll (60,000 Armenians and Greeks homeless, 1,000+ dead).

**De Standaard** (Anti-Revolutionary), 14–15 September, runs "Britsche persstemmen" and a "De les van Smyrna" passage that draws only a lesson about force and treaties; on 26 September a French commissioner presses "Kemal" to evacuate the neutral zone. No view of him is stated.

Dutch quotations are copied exactly as the KB/Delpher OCR gives them (the OCR of 1920s newspaper print is noisy; I quote only runs that read cleanly). Confessional labels (Catholic, Protestant, social-democratic) come from the paper's own KB subtitle where it has one and otherwise from general knowledge of the Dutch press, and are marked as such.
