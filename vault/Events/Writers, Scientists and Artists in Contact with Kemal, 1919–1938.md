# Writers, Scientists and Artists in Contact with Kemal, 1919–1938

Contemporary records for world-famous writers, scientists and artists: their own words about Kemal, or a documented first-hand contact with him (a letter, a meeting, a substantive exchange), under the census's first-hand contact rule (5 Nov 2026).

## T. E. Lawrence, 1919-09-15
*British officer and Foreign Office adviser ('Lawrence of Arabia')* — conflicted, report, evidence P.

Judged Mustafa Kemal far abler than Enver but without his magnetism, and too hesitant to act except in desperate or very favourable circumstances.

> Of course Mustafa Kemal is much the more able of the two, but lacks Enver's personal magnetism.

⚠️ Secret memorandum to the Foreign Office of 15 Sept 1919 (No. 115 in Garnett, The Letters of T. E. Lawrence, London: Cape 1938, pp. 288–291; quote p. 291, note G); an intelligence appraisal, not a public statement; no meeting is documented (his 1926 claim of 1918 talks, in a Childs FO minute, is doubted by Sonyel 1988).

## Clair Price, 1923
*American journalist (Current History), a month in Angora* — admiring, book, evidence P.

Interviewed Kemal at the Assembly in Angora; saw a Westerner born under Abdul Hamid's absolutism, the old Ottoman officer type at its best.

> He is a great Turk and as a man among men he towers head and shoulders above the type of man which our Western democracies have sometimes projected into political life.

⚠️ The Rebirth of Turkey (New York: Thomas Seltzer, 1923), ch. I 'Mustapha Kemal Pasha, the Man', pp. 6–7; interview date not given (1922, during his month in Angora). The vault card's claim that the book shifted US policy is not tested.

## Grace Ellison, 1923
*English journalist and author, in Angora winter 1922–23* — admiring, book, evidence P.

Met and lunched with Mustapha Kemal in Angora as Lausanne opened; called him the greatest man in Turkey, past-master as statesman, soldier and orator.

> It is not necessary to see M. Kemal Pasha to realise that he is the greatest man in Turkey to-day, quite apart from his actual achievements.

⚠️ An Englishwoman in Angora (London: Hutchinson, 1923), ch. XVIII 'The Ghazi Mustapha Kemal Pasha — The Greatest Man in Turkey To-day', p. 159 (OCR 'necessarv' corrected); a separate interview chapter follows (p. 174). The vault card's claims that she defied her government and shifted British opinion are not tested here.

## Aga Khan III, 1923-11
*Imam of the Nizari Ismailis (letter co-signed with Syed Ameer Ali)* — conflicted, letter, evidence P.

Writing to Prime Minister İsmet as 'consistent friends of new Turkey', urged keeping the Caliphate, sure President Mustafa Kemal could not calmly accept Islam's disintegration.

> a contingency which, we are sure, neither the Grand National Assembly nor His Excellency the President, Ghazi Mustafa Kemal Pasha, can view with equanimity

Mediated by: Indian Quarterly Register 1924 vol. I (Calcutta, H. N. Mitra), 'The Khilafat Movement', pp. 85–86, reprinting the letter.

⚠️ Addressed to Prime Minister İsmet, not to Kemal; letter date not printed in the reprint (published in the Istanbul papers a week after dispatch, a week after dispatch). The register quotes İsmet's remarks to an interviewer objecting to its publication; the Independence Tribunal prosecution of the editors who printed it followed. The Istanbul editors who printed it were soon acquitted.

## Le Corbusier, 1925
*Swiss-French architect and writer* — conflicted, book, evidence P.

Saw Ankara and the Mustapha-Kemal monument as the West's triumph: one more centuries-old civilisation going to ruin, no more whitewash in Turkey.

> And already today we have Ankara, and the monument to Mustapha-Kemal! Events move fast. The die is cast: one more centuries-old civilisation goes to ruin. [translation: James Dunnett, The Decorative Art of Today, London: Architectural Press 1987]

Mediated by: Dunnett's English translation (1987) of L'Art décoratif d'aujourd'hui (Paris: Crès 1925), read as archive.org search-inside snippets.

⚠️ Snippet only (archive.org decorativeartoft0000leco, lending-only; page not shown; the sentence is ); the French original was not reached. Context in the snippet: the Young Turks had come, the Bazaar was changing its signs, 'the West was triumphing'. His tone mixes nostalgia for the folk Orient with acceptance. The 1933 letter applying for the Istanbul plan is known only from his 1948 remark to Şemsa Demiren and is not staged. The passage reads as a lament for the passing of the old culture; 'conflicted' rests on that reading. French original and page not reached.

## Ferhat Abbas, 1931
*Algerian pharmacist and reformist writer; later first president of the Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic (GPRA)* — admiring, book, evidence S.

In Le Jeune Algérien, welcoming the Muslim world's awakening, admired Mustafa Kemal alongside Abduh and Ibn Saud; wrote under the pen name 'Kémal Abencérages'.

> None

Mediated by: Guy Pervillé, 'Le sentiment national des étudiants algériens de culture française de 1912 à 1962', Relations internationales no. 2 (Nov 1974), pp. 233–259 (reposted 2 Jul 2007) <- Abbas, Le Jeune Algérien (1931).

⚠️ Secondary paraphrase only: 'Il admire Mohammed Abdou, Ibn Séoud et Mustapha Kémal, en dépit de leurs divergences'. Abbas's own words were not read: Le Jeune Algérien was not found on archive.org, and Gallica was not reached. Pervillé does not date the pen name 'Kémal Abencérages', and he notes that by 1936 Abbas denied that an Algerian nation existed. Abbas never met Kemal.

## Johann von Leers, 1933
*Nazi propagandist and writer* — instrumental, book, evidence P.

Argued National Socialism made Germans immune to communism, just as the Ghazi's work had made modern Turkey immune to it.

> Die wirkliche Neugestaltung, wie der Nationalsozialismus sie verwirklicht, macht das deutsche Volk innerlich immun gegen weitere Beeinflussung durch die kommunistische Idee, wie die moderne Türkei durch das Werk des Ghazi Mustapha Kemal Pascha gegen den Kommunismus immun geworden ist. [translation: The real reshaping that National Socialism is carrying out makes the German people inwardly immune to further influence by the communist idea, as modern Turkey has become immune to communism through the work of the Ghazi Mustapha Kemal Pasha.]

⚠️ Deutschlands Stellung in der Welt (Leipzig: Quelle & Meyer, 1933, series 'Das Dritte Reich'), p. 85; scan hosted by a revisionist uploader on archive.org, text checked in its OCR. The 1932 Hitler biography (Ihrig's lead) is not online; also untested: his 1934 article 'Mustafa Kemal Pascha', Zeitschrift für Politik 24, pp. 4–27, and his 1934 novel Kanonen über der Steppe.

## Albert Einstein, 1933-09-17
*Physicist; honorary president of the OSE (Union for the Protection of the Health of Jews)* — witness, letter, evidence P+S.

Wrote to Prime Minister İnönü, not Atatürk, asking that forty scholars barred in Germany work in Turkey unpaid for a year; the ministry refused.

> These scientists are willing to work for a year without any remuneration in any of your institutions according to the orders of your Government.

Mediated by: İnönü Foundation transcription ← Prime Ministry archive original; Reisman (HNN) for the ministry annotations.

⚠️ Counted under the first-hand contact rule for major figures (Okan, 5 Nov 2026). The letter (17 Sept 1933, as honorary president of the OSE) is addressed to Prime Minister İsmet İnönü, not to Atatürk, and names neither him nor his reforms. İnönü sent it to the Education Ministry on 9 Oct 1933; marginal notes rejected it ('incompatible…', 'impossible to accept…') — Reisman attributes them to minister Reşit Galip (Galip may already have left office in Aug 1933). Neither Einstein archive (Princeton, Hebrew University) had seen the letter; one archivist suggested he signed an OSE-drafted text. Turkey did take in many German émigré scholars in 1933 through other channels.

## T. E. Lawrence, 1934-11-28
*British writer and former officer ('Lawrence of Arabia')* — admiring, letter, evidence P.

Told Liddell Hart that Mustapha Kemal was a great patriot, anti-foreign from 1913, his nationalism founded to combat Enver's pro-German tendency.

> Mustapha Kemal was a great patriot, and anti-foreign from 1913 onwards.

⚠️ Letter No. 544 to B. H. Liddell Hart, Bridlington, 28 Nov 1934, answering questions from the historian Edward Mead Earle (Garnett 1938, p. 831); two sentences in a short reply. The vault card's 'one of the greatest leaders of the era' line is not in this letter.

## B. R. Ambedkar, 1935-11-10
*Dalit leader, jurist; later chief architect of India's constitution* — admiring, quote, evidence S.

Told Nasik caste-Hindu reformers India needed a dictator like Kemal Pasha, who made Turkey powerful and reformed Muslim practice; no salvation without such a leader.

> I feel the greatest respect for Kemal Pasha, it is he that has made Turkey into a powerful nation.

Mediated by: Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches vol. 17 pt 1, pp. 237–238 ← The Times of India, 30 Nov 1935 ← statement by the Nasik Progressive Hindus' deputation.

⚠️ Reported speech from a three-hour interview at his residence on 10 Nov 1935, after the Yeola conversion speech; also 'India wants a dictator, a Kemal Pasha or a Mussolini.' The vault card's claim that he cited Turkey in the Hindu Code Bill debates is not borne out (BAWS vol. 14's two Turkey mentions are other members).

## Erich Auerbach, 1937-01-03
*German Romance philologist, professor at Istanbul University (author of Mimesis)* — conflicted, letter, evidence P.

Called Atatürk a likeable, clever autocrat who truly made the country a state, but his result a fanatical anti-traditional nationalism destroying national character.

> The “grand chef” is a sympathetic autocrat, smart, grand, and imaginative, completely different from his European counterparts because he has actually himself turned this country into a state and because he is absolutely without eloquence. [translation: Elsky, Vialon and Stein, PMLA 2007]

Mediated by: We Refugees Archive edition ← Barck, Zeitschrift für Germanistik 9:6 (1988) pp. 691–692; English: PMLA 122:3 (2007) pp. 750–751.

⚠️ Private letter to Walter Benjamin, Istanbul-Bebek, 3 Jan 1937. German original: 'Der grand chef ist ein sympathischer Autokrat, klug, großzügig und witzig, vollkommen verschieden von seinen europäischen Kollegen ... und das Resultat ist ein fanatischer antitraditioneller Nationalismus.' The PMLA translation renders 'witzig' as 'imaginative' and 'phrasenlos' as 'without eloquence'. Not a meeting: Auerbach was an employee of Kemal's university reform, not a personal contact.
