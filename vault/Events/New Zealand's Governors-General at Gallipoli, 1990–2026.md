# New Zealand's Governors-General at Gallipoli, 1990–2026

Every few years New Zealand's Governor-General goes to Gallipoli for Anzac Day, and Atatürk is in almost every speech. Usually it is his 1934 message to the Anzac mothers, read aloud. Some of them also say what they think of him. The speeches are published on gg.govt.nz, which answers scripts with a bot check; they were read in a browser on 18 Nov 2026. A search of the site for "Ataturk" and "Atatürk" returned 22 pages.

- **Sir Paul Reeves, 25 April 1990**, at the Çanakkale Memorial on the 75th anniversary: the Allied generals, had they studied Mustafa Kemal's record, "might have had a more realistic appreciation of the difficulties that lay ahead"; Atatürk's motto "Peace at home and peace in the world" is "our duty" to make real. `paul-reeves-1990-04-25`
- **Sir Anand Satyanand, 27 April 2009**, at the state dinner in Çankaya: Atatürk is "the father of New Zealand's relationship with Turkey" and "a man of vision" whose outstretched hand "secured his place in the hearts of New Zealanders". `anand-satyanand-2009-04-27`
- **Sir Jerry Mateparae, 29 April 2014**, at the 150th commemoration of Gate Pā, a battle of the New Zealand Wars: he takes "the spirit of reconciliation expressed by Mustafa Kemal Ataturk" home and turns the 1934 words into a message to the families of the Māori and British dead, in English and then Māori. Four days earlier, at Anzac Cove, he had called Atatürk "a leader in the art of peace and the art of nation-building as much he was in the art of war". `jerry-mateparae-2014-04-29`
- **Dame Cindy Kiro, 25 April 2026**, at Chunuk Bair: "that great soldier and visionary statesman", whose statue stands a few metres from the New Zealand Memorial. `cindy-kiro-2026-04-25`

Not entered: speeches that only read the 1934 message, or name him in passing (Satyanand at Anzac Cove and at the international service, 2009; Hardie Boys to the Wellington College Old Boys, 1998; the Chunuk Bair services).

Compare the press of the 1920s and 1930s in [[New Zealand Editors on Kemal, September 1922 – 1923]] and [[New Zealand Editors on the Dictator, 1929–1938]].
