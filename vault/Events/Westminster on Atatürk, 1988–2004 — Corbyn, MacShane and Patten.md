# Westminster on Atatürk, 1988–2004 — Corbyn, MacShane and Patten

The same search for the later decades. Atatürk is now a standard for a secular Turkey: Patten and MacShane invoke him in the EU-accession years; Corbyn blames his rise for the failure of the Sèvres promise to the Kurds (sensitive thread, speaker's framing). Earlier records: [[Hansard After Atatürk, 1938–1939]].

## Denis MacShane (2004-06-23)

> the Turkish nation will embark on its second great modernisation after that of Ataturk.

Turkey (EU Accession), HC Deb 23 Jun 2004, c1425. Same phrase in Oral Answers, 15 Jun 2004 ('the second great modernisation of Turkey, after the Ataturk modernisation'). Used for the EU accession case.

## John Patten (Lord Patten) (2004-12-15)

> There is also the reassurance that we all seek that the legacy of Kemal Atatürk—Turkey as a secular society—is going to be preserved inside or outside of the European Union and that there is no possibility of a theocratic state emerging in Turkey.

Turkey and the EU, HL Deb 15 Dec 2004, cc1355-57. Column given as the span of Patten's speech (his speech follows Lord Rea's, ends before Lord Dahrendorf's, per the page's citation lines). Also Patten's written question 23 Oct 2006 asks what representations were made over the trial of Ipek Calislar for 'allegedly insulting Kemal Atatürk': a procedural mention, not recorded.

## Jeremy Corbyn (1988-03-25)

> It was not until 1920 and the treaty of Sevres that the Kurdish nation was recognised, but the treaty was never fully carried out or established because of the rise of Kemal Ataturk in Turkey, who ensured that it was never brought to fruition.

Adjournment (Easter and Monday 2nd May), HC Deb 25 Mar 1988, c653. Sensitive thread (Kurds): Corbyn's framing, in an adjournment speech on the Halabja-era Kurdish plight; the vault does not adopt it. Hostility is mild and implicit (Kemal as the obstacle to Kurdish independence). He repeats it on 6 Jun 1991 (Kurdish People debate, c487: 'Mustafa Kemal led the development of the modern state of Turkey, and the British Government decided to back modern Turkey rather than the aspirations of the Kurdish people'), recorded here once.
