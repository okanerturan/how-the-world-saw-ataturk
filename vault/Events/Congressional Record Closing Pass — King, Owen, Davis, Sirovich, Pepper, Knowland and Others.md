# Congressional Record Closing Pass — King, Owen, Davis, Sirovich, Pepper, Knowland and Others

Twenty-seven statements by members of the US Congress, 1921–2020, found in the last pass through the *Congressional Record* on GovInfo (round 24). They come from three places that rounds 22–23 had not read: (1) the **daily edition** 1994–2026 (all 86 granules that the GovInfo web search returns for "Ataturk", "Atatürk", "Kemal Pasha", "Mustafa Kemal", "Kemal Ataturk"), (2) the **bound edition** chamber granules of 1920–1992 that the same search lists but which were not fetched earlier (48 sittings, among them the Senate debates of 1921–23 on the Pontic Greeks and the Lausanne treaty), and (3) **43 Appendix (Extensions of Remarks) volumes** of 1922–1967 and **83 granules** that mention only "Kemal". The bound 1993 volume has no hit for any spelling and six sampled 1993 granules (the Armenian commemoration sittings of 21 April 1993) were read to confirm it.

The rule is as before: only the member's own words count. Articles, letters, resolutions and reports that a member had printed are excluded (Harbord's report, Sherrill's radio address, John Rufi's letter, Ahmet Ertegun's speech, Brademas's lecture and McGhee's speech are noted as leads in the candidates file). **Evidence P** (the official record). Scans of the bound edition are two-column OCR, so page numbers are read from running heads and marked "~" where uncertain. Several names are misspelt by the OCR ("Ats.turk", "Attaturk", "Atattirk"), which is why rounds 22–23 missed Pepper (1942) and Javits (1978).

The oldest voices are the most striking. **Senator William H. King** of Utah (1921 and 1923) asks for outside force to restrain "the bloody hands of Mustapha Kemal" and calls Kemal's appeal to America "a most adroit and intriguing document", while **Senator Robert L. Owen** (26 January 1923, during the Lausanne conference) presents the same address as the work of an educated man who "is entitled to a hearing". Admiring and mixed voices follow: **James J. Davis** (1933, the narcotics ban), **William Sirovich** (1934), **Claude Pepper** (1942: the Turks "have made greater strides forward than any other people"), **William Knowland** (1953), and in the daily edition Wexler, Schmidt, Foxx, Cohen and Steve King. Fifteen staged statements were dropped at the independent check: the Berger 1927 passage is Rep. Upshaw's, Dreier 2007 is Diaz-Balart's, Culkin 1941 and Dorn 1956 are inserted texts, and Kefauver, Javits, Celler, Gosar, Maloney, Bolton, Hand, Pittman, Connolly, Wilson and Powell 1956 state no view of the man or are borrowed text. King's 1921 resolution concerns the Pontic Greeks: sensitive thread, the speaker's framing.

## William H. King — 1921-12-22

Presenting his Pontic Greeks resolution, says only outside force can restrain 'the bloody hands of Mustapha Kemal'.

> Unless some steps shall be taken by forces outside of Turkey to restrain the bloody hands of Mustapha Kemal, there would seem to be no hope of salvation for these unfortunate people, whose only offense is that they are Christians

**Source.** Senate, 22 December 1921, Congressional Record vol. 62 (bound), p. ~691 (granule CRECB-1922-pt1-v62-16-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1922-pt1-v62/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1922-pt1-v62-16-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** His own words in the Senate; the resolutions, the Ecumenical Patriarchate statements ('tyrant of Asia Minor') and the mass-meeting texts he has printed with them are not counted. Sensitive thread (Pontic Greeks, 1919-23): the speech is King's advocacy and his framing, not the vault's. He repeats the charge on 2 Feb 1922 (p. ~2048). Page from the OCR running heads; the scan is two-column.

## Robert L. Owen — 1923-01-26

Introduces Kemal's address to America as 'remarkable': an educated man, speaking for Turks who, like the Founders, want freedom from outside domination.

> here is a man who speaks the American language, though he be a Turk; a man who is highly educated; one who is informed with regard to the history of the nations of the world
>
> He is entitled to a hearing.

**Source.** Senate, 26 January 1923, 'Mustapha Kemal — Conditions in Near East', Congressional Record vol. 64 (bound), p. ~2487 (granule CRECB-1923-pt3-v64-4-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1923-pt3-v64/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1923-pt3-v64-4-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** Owen's own introduction (about 200 words); the Herald interview and the address he has printed ('Kemal demands absolute independence for Turkey', including the 'George Washington' remark) are Kemal's and are not counted. Senator King answers him in the same sitting (see his record). Cited alongside the Lausanne conference, a month into its first session.

## William H. King — 1923-01-26

Answering Owen, calls Kemal Pasha's appeal to America 'a most adroit and intriguing document' meant to soften criticism of Kemalist treatment of Christians.

> The appeal by Kemal Pasha, to which the Senator refers, is a most adroit and intriguing document.
>
> the barbarities perpetrated by the Kemalists and the Turks upon Christian minorities

**Source.** Senate, 26 January 1923, Congressional Record vol. 64 (bound), p. ~2488 (granule CRECB-1923-pt3-v64-4-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1923-pt3-v64/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1923-pt3-v64-4-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** His own words in reply to Owen; he goes on to review the Armenian case. Sensitive thread (Armenians, Greeks): the speaker's framing. Two-column OCR; the second fragment is taken from the next lines of the same speech and joined with an ellipsis.

## James J. Davis — 1933-01-03

Hails Kemal's Christmas Day 1932 narcotics ban as a 'magnificent Christmas gift' making Turkey's President an outstanding anti-narcotics leader.

> The world, and especially America, received this magnificent Christmas gift in the form of a ban on that day on the narcotic trade by President Mustapha Kemal, of Turkey.
>
> This makes the President of Turkey one of the outstanding leaders in the international antinarcotic war.

**Source.** Senate, 3 January 1933, Congressional Record vol. 76 (bound), p. ~1195 (granule CRECB-1933-pt2-v76-1-1); the same words are quoted as his in Sherrill's address printed by him on 24 March 1934 (vol. 78, p. ~5299) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1933-pt2-v76/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1933-pt2-v76-1-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** Davis's own paragraph beside the Washington Star and Washington Herald cuttings that he has printed (not counted: 'Kemal places ban on narcotic trade'). In the 24 March 1934 sitting he introduces Ambassador Sherrill's radio address on Kemal's narcotics crusade, saying 'the gigantic strides Mustafa Kemal has made' in transforming his nation; that sentence is a second statement of the same view. A second Davis record already exists (22 April 1943). Two-column OCR.

## William I. Sirovich — 1934-04-24

Calls Kemal Pasha 'the dictator of Turkey' yet credits his reforms with creating a new nation 'dedicated to the principles of liberalism and progressivism'.

> Kemal Pasha, the dictator of Turkey. What a name to conjure with.
>
> a new nation with new ideals, dedicated to the principles of liberalism and progressivism

**Source.** House of Representatives, 24 April 1934, hour-long special order 'The Administration of President Roosevelt', Congressional Record vol. 78 (bound), pp. ~7233-7234 (granule CRECB-1934-pt7-v78-6-2) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1934-pt7-v78/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1934-pt7-v78-6-2.pdf>

**Caveat.** His own words, one passage in a survey of dictators (Mussolini, Hitler, Dollfuss, Stalin, Kemal) used to argue that the New Deal is none of them: Kemal is ranked as a dictator whose reforms nonetheless worked ('Turkey is growing and developing under the benign influence of occidental civilization'). The paragraph sits in two columns of OCR with hyphenation broken, so the quote is rebuilt from adjacent lines. Sirovich was a physician and playwright and chaired the House Patents Committee.

## Robert Wexler — 2001-10-31

On Republic Day, says Turkey became the secular, modern democracy Atatürk envisioned and calls him one of the twentieth century's greatest leaders.

> Over the past seventy-eight years, Turkey has emerged as the secular and modern democracy that Kemal Ataturk envisioned in 1923.
>
> one of the twentieth century's greatest leaders, Mustafa Kemal Ataturk

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks (House), 31 October 2001, 'Tribute to the Government of the Republic of Turkey', Congressional Record vol. 147, pp. E1969-E1970 (granule CREC-2001-10-31-pt1-PgE1969-3) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2001-10-31/html/CREC-2001-10-31-pt1-PgE1969-3.htm>

**Caveat.** His own Republic Day felicitation, written seven weeks after 11 September to praise Turkey's support. Ankara-friendly courtesy text as well as a view.

## Jean Schmidt — 2008-05-22

Marking 19 May, says Atatürk's vision of a pro-Western, secular, democratic state under the rule of law quickly became reality.

> Ataturk had a vision for Turkey and he set about reforming her. His vision of a pro-western, secular, and democratic state under the rule of law quickly became reality.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks (House), 22 May 2008, 'Commemorating May 19 as Ataturk, Youth and Sports Day in Turkey', Congressional Record vol. 154, p. E1094 (granule CREC-2008-05-23-pt1-PgE1094) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2008-05-23/html/CREC-2008-05-23-pt1-PgE1094.htm>

**Caveat.** Her own statement, which also quotes Kennedy and MacArthur on Atatürk (those quotations are not counted). Text closely parallels statements by Foxx, Cohen and Poe in other years, i.e. a template supplied by the Turkish-American lobby; a courtesy text as well as a view.

## Virginia Foxx — 2009-05-19

On his 128th birthday calls Atatürk 'a unique and inspirational figure', an impatient reformer, and urges Muslim leaders to take inspiration from his revolution.

> Ataturk was a unique and inspirational figure who laid the foundation for the Republic of Turkey.
>
> By any measure, Ataturk was an historic reformer.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks (House), 19 May 2009, '128th Anniversary of the Birth of Kemal Ataturk, Founder of Modern Turkey', Congressional Record vol. 155, p. E1196 (granule CREC-2009-05-19-pt1-PgE1196-3) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2009-05-19/html/CREC-2009-05-19-pt1-PgE1196-3.htm>

**Caveat.** Her own statement; near-identical in structure to Tom Lantos's 2006 statement (a staged record), which she follows. She also spoke on 15 Oct 2007 ('the visionary leader of modern Turkey', Armenian resolution debate), 22 Apr 2008 and 28 May 2010 (a birthday-party speech by a 16-year-old, not counted).

## Steve Cohen — 2012-11-13

Marking Atatürk's death anniversary, calls him the leader who made an agrarian society a modern, secular, Western-facing model: 'a great man'.

> Kemal Ataturk, the leader of modern Turkey who transformed that country from an agrarian society caught in the past to a modern, secular and forward looking western model.
>
> I join my friends in paying tribute to a great man and a great leader.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks (House), 13 November 2012, 'Remembering Kemal Ataturk', Congressional Record vol. 158, p. E1741 (granule CREC-2012-11-13-pt1-PgE1741-2) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2012-11-13/html/CREC-2012-11-13-pt1-PgE1741-2.htm>

**Caveat.** His own statement, which also quotes Kennedy and a Churchill line of unverified provenance on Atatürk (not counted). Cohen made similar statements on 19 May 2011, 17 May 2012, 17 Nov 2010 and 26 Oct 2023.

## Steve King — 2016-01-11

Praising Egypt's Sisi, recalls Atatürk as a bridge between East and West and hopes Sisi can become 'the Ataturk for the world'.

> When I look at the work that was done by Ataturk in Turkey, how he provided a bridge between the East and the West, and that has been drifting back a bit the other way under Erdogan
>
> could become the Ataturk for the world to bring about that bridge between the Muslim world and the Christian world and the West

**Source.** House of Representatives, 11 January 2016, 'Egypt Talking Points', Congressional Record vol. 162, pp. H264-H267 (granule CREC-2016-01-11-pt1-PgH264) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2016-01-11/html/CREC-2016-01-11-pt1-PgH264.htm>

**Caveat.** One-minute-style floor speech on Egypt; Atatürk is the template for what he wants Sisi to be. The second fragment follows after the page break to H267. A second King remark, 13 July 2017 (p. H5823), says Atatürk 'for 40 years, moved Turkey to the West' (not staged separately).

## William F. Knowland — 1953-10-30

Judging Egypt's Naguib after an Asian tour, says he has 'some of the zeal that Kemal Ataturk had' in modernising Turkey.

> I think they have some of the zeal that Kemal Ataturk had when he tried to modernize and clean up the situation in Turkey.
>
> It is too early to tell whether these people have or will develop the same success that Kemal Ataturk had in modernizing and bringing Turkey up to the very strong position she is in today

**Source.** Interview in U.S. News & World Report of 30 October 1953, printed at his request in the Senate Appendix on 7 January 1954, Congressional Record vol. 100 (bound), granule CRECB-1954-pt15-1 — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1954-pt15/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1954-pt15-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** His own answers in a magazine interview which he had printed in the Record (the questions and editor's note are not counted); Atatürk is a yardstick for Naguib, i.e. instrumental. OCR 'moderniz.e' repaired.

## Claude Pepper — 1942-04-23

Praises Atatürk's 'thorough and radical revolution' and says that under him and Inönü the Turks made 'greater strides forward than any other people'.

> Thus, under the leadership of Kemal Atatürk and Ismet Inonu, the Turks have made greater strides forward than any other people in the years following the World War.
>
> The astute Kemal Atatürk had perceived that the progressive Turkish people, who had been in constant contact with western civilization, were ripe for these innovations.

**Source.** Senate, 23 April 1942, 'Twenty-third Anniversary of the Turkish National Assembly', Congressional Record vol. 88 (bound), pp. 3670-3671 (granule CRECB-1942-pt3-19-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1942-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1942-pt3-19-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** His own speech (about 450 words) in the first year of America's war, when Turkey's neutrality mattered; he lists the reforms (religious courts abolished, Latin letters, Christian calendar) and warns of a German drive on Turkey. Not found in round 22 because the OCR prints the name as 'Atattirk'/'Atatiirk' (the GovInfo search hit the day on 'Kemal'). A third Pepper record after those of 17 April 1947 and 22 December 1950. The OCR prints 'Atattirk'; spelled Atatürk here.
