# Tampa's La Gaceta Calls Kemal a Dictator, 1930–1935

Tampa's *La Gaceta*, the Spanish-language daily of the cigar-makers of Ybor City and West Tampa, ran a front-page column,
**"Chungas y no chungas"**, that kept returning to Kemal as a type: the **Dictator**. The earlier card
[[Spanish-Language Newspapers Across the Americas Read Kemal, 1922–1935]] holds two *La Gaceta* items (14 Oct 1930,
21 Feb 1935). This card adds seven more from the 1930–35 run, read as page OCR. The column's habit is a litany of rulers (Italy, Poland, Turkey, Russia, Cuba, Venezuela) — each a name for the same fault; the column's own targets, to judge by these items, were as often
Machado's Cuba and Mussolini's Italy as Ankara. Kemal is almost never discussed on his own account; he is a
data point in an argument about dictatorship. Two lines are exceptions: the 2 Mar 1931 item (hanging dervishes —
"the republic imitates the extinct sultanate") and the 28 May 1932 characterisation (a hard-handed Dictator who is
also an "intelligent gentleman" and gets on with everyone from Mussolini to Stalin). The one admiring item (1 Dec 1934, the clergy-dress ban) is
in the same voice: tolerant, ironic, American-inflected.

- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1930-06-27, p. 1 (hostile). *Column lists 'Dictadura de Kemal Pacha en Turquía' with Mussolini, Pilsudski and Stalin and condemns all dictatorships as bad and undemocratic.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “Todas las dictaduras son malas, antidemocráticas” — English (vault translation): “All dictatorships are bad, undemocratic”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1930-06-27/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned front-page column; the list also names Primo de Rivera, Leguía, Ibáñez and Stalin, so Kemal is one entry in a general indictment._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1930-11-04, p. 1 (hostile). *Column on workers' rights: under dictatorship the law is the ruler, in Turkey 'Sr. Kemal Pasha', as in Italy, Poland, Russia.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “Las leyes para la Dictadura, sean buenas o malas, no existen.” — English (vault translation): “The laws for the Dictatorship, whether good or bad, do not exist.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1930-11-04/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned column; the quoted sentence is the general principle, the Kemal Pasha clause follows it two sentences later._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1931-03-02, p. 1 (hostile). *Column item says Kemal is hanging dervishes and secret-order members, keeping the old custom of hanging the government's enemies, as the sultanate did.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “La república en eso imita al extinguido sultanato.” — English (vault translation): “The republic in this imitates the extinct sultanate.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1931-03-02/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: OCR of the preceding lines is damaged ('Keraal Pasha', doubled words); the quoted sentence is clean. Unsigned._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1932-05-28, p. 1 (conflicted). *Column on Turkey's friendly ties with both Mussolini and Soviet Russia: Kemal a hard-handed Dictator who befriends all and rules Turks as he sees fit.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “Dictador de mano dura” — English (vault translation): “Hard-handed Dictator”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1932-05-28/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned. Same issue's p. 4 carries a British Gallipoli-history item calling Kemal a noble adversary (not a voice of the paper)._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1934-12-01, p. 1 (admiring). *Column on the ban on clerics' street dress, applied to all faiths: Kemal's measure follows American custom; 'Turkey progresses', Muslim priests 'tamed'.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “La medida de Kemal Pasha se inspira en las costumbres norteamericanas.” — English (vault translation): “Kemal Pasha's measure is inspired by North American customs.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1934-12-01/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned column item; tone mildly approving and ironic ('amansados')._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1935-01-24, p. 1 (hostile). *Answering Keyserling: the masses share 'sentimental life' yoked to the cart of a Mussolini, a Hitler or a Mustafá Kemal, an enemy of liberty.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “Un sentimentalismo de nuevo cuño, enemigo de la libertad y de la justicia.” — English (vault translation): “A sentimentality of a new mint, enemy of liberty and of justice.”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1935-01-24/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: Unsigned; the quoted sentence follows the passage naming Kemal and concerns the whole fascist-style set._
- **La Gaceta (Tampa)**, 1935-11-01, p. 1 (hostile). *On the Ethiopian crisis: the 'Turkish dictator' offers Britain help against Italy and asks for the Dodecanese; sarcastic about Kemal's 'excess population'.* Original (es, OCR-verbatim): “El dictador turco pide las islas del Dodecaneso, que ahora domina Italia.” — English (vault translation): “The Turkish dictator asks for the islands”. [page](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86002403/1935-11-01/ed-1/?sp=1). _Caveat: OCR of the island name is damaged ('Ad Dodecaneso'); only the clean clause is quoted. Unsigned._

**Not recorded:** *La Gaceta* wire items on Kemal (6 Dec 1934 voting rights for women, 4 Jan 1936 Spanish-Turkish treaty
"el patriota Kemal Pasha", Nov 1938 death notices, 16 Mar 1938 Bonardi's Franco-and-Kemal passage) — agency or reprinted copy.
