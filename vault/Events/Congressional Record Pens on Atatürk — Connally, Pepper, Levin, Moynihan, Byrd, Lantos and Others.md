# Congressional Record Pens on Atatürk — Connally, Pepper, Levin, Moynihan, Byrd, Lantos and Others

Fifteen statements by members of the US Congress, found by full-text search of the *Congressional Record* in the GovInfo API (collections CREC, 1994 on, and CRECB, the bound edition, 1873 on) and read in the GovInfo granule text (daily edition) or the PDF text layer (bound edition). Each is the member's own words on the floor or in the Extensions of Remarks; newspaper articles and letters that members had printed in the Record are not counted. **Evidence P** (the official record). The bound-edition scans are two-column OCR with some interleaving, so page numbers are approximate (from running heads) and quotes were rebuilt from the interleaved lines. The Armenian-question item (Levin) is the speakers' framing, not the vault's.

Names: [[Robert R. Reynolds]], [[Tom Connally]], [[James J. Davis]], [[Claude Pepper]], [[John P. Saylor]], [[Carl Levin]], [[Daniel Patrick Moynihan]], [[Tom Lantos]], [[Robert C. Byrd]], [[Louie Gohmert]].

## Robert R. Reynolds — 1939-04-13

In an aside on Mussolini's seizure of Albania, calls Atatürk 'that great man' who did more for Turkey than any other since its founding.

> that great man Ataturk who did more for Turkey than any other man since Turkey was established as a nation, happened to be born in Salonika.

**Source.** Senate, 13 April 1939, Congressional Record vol. 84 (bound), pp. ~4219-4222 (granule CRECB-1939-pt4-v84-12-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1939-pt4-v84/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1939-pt4-v84-12-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** Speaker identified from the transcript sequence ('Mr. REYNOLDS obtained the floor'); the two-column scan OCR interleaves with other speakers' lines, and the OCR reads 'sin~e' for 'since' (corrected). An aside, not the topic of the speech (Albania, Salonika, the European crisis).

## Tom Connally — 1942-10-29

Marks the Republic's 19th anniversary: 'the late great Kemal Ataturk', whose reforms rested on four foundations, none dependent on one man's life.

> The reforms and projects of Kemal Ataturk stood upon four square national foundations: peace, prosperity, honor, and strength. These foundations are not dependent upon one man's life or popularity for their execution.

**Source.** Senate, 29 October 1942, Congressional Record vol. 88 (bound), pp. ~8679-8680 (granule CRECB-1942-pt7-8-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1942-pt7/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1942-pt7-8-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** A formal felicitation to Turkey read with unanimous consent ('I feel certain that I am voicing the wishes of the United States Senate'), delivered in wartime when Turkey's neutrality was under Allied pressure; courtesy as well as view. Party, state and committee chairmanship are from the standard biographical record, not from the passage.

## James J. Davis — 1943-04-22

Marks the National Assembly's 24th anniversary: Atatürk's leadership brought a 'veritable social revolution' with no fascist badges or mass hysteria.

> Twenty-four years ago today, representatives from nearly every part of Turkey came to Ankara at the invitation of the late great Turkish leader, Kemal Ataturk, and laid the foundations of a new Turkey

**Source.** Senate, 22 April 1943, Congressional Record vol. 89 (bound), p. ~3693 (granule CRECB-1943-pt3-15-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1943-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1943-pt3-15-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** Heading 'Twenty-fourth anniversary of foundation of the Turkish National Assembly'; the same speech quotes Wendell Willkie's *One World* on Turkey (not counted). Senator's state from the standard record (only one Senator Davis served in 1943), not from the passage.

## Claude Pepper — 1947-04-17

Cites Atatürk's record to argue communism has no foothold in Turkey, and credits him with building a strong army and government.

> Anyone who has ever heard of Ataturk and his party and his government, which is now carried on by his chosen successor, President Inonu, knows that there is not any likelihood of communism getting a foothold in Turkey.
>
> Finally Ataturk took advantage of the strife which existed in Europe and developed a strong army and a strong government in Turkey

**Source.** Senate, 17 April 1947, Congressional Record vol. 93 (bound), pp. ~3593-3596 (granule CRECB-1947-pt3-13-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1947-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1947-pt3-13-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** Truman Doctrine debate: Pepper argues against the bill, so Atatürk is used as evidence for his case (no communist threat; the Dardanelles question). The OCR reads 'like·uhood' for 'likelihood' (corrected); the second quote is rebuilt from the interleaved two-column text layer (the source reads 'de- veloped', 'gov- ernment').

## Claude Pepper — 1950-12-22

Reporting on a trip, says the 'new Turkey oriented to the West' rests on the 'splendid edifice' Kemal Pasha built after the First World War.

> There is a new Turkey oriented to the West which has risen upon the splendid edifice established by Kemal Pasha after World War I.

**Source.** Senate, 22 December 1950, Congressional Record vol. 96 (bound), p. ~16974 (granule CRECB-1950-pt12-20-1) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1950-pt12/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1950-pt12-20-1.pdf>

**Caveat.** A one-sentence aside in a long report on Greece, Turkey and Israel; page from the OCR running heads (the scan interleaves columns). The speaker is read from the transcript sequence (Pepper in colloquy with Brewster).

## John P. Saylor — 1956-05-07

Offers Mustafa Kemal as a model for coal-state members of Congress: a 'fervent patriot' banished for his views who later became Ataturk and President.

> He was a fervent patriot who as a youth learned that his political views were not acceptable to incumbent government leaders; in fact, he was at one time banished to Damascus for publicly expressing his views.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks, 7 May 1956, Congressional Record vol. 102 (bound), p. ~7669 (granule CRECB-1956-pt6-6-3) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1956-pt6/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1956-pt6-6-3.pdf>

**Caveat.** From an address to the American Mining Congress (Cincinnati) printed in the Extensions of Remarks; an analogy for the coal industry's lobbying ('we took somewhat the same course of action that Mustafa Kemal followed'), so Atatürk is a vehicle for the speaker's point. Two-column OCR; quote rebuilt from the interleaved lines.

## Carl Levin — 1994-04-19

In an Armenian genocide commemoration, cites Atatürk as having recognised the crimes, quoting a 1926 interview he says Atatürk gave.

> Indeed, the founder of modern Turkey, Kemal Ataturk, recognized the crimes committed by the Ottoman Empire. As I've discussed on this floor before, in a 1926 interview Ataturk himself stated that those responsible "should have been made to account for the lives of millions of our Chistian subjects who were ruthlessly driven en masse from their homes and massacred."

**Source.** Senate, 19 April 1994, 'The Genocide of Armenians', Congressional Record vol. 140, no. 43, p. S38 (granule CREC-1994-04-19-pt1-PgS38) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-1994-04-19/html/CREC-1994-04-19-pt1-PgS38.htm>

**Caveat.** Sensitive thread (the Armenian genocide): the Senator's framing and his attribution, which the vault does not endorse or test here; the 1926 interview is his citation ('Chistian' is the Record's misprint). He uses Atatürk to separate present-day Turkey from the Ottoman crimes.

## Daniel Patrick Moynihan — 1999-10-18

Opens a Turkey free-trade bill with Atatürk as 'legendary': he built a western-oriented, secular state from the ruins of the Ottoman Empire.

> Turkey has played a singular role at the crossroads of East and West since 1923, when the legendary Mustafa Kemal "Ataturk" built a western-oriented, secular state out of the ashes of the collapsed 600-year old Ottoman Empire.

**Source.** Senate, 18 October 1999, introduction of S. 1746 (U.S.-Turkey Free Trade Agreement Act of 1999), Congressional Record vol. 145, no. 141, p. S12786 (granule CREC-1999-10-18-pt1-PgS12786) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-1999-10-18/html/CREC-1999-10-18-pt1-PgS12786.htm>

**Caveat.** Introductory framing of a trade bill (the bill's purpose was to open talks on a Turkey FTA), so the praise sets the case for the bill.

## Tom Lantos — 2005-09-27

Says he urged Musharraf to follow 'the great modernizer' Atatürk, and that Atatürk would admire Musharraf's path.

> I urged him to follow the example of the great modernizer Mustafa Kemal Ataturk, the founder of the modern, secular Republic of Turkey, a nation with which President Musharraf is intimately familiar. He has, in fact, blazed a trail that I believe Ataturk himself would admire.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks, 27 September 2005, 'Speech of Pakistani President Pervez Musharraf...', Congressional Record vol. 151, p. E1958 (granule CREC-2005-09-27-pt1-PgE1958) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2005-09-27/html/CREC-2005-09-27-pt1-PgE1958.htm>

**Caveat.** Lantos's own introduction to Musharraf's speech to the American Jewish Congress; Atatürk is the standard he held up to a Muslim head of state, which makes the praise partly diplomatic. Party, committee role and state are from the standard record, not from the passage.

## Tom Lantos — 2006-05-19

Honours Atatürk on his 125th birth anniversary as the revolutionary who showed Islam and modernity are compatible, an example 'for our own era'.

> I rise today, May 19, to commemorate the 125th anniversary of the birth of the founder of modern Turkey, Mustafa Kemal Ataturk, a post-World War I revolutionary leader who demonstrated that Islam and modernity are fully compatible. His example provides instruction and hope for our own era.

**Source.** Extensions of Remarks, 19 May 2006, '125th Anniversary of Birth of Kemal Ataturk, Founder of Modern Turkey', Congressional Record vol. 152, no. 63, p. E917 (granule CREC-2006-05-19-pt1-PgE917-2) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2006-05-19/html/CREC-2006-05-19-pt1-PgE917-2.htm>

**Caveat.** A full commemorative statement (alphabet reform, 'his leadership style', secularism) in the Extensions of Remarks; it also quotes Atatürk's own words. Delivered in the period of Lantos's active support for the Turkey-US relationship and while he also backed Armenian-genocide recognition, which this statement does not mention.

## Robert C. Byrd — 2007-10-29

On Republic Day, describes Mustafa Kemal, later Atatürk, as a war hero who rejected the outdated modes of empire and chose reform and modernisation.

> Kemal, who was later given the name Ataturk, meaning "father of the Turks," rejected the crumbling structures and outdated modes of empire and embraced instead a platform of reform and modernization, a legacy that continues in Turkey to this day and to this hour.

**Source.** Senate, 29 October 2007, S. Res. 358 (friendship between the United States and Turkey), Congressional Record vol. 153, p. S13524 (granule CREC-2007-10-29-pt1-PgS13524) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2007-10-29/html/CREC-2007-10-29-pt1-PgS13524.htm>

**Caveat.** Cosponsor's statement on the 84th anniversary of the Republic, in the week the House was debating an Armenian genocide resolution; the vault does not read a link into the statement. Title and state are from the standard record.

## Louie Gohmert — 2014-01-15

Argues, on Egypt's constitution, that Atatürk defeated radical Islam in Turkey and that Turkey rose because of it.

> this is something that Ataturk fought against and was able to overcome in Turkey so many decades ago. And because he was able to overcome and overwhelm radical Islam in Turkey, Turkey has surged to the forefront over the past decades in all kinds of areas.

**Source.** House of Representatives, 15 January 2014, 'Conflict in the Middle East', Congressional Record vol. 160, p. H468 (granule CREC-2014-01-15-pt1-PgH468); the same theme in his 7 January 2014 floor speech (p. H9) — <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2014-01-15/html/CREC-2014-01-15-pt1-PgH468.htm>

**Caveat.** A floor aside in a special-order speech about the Egyptian referendum; Atatürk is used as proof for Gohmert's argument about the Muslim Brotherhood and the Egyptian constitution. On 7 January (p. H9) he says Atatürk 'overran radical Islam' and Turkey 'bloomed'.
